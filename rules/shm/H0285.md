# H 285 Singular vs. Plural Forms in Subject Headings

## Validation rules

1. A topical heading designating entities capable of being enumerated is in the plural form, e.g. `Cats`, `Artificial reefs`, `American newspapers`, `Austronesian languages`, `Children`, `African American lawyers`. (H 285 sec. 1)
2. A topical heading designating an abstract concept is in the singular form, e.g. `Sociology`, `Love`, `Art, French`. (H 285 sec. 1)
3. A singular form of a countable entity (e.g. `Cat`, `Artificial reef`) is not a valid topical heading unless it falls under an exception below; flag and suggest the plural. (H 285 sec. 1)
4. Headings for parts of the body are in general singular, e.g. `Eye`, `Heart`, `Hand` (not `Eyes`, `Hearts`, `Hands`). (H 285 sec. 2.a)
5. Exception: some parts of the body are established in the plural, e.g. `Kidneys`, `Fingers`; these plural forms are valid and retained. A plural body-part heading is valid only if it is so established. (H 285 sec. 2.a)
6. Headings designating brands, makes, or lines of products manufactured by a particular company are plural: `[Company/brand] [product type, plural]`, e.g. `General Motors automobiles`, `Sony televisions`, `IBM microcomputers`. (H 285 sec. 2.b)
7. Older established singular brand headings that do not conform (e.g. `Kodak camera`, `Ford automobile`) remain valid as established until changed; do not construct new singular brand headings on their pattern. (H 285 sec. 2.b)
8. Headings for specific named models of a product are singular, e.g. `Xerox 1075 (Copying machine)`, `Volkswagen New Beetle automobile`, `Boeing 767 (Jet transport)`. (H 285 sec. 2.b)
9. For singular/plural of biological names consult H 1332 sec. 2.b; of musical compositions H 1160 sec. 5 and H 1917.5 sec. 1.b; of musical instruments H 1918 sec. 1.a.(2). (H 285 sec. 2.c–e)

## Full text

### 1. General rule.

Use the plural form when establishing topical headings that designate entities capable of being enumerated. Use the singular form when establishing topical headings that designate abstract concepts. Examples:

| Plural | Singular |
|---|---|
| *Cats* | *Sociology* |
| *Artificial reefs* | *Love* |
| *American newspapers* | *Art, French* |
| *Austronesian languages* | |
| *Children* | |
| *African American lawyers* | |

### 2. Specific categories of headings.

Special rules for the categories of headings listed below have evolved in LCSH over the period of its development. For some of these categories, the specific instruction sheets cited provide detailed information on the use of the singular or plural form.

#### a. Parts of the body.

In general, use the singular form when establishing headings for parts of the body, for example, *Eye*; *Heart*; *Hand*. Some parts of the body, however, are exceptions to this practice and have been established in the plural form, for example, *Kidneys*; *Fingers*. These headings may be retained in the plural form.

#### b. Named products.

Use the plural form when establishing headings that designate brands, makes, or lines of products manufactured by a particular company. Examples:

```
General Motors automobiles
Sony televisions
IBM microcomputers
```

Note: Many older headings established in LCSH do not conform to this policy, for example, *Kodak camera*; *Ford automobile*. Headings of this type may be changed as workload permits.

Use the singular form when establishing headings for specific named models of a product. Examples:

```
Xerox 1075 (Copying machine)
Volkswagen New Beetle automobile
Boeing 767 (Jet transport)
```

#### c. Biological names.

See H 1332, sec. 2.b.

#### d. Musical compositions.

See H 1160, sec. 5, and H 1917.5, sec. 1.b.

#### e. Musical instruments.

See H 1918, sec. 1.a.(2).

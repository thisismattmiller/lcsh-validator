# H 1945.5 Posters

## Validation rules

1. `--Posters` is a free-floating subdivision usable under names of individual places, persons and corporate bodies, classes of persons, ethnic groups, topical subjects, and wars. (H 1945.5 sec. 1)
2. Code `--Posters` as `$v` for individual posters or collections of posters, and as `$x` for discussions of posters. (H 1945.5 sec. 1)
3. Broad categories of posters are established as phrase headings, e.g. `Dance posters`, `Film posters`, `Political posters`, `Sports posters`, `Theatrical posters`, `Travel posters`, `War posters`; use these for posters of those types. (H 1945.5 sec. 2)
4. Nationality of the artists is expressed by qualifying the phrase heading for the type of poster with a nationality adjective after a comma: `[type] posters, [nationality]`, e.g. `Political posters, French`, `Theatrical posters, German`. (H 1945.5 sec. 3)
5. If no type-specific phrase heading applies, use `Posters, [nationality]`, e.g. `Posters, American`, `Posters, Polish`. (H 1945.5 sec. 3)
6. Headings of the type `Posters, [nationality]` / `[type] posters, [nationality]` may be subdivided by place of origin only to bring out a place not expressed in the nationality qualifier, e.g. `Posters, French--France--Paris`. (H 1945.5 sec. 3)
7. Travel posters take the form `Travel posters, [nationality]`, where the nationality is that of the place where the posters were produced. (H 1945.5 sec. 4.b)

## Full text

BACKGROUND: The term *posters* was used originally in England in the 19th century to refer to printed sheets of paper combining text and illustrations that were displayed in public places as announcements or advertisements. Since World War II, a new kind of poster has evolved, sometimes without text, that has advertising or promotional aims, but is a vehicle for philosophical, moral, or purely aesthetic messages. This instruction sheet provides guidelines for assigning an array of headings to individual posters and collections and/or discussions of posters depicting special topics, themes, products, events, etc. It does not cover posters treated purely as an art form. For instructions on cataloging these posters, see H 1250.

### 1. --Posters

Assign the free-floating subdivision --Posters under names of individual places, persons and corporate bodies, classes of persons, ethnic groups, topical subjects, and wars for individual posters and collections and/or discussions of posters about these subjects. Code the subdivision as a $v subfield for individual posters or collections of posters, and as an $x subfield for discussions of posters. Examples:

```
650 #0 $a Automobile racing $x Posters.
610 20 $a Beatles $v Posters.
600 10 $a Castro, Fidel, $d 1926- $x Posters.
650 #0 $a Industrial safety $z Poland $x Posters.
651 #0 $a Outer space $v Posters.
610 20 $a Socialist Party of Ireland $x Posters.
650 #0 $a World War, 1939-1945 $v Posters.
```

### 2. Phrase headings

Certain broad categories of posters have been established as phrase headings. Assign phrase headings to individual posters and collections and/or discussions of these types of posters. Examples:

```
650 #0 $a Dance posters.
650 #0 $a Film posters.
650 #0 $a Political posters.
650 #0 $a Sports posters.
650 #0 $a Theatrical posters.
650 #0 $a Travel posters.
650 #0 $a War posters.
```

### 3. Nationality

To individual posters and collections and/or discussions of posters produced by artists of a particular nationality, assign a phrase heading for the specific type of poster, qualified by the nationality. Examples:

```
650 #0 $a Political posters, French.
650 #0 $a Theatrical posters, German.
```

If the posters do not belong to a specific type and a phrase heading cannot be used, assign the heading Posters, qualified by the nationality. Examples:

```
650 #0 $a Posters, American.
650 #0 $a Posters, Polish.
```

Subdivide headings of this type by the place of origin only if it is necessary to bring out a particular place that is not expressed in the nationality qualifier, for example, Posters, French--France--Paris.

### 4. Assigning an array of headings

#### a. General

To individual posters and collections and/or discussions of posters, assign an array of headings to bring out the theme, type, and nationality of the posters. Usually at least two headings will be required to cover all aspects of the work. Examples:

```
Title: Affiches.
610 20 $a Parti socialiste (France) $x Posters.
650 #0 $a Socialism $z France $x Posters.
650 #0 $a Political posters, French.

Title: 100 posters of Buffalo Bill's Wild West.
600 00 $a Buffalo Bill, $d 1846-1917 $v Posters.
610 20 $a Buffalo Bill's Wild West Company $v Posters.
650 #0 $a Posters, American.

Title: Poster propaganda : contemporary political posters.
650 #0 $a World politics $y 20th century $x Posters.
650 #0 $a Political posters, German.

Title: Paris theatrical posters.
650 #0 $a Theatrical posters, French $z France $z Paris.
```

#### b. Travel posters

To individual posters and collections and/or discussions of travel posters, assign a heading of the type Travel posters, [nationality] to bring out the place where the posters were produced and, if appropriate, headings for the place depicted in the posters, the company issuing the posters, etc. Examples:

```
Title: 15 posters for Air France.
650 #0 $a Travel posters, French.
610 20 $a Air France $v Posters.

Title: Switzerland in posters.
651 #0 $a Switzerland $x Posters.
650 #0 $a Travel posters, American.
```

### 5. Poster collections

For instructions on assigning headings to discussions of individual poster collections, see H 1427. For instructions on assigning headings to catalogs of individual poster collections, see H 1360 and H 1427.

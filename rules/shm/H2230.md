# H 2230 Visual Materials and Non-Music Sound Recordings

## Validation rules

1. These guidelines apply to motion pictures, slides, filmstrips, video recordings, other types of visual materials, and non-music sound recordings, and are followed in addition to the normal rules for assigning subject headings; "film" means any type of visual material. (H 2230 Background)
2. Assign subject headings for all important topics mentioned in the summary statement (520 field). (H 2230 sec. 1)
3. If a specific topic is emphasized to illustrate a more general concept, assign headings for both the specific and the general topic (e.g., `Industries--India` and `Steel industry and trade--India`). (H 2230 sec. 1)
4. Assign form subdivisions to visual materials and non-music sound recordings only to the extent that the subdivision is applicable to both print and audiovisual media. (H 2230 sec. 1)
5. Do not use the form subdivision `--Pictorial works` for visual materials; a heading string ending in `--Pictorial works` assigned to a film/video/visual material is invalid under this sheet. (H 2230 sec. 1)
6. When a topic is discussed in conjunction with a particular place, make, insofar as possible, a subject entry under both the topic (subdivided geographically, e.g., `Oases--Sahara`, `Drug abuse--New York (State)--New York`) and the place (e.g., `Sahara--Description and travel`, `New York (N.Y.)--Social conditions`). (H 2230 sec. 2)
7. When a particular person is treated as illustrative of a profession or activity, assign a heading for both the person and the field of endeavor (e.g., `Ali, Muhammad, 1942-` and `Boxing`). Do not, as a general rule, treat such materials as biographies (i.e., do not add biography subdivisions). (H 2230 sec. 3)
8. Fiction films: assign topical headings with the subdivision `--Drama`; for juvenile fiction films use the subdivision `--Juvenile films` instead. Assign such headings to the same extent as they are assigned to individual dramas in book form (cf. H 1780 sec. 4). (H 2230 sec. 4.a)
9. Foreign language teaching films: assign the heading of the form `[...] language--Films for [...] speakers` as the first heading, and bring out any special topics with additional headings. (H 2230 sec. 4.b)
10. For juvenile films see H 1690; for films for the hearing impaired and for people with disabilities see H 1913 sec. 1.e; for moving image genre/form headings see H 1913; for radio program genre/form terms see H 1969.5. (H 2230 secs. 4.c, 4.d, 5, 6)
11. [LC practice] If the contents of a printed edition and a sound recording or visual presentation are the same, the subject cataloging should match, even if new, more appropriate headings must be assigned to the printed edition(s). (H 2230 sec. 7.a)
12. [LC practice] A work issued in more than one format (e.g., motion picture and video recording; cassette and compact disc) should have matching subject headings in all formats with MARC records, even if the headings originally assigned in another format must be corrected. (H 2230 sec. 7.b)
13. Assign geographic area codes in the 043 field as appropriate. (H 2230 sec. 7 [second], "043 fields")

## Full text

BACKGROUND: This instruction sheet provides special guidelines for the assignment of subject headings to motion pictures, slides, filmstrips, video recordings, and other types of visual materials, as well as to non-music sound recordings. These guidelines are followed in addition to the normal rules for the assignment of subject headings.

Note: As used in this instruction sheet, the word *film* is understood to refer to any type of visual material.

### 1. General rules

Assign subject headings for all important topics mentioned in the summary statement (520 field).

If a specific topic is emphasized in order to illustrate a more general concept, assign subject headings for both the specific and the general topics.

Assign form subdivisions only to the extent that such subdivisions are applicable both to print and audiovisual media. Do not use the form subdivision --Pictorial works for visual materials.

Examples:

```
520 ## $a Describes the highlights of Colombia, including the
            production of coffee.
651 #0 $a Colombia $x Description and travel.
650 #0 $a Coffee $z Colombia.
```

```
520 ## $a Surveys the industries of India, with special emphasis
            on the steel industry.
650 #0 $a Industries $z India.
650 #0 $a Steel industry and trade $z India.
```

```
520 ## $a Documents the intellectual expansion in medieval
            Germany, as illustrated by the Nuremberg chronicle.
600 10 $a Schedel, Hartmann, $d 1440-1514. $t Liber
            chronicarum.
651 #0 $a Germany $x Intellectual life.
```

### 2. Particular places

When a topic is discussed in conjunction with a particular place, make, insofar as possible, a subject entry under both the topic and the place. Examples:

```
520 ## $a Describes the oases of the Sahara.
650 #0 $a Oases $z Sahara.
651 #0 $a Sahara $x Description and travel.
```

```
520 ## $a Interviews with medical personnel and participants in a
            drug abuse treatment program in New York City.
650 #0 $a Drug abuse $z New York (State) $z New York.
651 #0 $a New York (N.Y.) $x Social conditions.
```

### 3. Particular persons

When a particular person is treated as illustrative of a profession or activity, assign a heading for both the person and the field of endeavor. Do not, as a general rule, treat such materials as biographies. Example:

```
520 ## $a A day in the life of prizefighter Muhammad Ali as he
            trains for a championship bout.
600 10 $a Ali, Muhammad, $d 1942-
650 #0 $a Boxing.
```

### 4. Special types of visual materials

#### a. Fiction films

Assign topical headings with the subdivision --Drama (or, in the case of juvenile fiction films, the subdivision --Juvenile films). Assign headings of this type to the same extent that such headings are assigned to individual dramas in book form (cf. H 1780, sec. 4).

#### b. Foreign language teaching films

Assign the heading [...] language--Films for [...] speakers as the first heading, and bring out any special topics by assigning additional headings.

#### c. Juvenile films

See H 1690.

#### d. Films for the hearing impaired and for people with disabilities

See H 1913, sec. 1.e.

### 5. Moving image genre/form headings

See H 1913.

### 6. Radio program genre/form terms

See H 1969.5.

### 7. Treatment of "editions"

LC practice:

#### a. Audio or visual presentions of published works

If the contents of the printed edition and the sound recording or visual presentation are the same, the subject cataloging should match, even if it is necessary to assign new, more appropriate headings to the printed edition(s).

#### b. Identical works in different formats

A single work is sometimes issued in more than one format, for example, as a motion picture and as a video recording, or as a cassette and as a compact disc. The subject headings for the work in all formats with records in MARC should match, even if it is necessary to correct the subject headings that were assigned when the work was originally cataloged in another format.

### 7. 043 fields

Assign geographic area codes in the 043 field as appropriate.

(Note: the source numbers both "Treatment of 'editions'" and "043 fields" as section 7.)

# H 1775 Literature: General

## Validation rules

1. Literary form or genre is normally combined with nationality or language in a phrase heading, e.g. `American poetry`; inverted forms combine genre/theme with nationality, e.g. `Sonnets, American`, `Romance fiction, French`, `Soldiers' writings, Canadian`. (H 1775 sec. E.1, E.2)
2. For collections by several authors, time period, translations, and locality are normally expressed by subdivisions under the literary form heading (e.g. `[nationality] [form]--[period]`, `--Translations into [language]`). (H 1775 sec. E.1)
3. Themes or topics in collections are expressed as `[topic]--[subdivision]`, e.g. `Cats--Literary collections`, or as phrase headings, e.g. `Detective and mystery stories`. (H 1775 sec. E.1)
4. Groups of authors are expressed as subdivisions, e.g. `American fiction--Women authors`, or as phrases, e.g. `Soldiers' writings, Canadian`. (H 1775 sec. E.1)
5. For collections of texts by one author, nationality or language is expressed only when combined with genre or theme in a phrase heading (e.g. `Sonnets, American`); do not assign plain `[nationality] [form]` headings; time period, locality, movements, etc. are not expressed. (H 1775 sec. E.2)
6. Translations of a single author's works are shown by `[author]--Translations into [language]`, e.g. `Poe, Edgar Allan, 1809-1849--Translations into French`. (H 1775 sec. E.2)
7. Individual poems or plays receive headings for theme and highly specific forms, e.g. `Christmas--Poetry`, `Monodramas`; individual works of fiction receive headings for persons, historical events or periods, and animals, e.g. `Washington, George, 1732-1799--Fiction`, `Thirty Years' War, 1618-1648--Fiction`, `Horses--Fiction`. (H 1775 sec. E.3)
8. Works discussing several authors require a subdivision on the literary form heading showing the work is about literature, most commonly `--History and criticism` (e.g. `American poetry--History and criticism`); a bare form heading denotes a collection of texts. (H 1775 sec. E.4)
9. In works about literature, themes are expressed as `[topic] in literature`, `[topic]--In literature`, or `[topic] and literature`. (H 1775 sec. E.4)
10. Discussion of one author takes the author's name with free-floating subdivisions from the pattern lists (H 1110, H 1155.2-H 1156); biographies follow H 1330. (H 1775 sec. E.5)
11. Discussion of a single literary text takes the uniform title (usually author-title) with pattern-list subdivisions; form headings or subdivisions that would apply to the text are converted to their topical equivalents. (H 1775 sec. E.6)
12. [LC practice] LC no longer uses form subdivisions (e.g. `--Literary collections`, `--Poetry`, `--Fiction`, `--Drama`); it assigns LCGFT (e.g. `Drama`, `Free verse`, `Bibliographies`, `Essays`, `Biographies`) and LCDGT. These subdivisions remain valid LCSH. (H 1775 sec. A, E.1-E.6)

## Full text

### A. Background

This instruction sheet provides a general overview of the assignment of subject headings in the field of literature. Specific instructions are given in other instruction sheets, especially those listed in section C, below.

LC practice: As stated in H 1075, LC catalogers will no longer use form subdivisions. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT), e.g. *Drama*, *Free verse*, etc. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign Library of Congress Demographic Groups Terms (LCDGT) to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*, *German speakers*, etc.

### D. Most commonly expressed concepts in subject headings for literature

The following concepts are frequently encountered in the cataloging of literary materials. These concepts are expressed in the assignment of subject headings according to the type of work being cataloged as explained in section E, below.

| Concept | Examples |
|---|---|
| Nationality | American, Mexican, Swiss |
| Form or genre | poetry, short stories, one-act plays |
| Theme or topic | love, mystery, Napoleon, Korean War |
| Time period | 20th century, Renaissance |
| Special group of authors | Jews, women, prisoners |
| Translations | from French into English |
| Movements | Romanticism, Surrealism |
| Languages | Basque, Yiddish, Catalan |
| Locality where written | New York City, Normandy, Southern States |

### E. Most commonly found types of works in literature cataloging

The following are the six basic categories of works found in the field of literature.

### E.1. Collections of literary texts by several authors

Examples:

- a collection of 20th century American poetry
- an anthology of German literature
- selections from classical Spanish drama
- English translations of Portuguese love poems

Any of the concepts in section D, above, may be expressed for this category of work. Subdivisions are normally used to express time period, translations, and locality. Form or genre is normally combined with nationality or language in a phrase heading, for example, *American poetry*. Themes or topics may be expressed as *[topic]--[subdivision]*, for example, *Cats--Literary collections*; or as a phrase, for example, *Detective and mystery stories*. Groups of authors may be expressed as subdivisions or as phrases, e.g. *American fiction--Women authors*; *Soldiers' writings, Canadian*.

LC practice: Do not use form subdivisions. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Drama* or *Free verse*. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign LCDGT to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*, *German speakers*, etc.

### E.2. Collections of literary texts by one author

Examples:

- a collection of poems of one author
- complete work of a novelist
- selected short stories of one author

Theme or topic is expressed for this category of work; genre may be shown if highly specific and emphasized in the work. Nationality or language is expressed only if combined with genre or theme in a phrase heading, for example, *Sonnets, American*; *Romance fiction, French*. Translations are shown by subdivision under the name of the author, for example, *Poe, Edgar Allan, 1809-1849--Translations into French*. Other concepts from sec. D, above, are not expressed.

LC practice: Do not use form subdivisions. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Drama* or *Free verse*. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign LCDGT to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*, *German speakers*, etc.

### E.3. Single literary text

Examples:

- a single novel
- a single play

Individual poems or plays are assigned headings for theme and for highly specific forms, for example, *Christmas--Poetry*; *Monodramas*. Individual works of fiction are assigned headings for persons, historical events or periods, and animals, for example, *Washington, George, 1732-1799--Fiction*; *Thirty Years' War, 1618-1648--Fiction*; *Horses--Fiction*. Other concepts from section D, above, are not normally expressed.

LC practice: Do not use form subdivisions. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Drama* or *Free verse*. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign LCDGT to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*, *German speakers*, etc.

### E.4. Discussion of several authors

Examples:

- a history of Argentine literature
- a bibliography of French science fiction
- essays on 20th century Jewish novelists
- treatise on the use of satire in American novels
- biographical directory of Canadian authors

Any of the concepts from section D, above, may be expressed. Subdivisions must be added to literary form headings to show that the work is about literature, not a collection of texts; the most common of these subdivisions is *--History and criticism*. Themes or topics are usually expressed as *[topic] in literature*, *[topic]--In literature*, or *[topic] and literature*.

LC practice: Do not use form subdivisions. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Bibliographies* or *Essays*.

### E.5. Discussion of one author

Examples:

- autobiography of a single author
- stage history of Shakespeare's plays
- a treatise on Victor Hugo's poetry

The major subject heading assigned is the name of the author discussed, with subdivisions as appropriate from the pattern lists of free-floating subdivisions. Theme or topic may also be expressed. Other concepts from section D, above, are not normally expressed. Biographical works follow provisions for biography given in H 1330.

LC practice: Do not use form subdivisions. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Biographies*.

### E.6. Discussion of single literary text

Examples:

- essays on Milton's Paradise lost
- treatise on Margaret Mitchell's Gone with the wind

The major subject heading assigned is the uniform title for the work, usually as an author-title subject entry. Subdivisions may be assigned from the appropriate pattern list. Theme or topic may also be expressed. Form headings or subdivisions that were assigned to the literary text are converted to the appropriate topical equivalent. Other concepts from section D, above, are not normally expressed.

LC practice: Do not use form subdivisions. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Essays*.

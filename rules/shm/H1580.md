# H 1580 Effect of One Topic on Another

## Validation rules

1. The effect of an agent or activity on a topic is expressed as `[topic affected]--Effect of [agent or activity] on`, e.g. `Body temperature--Effect of drugs on`; `Housing--Effect of inflation on`; `Collective bargaining--Effect of mass media on`; `Aquatic animals--Effect of water pollution on`. The subdivision begins with "Effect of" and ends with the word "on". (H 1580 sec. 1)
2. `--Effect of [...] on` is May Subd Geog; the geographic subdivision follows it. (H 1580 sec. 1)
3. It is established mainly where clear patterns exist: under plants, animals, materials, parts of the body, and some business and economics topics. (H 1580 sec. 1)
4. Do not establish `[topic]--Effect on [...]`; establish `--Effect of [...] on` under the topic affected instead (i.e. the heading is the thing affected, not the agent). (H 1580 sec. 1.a)
5. Do not establish new headings with the obsolete subdivisions `--Influence of [...]` or `--Influence on [...]`; existing headings with these subdivisions remain in use. (H 1580 sec. 1.b)
6. Do not establish inverted phrase headings `[topic], Effect of [...] on`; use the subdivision under the topic affected. (H 1580 sec. 1.c)
7. Most headings of the forms `[topic], Effect of [...] on`, `[topic]--Influence of [...]`, `[topic]--Influence on [...]` are obsolete and replaced by `[topic]--Effect of [...] on`. (H 1580 Background)
8. `--Effect of [...] on` subdivisions established under pattern headings are free-floating under any heading in that pattern category (H 1146); all others must be established individually. (H 1580 sec. 2, 3)
9. For an established `[topic]--Effect of [...] on`, the broader term is the heading producing the effect (e.g. `Inflation (Finance)--Effect of productivity on` BT `Industrial productivity`; `Microorganisms--Effect of antibiotics on` BT `Antibiotics--Physiological effect`); no UF unless no specific heading exists for the agent. (H 1580 sec. 3)
10. The wording inside the subdivision need not match the agent heading verbatim (e.g. `--Effect of explosive devices on` BT `Explosives`; `--Effect of productivity on` BT `Industrial productivity`). (H 1580 sec. 3 examples)
11. For `--Influence` and `--[...] influences` see H 1675; for headings expressing a general relationship between two topics see H 310. (H 1580 Background)

## Full text

### Background

In subject heading practice several types of headings have been used for works discussing the effect or influence of one topic on another:

- [topic]--Effect of [ . . . ] on
- [topic]--Influence
- [topic]--[ . . . ] influences
- [topic], Influence of
- [topic], Influence of [ . . . ] on
- [topic], Effect of [ . . . ] on
- [topic]--Influence of [ . . . ]
- [topic]--Influence on [ . . . ]

Most headings of the last three types are obsolete and have been replaced by [topic]--Effect of [ . . . ] on. In the future, --Effect of [ . . . ] on should be established and used for works which discuss the effect of one topic on another. This instruction sheet presents guidelines for the use of the subdivision --Effect of [ . . . ] on. For provisions involving the use of the general subdivisions --Influence and --[ . . . ] influences, see H 1675. For information on headings that express the general relationship between two topics, see H 310.

### 1. Usage of --Effect of [ . . . ] on

Establish the subdivision --Effect of [ . . . ] on (May Subd Geog) for works discussing the effects of an agent or activity on a topic in those areas where clear patterns of using such subdivisions already exist, such as under plants, animals, materials, parts of the body, and some topics in the fields of business and economics. Examples:

```
650 #0 $a Body temperature $x Effect of drugs on.
650 #0 $a Housing $x Effect of inflation on.
650 #0 $a Collective bargaining $x Effect of mass media on.
650 #0 $a Aquatic animals $x Effect of water pollution on.
```

#### a. --Effect on [ . . . ] not used

Do not establish headings of the type [topic]--Effect on [ . . . ]. Instead, establish the subdivision --Effect of [ . . . ] on under the heading for the topic affected.

#### b. --Influence of [ . . . ]; --Influence on [ . . . ]

Do not establish new headings using the obsolete subdivisions --Influence of [ . . . ] or --Influence on [ . . . ]. However, continue using existing headings with these subdivisions.

#### c. [topic], Effect of [ . . . ] on

Do not establish inverted phrase headings of the type [topic], Effect of [ . . . ] on. Instead, establish the subdivision --Effect of [ . . . ] on under the heading for the topic affected.

### 2. Subdivisions controlled by pattern headings

Certain --Effect of [ . . . ] on subdivisions are established under pattern headings, and as such, are free-floating subdivisions under any other heading belonging to the category represented by the pattern heading (cf. H 1146).

### 3. References

Establish editorially each [topic]--Effect of [ . . . ] on heading where the subdivision is not free-floating because it is not controlled by a pattern heading. Use the heading producing the effect as the broader term. Do not make a UF reference unless no specific heading exists for the agent or activity producing the effect. Examples:

```
150 ## $a Inflation (Finance) $x Effect of productivity on
550 ## $w g $a Industrial productivity
```

```
150 ## $a Ruby lasers $x Effect of radiation on
550 ## $w g $a Radiation
```

```
150 ## $a Bridges, Concrete $x Effect of temperature on
550 ## $w g $a Temperature
```

```
150 ## $a Automobiles $x Effect of explosive devices on
550 ## $w g $a Explosives
```

```
150 ## $a Microorganisms $x Effect of antibiotics on
550 ## $w g $a Antibiotics $x Physiological effect
```

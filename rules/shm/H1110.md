# H 1110 Free-Floating Subdivisions: Names of Persons

## Validation rules

### Scope: which headings the list applies to

1. The subdivisions on this list may be used as free-floating subdivisions, within the limitations of their usage notes, only under names of individual persons established as headings in the name authority file (MARC 600 personal names). (H 1110 sec. C)
2. The list applies to all personal names in the name authority file except names of non-human entities: individual fictitious and legendary characters, individual gods and mythological figures, and individually named animals. Do not validate a subdivision under such a name against this list; use H 1095 instead. (H 1110 sec. C)
3. General form and topical subdivisions in H 1095 carrying the statement "Use under subjects" are also valid under personal name headings, in addition to this list. (H 1110 sec. F)
4. A subdivision applicable to the topic may be used under any person regardless of how large the file on that person is (it is not reserved for prominent persons). (H 1110 sec. F)
5. Under a few unique and highly significant individuals — *Gautama Buddha*; *Muḥammad, Prophet, -632*; *Jesus Christ*; *Shakespeare, William, 1564-1616* — varying, conflicting, or additional subdivisions are established in the subject authority file and take precedence over this list. A subdivision that conflicts with this list is not an error under those headings if it is the established one. (H 1110 sec. G)
6. Specific-topic subdivisions for unique or highly significant persons are established individually in the subject authority file (e.g. `Mary, Blessed Virgin, Saint--Annunciation`); such a subdivision is valid only under that person, not free-floating under other persons. (H 1110 sec. H)
7. Do not establish or accept specific-topic subdivisions under names of persons who are not prominent or well known; use the closest subdivision on the list or the unsubdivided name plus an additional heading. (H 1110 sec. H)
8. Subtopics too specific to be separately established are expressed by the broader list subdivision plus a separate heading for the specific topic (e.g. `Smith, John--Ethics` + `Surgeons--Professional ethics`, not `Smith, John--Professional ethics`; `Jackson, Wilbur--Trials, litigation, etc.` + `Trials (Murder)--Pennsylvania--Pittsburgh`). (H 1110 sec. I)
9. The persons-list subdivisions are free-floating only in the exact wording printed; rows on the list without a `$x`/`$v` code (e.g. "Acting", "Associates", "Biography, [Person]", "Weblogs") are see/use references, not valid subdivisions; replace them with the referenced subdivision (see JSON `see_references`). (H 1110 sec. O, P)

### Unsubdivided name; biography

10. Complete biography (including autobiography) and general discussion or criticism of a person's life and/or work take the unsubdivided personal name heading. `--Biography` and `--Autobiography` are not used under individual persons: "Biography, [Person]" and "Autobiography, [Person]" are references to "Use [name of person]". (H 1110 sec. E, sec. O)
11. Use the unsubdivided personal name for works on more than three separate aspects of an individual's life or work, or on topics not covered by any subdivision on the list. (H 1110 sec. E)

### Multifaceted careers and conflicts

12. For persons with multifaceted careers, subdivisions are chosen according to the emphasis of the work (e.g. *Luce, Clare Boothe, 1903-1987* as diplomat/politician vs. as playwright), unless a direct conflict exists between subdivisions on the list. (H 1110 sec. J.1)
13. Where a general subdivision directly conflicts with one designated only for literary authors, use the one pertinent to the field in which the person is better known, and keep using it for that concept under that person even if a later work focuses on another field. (H 1110 sec. J.2)
14. `--Writing skill` is used only under persons not primarily known as literary authors; do not use it under literary authors (e.g. not under *Fitzgerald, F. Scott (Francis Scott), 1896-1940*) or under multi-career persons also recognized as literary authors. (H 1110 sec. J.2; sec. O "Writing skill")
15. [LC practice] When a new list subdivision is assigned for the first time under a person, LC corrects existing records that use a conflicting subdivision under that person; existing records are otherwise not systematically revised. (H 1110 sec. N)

### Cancelled / discontinued subdivisions (invalid strings)

16. `--Career in [specific field or discipline]` (e.g. `--Career in politics`) was cancelled in July 2020. Do not use it; assign the unsubdivided name plus a heading for the field or discipline (e.g. `Washington, George, 1732-1799` + `Surveying`). "Career in politics" refers to `--Political activity`. (H 1110 sec. K, K.1)
17. `--Characters--[name of individual character]` and `--Characters--Children, [Jews, Physicians, etc.]` were cancelled/discontinued in 2020. Use `--Characters` alone plus an additional heading. (H 1110 sec. K, K.3; sec. O "Characters")
18. `--Knowledge--[specific topic]` was cancelled in July 2020; use `--Knowledge and learning` plus a heading for the topic. (H 1110 sec. K, K.2)
19. `--Contributions in [specific field or topic]` and `--Views on [specific topic]` were discontinued in September 2004 with no replacement; `--Views on [specific topic]` was also discontinued in July 2020 under *Jesus Christ* and *Lincoln, Abraham, 1809-1865*. Do not use them; assign the name plus a heading for the topic. "Views on aesthetics/ethics/politics and society/religion/society" refer to `--Aesthetics`, `--Ethics`, `--Political and social views`, `--Religion`. (H 1110 sec. K.2; sec. O)
20. `--Relations with [specific class of persons or ethnic group]` was cancelled in July 2020; assign the name plus a heading for the class of persons or ethnic group. (H 1110 sec. L)
21. `--Relations with men` and `--Relations with women` remain valid (intimate relations); for an individual man/woman add a heading for that person. `--Relations with employees` and `--Relations with friends and associates` are not valid; use `--Employees` and `--Friends and associates`. (H 1110 sec. L; sec. O)
22. The free-floating phrase heading `[name of person] in fiction, drama, poetry, etc.` was discontinued in August 1993; use `--In literature` (topical) or the form subdivisions `--Drama`, `--Fiction`, `--Literary collections`, `--Poetry`. (H 1110 sec. O "In literature")

### Relationship subdivisions

23. `--Adversaries`, `--Contemporaries`, `--Disciples`, `--Employees`, `--Family`, and `--Friends and associates` remain valid and express the person's relationships with those groups. (H 1110 sec. L)
24. `--Adversaries`: contemporaries who opposed the person's point of view or work ("Enemies", "Opponents" refer here). (H 1110 sec. O)
25. `--Family`: the person's family, relations with family members, and genealogical works; assign an additional heading for the family name ("Genealogy" refers here in the topical list; `--Genealogy` also appears as a `$v` form subdivision). (H 1110 sec. O, P)
26. `--Friends and associates`: close and immediate contacts ("Associates", "Companions", "Relations with friends and associates" refer here). (H 1110 sec. O)

### Literary authors and creative persons

27. `--Criticism and interpretation` is used only under persons active in the fine arts, literature, music, and performing arts; it may be followed by `--History` and `--History--To 1500`, `--16th century`, `--17th century`, `--18th century`, `--19th century`, `--20th century`, `--21st century` (only those chronological subdivisions). "Commentaries" and "Interpretation" refer here. (H 1110 sec. O)
28. `--Appreciation` (May Subd Geog) is used under persons active in the fine arts, literature, music, and performing arts for public response and reception of their works. (H 1110 sec. O)
29. `--Characters` is used under literary authors; for a historical/mythological/legendary character add `[name]--In literature`; for a group add `[group or category] in literature`; for a fictitious character add a heading for the character per H 1610. (H 1110 sec. K.3; sec. O)
30. Genre-criticism subdivisions `--Comedies`, `--Tragedies`, `--Tragicomedies` are not used under dramatists who write principally that genre; `--Dramatic works` not under authors who write principally drama; `--Fictional works` not under authors who write principally fiction; `--Poetic works` not under authors who write principally poetry; `--Prose` not under authors who write principally prose; `--Motion picture plays` and `--Radio and television plays` not under authors who write principally those. (H 1110 sec. O)
31. `--Dramatic production`, `--Literary style`, `--Settings`, `--Stage history` (May Subd Geog), and `--Technique` are used under literary authors. (H 1110 sec. O)
32. `--Dramaturgy`, `--Harmony`, `--Thematic catalogs`, `--Themes, motives` (form) are used under composers; `--Stories, plots, etc.` (form) under composers, choreographers, and literary authors; `--Performances` (May Subd Geog) under performing artists and under composers, choreographers, etc. (H 1110 sec. O, P)
33. `--Written works` is used under persons active in the fine arts, music and performing arts for non-literary textual works; do not use under persons also known as literary authors. (H 1110 sec. O)
34. `--Translations` and `--Translations into French, [German, etc.]` (the latter completed with a language name, e.g. `--Translations into German`) are valid as `$x` (each may be followed by `--History and criticism`) and as `$v`; the form `--Translations` is for collections of translations of a literary author, not single translations. (H 1110 sec. O, P)
35. `--Philosophy` is not used under names of philosophers; `--Religion` not under names of theologians; `--Political activity` (May Subd Geog) not under persons chiefly known as politicians. (H 1110 sec. O)

### Specific subdivisions with literal usage constraints

36. `--Correspondence` (both `$x` and `$v`) is for letters from and/or to the person; assign an additional heading for individual correspondents ("Letters" refers here). `--Correspondence--Microform catalogs` (`$x Correspondence $v Microform catalogs`) is a valid form combination under names of individual persons. (H 1110 sec. O, P)
37. `--Diaries` (both `$x` and `$v`) is for collections or discussions of the person's diaries and also for individual diaries ("Journals" refers here). (H 1110 sec. O, P)
38. `--In literature` is the topical subdivision for the person as a theme in belles lettres; criticism of plays, fiction, or poetry about a person is `[name of person]--In literature`, while the works themselves take `--Drama`, `--Fiction`, `--Poetry`, or `--Literary collections` (two or more literary forms). (H 1110 sec. O, P)
39. `--Homes and haunts` (May Subd Geog) covers homes, dwellings, favorite and frequented places ("Dwellings", "Haunts", "Places frequented", "Residences" refer here). (H 1110 sec. O)
40. `--Art` is used for persons who lived before 1400; `--Portraits` for persons living after 1400 A.D. (H 1110 sec. O, P)
41. `--Travel` (May Subd Geog): when further subdivided by place, also assign `[place]--Description and travel` (e.g. `Smith, John--Travel--China` + `China--Description and travel`). "Journeys" and "Voyages" refer here. (H 1110 sec. M, sec. O)
42. `--Claims vs. …` must be completed with the name of the jurisdiction against which the claim was brought. (H 1110 sec. O)
43. `--Cult` (May Subd Geog) is used under divine persons, saints, or persons worshipped; `--Miracles` under persons to whom miracles are attributed; `--Hadith` under imams. (H 1110 sec. O)
44. `--Death and burial` covers death, funeral, burial, last illness ("Burial", "Funeral", "Internment", "Last illness" refer here); `--Tomb` covers grave and interred bones ("Grave", "Sepulchral monument"); disinterred bones go under `--Relics`. (H 1110 sec. O)
45. Only the following topical subdivisions are marked May Subd Geog: `--Appreciation`, `--Censorship`, `--Collectibles`, `--Cult`, `--Exile`, `--Forgeries`, `--Homes and haunts`, `--Monuments`, `--Museums`, `--Palaces`, `--Performances`, `--Political activity`, `--Relics`, `--Shrines`, `--Stage history`, `--Statues`, `--Travel`; of the form subdivisions only `--Political activity` is marked. A `$z` following any other subdivision from this list is invalid. (H 1110 sec. O, P)
46. `--Knowledge and learning` covers learning/scholarship in general and knowledge of a specific topic (plus a heading for the topic); "Education", "Learning", "Scholarship" refer here. (H 1110 sec. K.2; sec. O)

### Additional headings

47. Assign an additional heading for the topic of a subdivision when it represents an important retrieval aspect (e.g. `Goethe, Johann Wolfgang von, 1749-1832--Knowledge and learning` + `Alchemy`); personal-aspect subdivisions (e.g. `--Childhood and youth`) and some form subdivisions (e.g. `--Pictorial works`) do not need one. (H 1110 sec. D, M)
48. Additional headings for biographical works, anecdotes, correspondence, diaries, interviews, portraits, etc., follow H 1330, H 1480, H 1538, H 1678, etc. (H 1110 sec. M)

### LC practice

49. Form (`$v`) subdivisions on this list (sec. P) remain valid LCSH. (H 1110 sec. A, P)
50. [LC practice] LC catalogers no longer use form subdivisions; they assign LCGFT terms instead (e.g. *Textbooks*), and for form subdivisions that include a demographic group also assign LCDGT terms (e.g. *German speakers*, *Arabic, Students of*). (H 1110 sec. A)
51. [LC practice] The procedures apply mainly to new cataloging; existing LC records are revised only when encountered. (H 1110 sec. N)

## Full text

### A. Background

In the past, sets of form and topical subdivisions evolved for use under personal name headings belonging to a few broad categories of persons: rulers and statesmen, musicians, philosophers, founders of religions, and literary authors. These subdivisions were established and printed in Library of Congress Subject Headings under representative pattern personal name headings in order to reduce and keep to a minimum the number of personal names included in Library of Congress Subject Headings. Separate lists of the subdivisions were issued and maintained for each category. General subdivisions usable under any personal name heading were included in the list of most commonly used subdivisions (H 1095).

This present list is a consolidation and revision of the general subdivisions that could be used under any personal name and the subdivisions used under personal names belonging to the special categories. The separate subdivision list for literary authors (H 1155.4) was discontinued in 1998.

LC practice: As stated in H 1075, LC catalogers will no longer use form subdivisions. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT), e.g. *Textbooks*. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign Library of Congress Demographic Groups Terms (LCDGT) to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *German speakers* or *Arabic, Students of*.

### B. Recent revisions

2026-01: Updated template. Separated general and form subdivisions. Added instructions related to LCGFT implementation.

### C. Types of headings covered

The subdivisions listed below may be used, within the limitations of the usage notes, as free-floating subdivisions under names of individual persons established as headings in the name authority file. The category includes all personal names established in the name authority file except for names of non-human entities (e.g., names of individual fictitious and legendary characters, individual gods and mythological figures, and individually named animals). For free-floating subdivisions that may be used with names of non-human entities, see H 1095.

### D. General Principle

When assigning a name heading for a person, subdivide the name on a free-floating basis by the appropriate form and topical subdivisions listed below. If a topical subdivision is assigned which represents an important approach to the work, also provide direct subject access to that topic by assigning an additional heading to the work.

### E. Personal name headings

Assign an unsubdivided personal name heading for the complete biography of a person, including autobiography, or for general discussion or criticism of the person’s life and/or work. Also use unsubdivided personal name headings for works on more than three separate aspects of an individual’s life or work, or discussing one or more specific topics not covered by subdivisions on this list. Consult H 1330 for guidelines and instructions on assigning subject headings to biographical works.

### F. Free-floating subdivisions

As needed, subdivide personal name headings by appropriate form and topical subdivisions from the list below on a free-floating basis. General form and topical subdivisions listed in H 1095 with the statement “Use under subjects” are also available for use under personal name headings. Assign the subdivisions according to the guidelines and scope notes provided. References to instruction sheets that mention or may contain more information about a specific subdivision are included. If a specific subdivision is applicable to the topic of the work being cataloged, use it without regard to the size of the file already accumulated or anticipated about the person in the database.

### G. Conflicts

Varying or conflicting and additional subdivisions have been established under a few unique and highly significant individuals, for example, *Gautama Buddha*; *Muḥammad, Prophet, -632*; *Jesus Christ*; and *Shakespeare, William, 1564-1616*. In those cases, the established subdivisions take precedence over this list and should continue to be used.

### H. New subdivisions

Propose as additions to this list only new subdivisions having a wide range of potential application. For specific topics applying only to unique or highly significant persons, establish the subdivision under the name of the pertinent person for inclusion in the subject authority file, for example, *Mary, Blessed Virgin, Saint--Annunciation*. Do not establish specific topics as subdivisions under the names of persons who are not prominent or well known. Instead assign the closest subdivision from the list as described in sec. O below or use the unsubdivided personal name heading with additional headings to bring out the particular topic.

### I. Subtopics

For works discussing subtopics or aspects of topical subdivisions on the list that are too specific to be separately established, assign the broader subdivision under which the topic is subsumed. Make an additional entry under the specific topic.

```
Example:
Title: The professional ethics of Dr. John Smith, surgeon.
600 00 $a Smith, John $x Ethics.
650 #0 $a Surgeons $x Professional ethics.
```

```
Example:
Title: The murder trial of Wilbur Jackson
600 00 $a Jackson, Wilbur $x Trials, litigation, etc.
650 #0 $a Trials (Murder) $z Pennsylvania $z Pittsburgh.
```

### J. Persons with multifaceted careers

### J.1. General Rule

Treat persons with multifaceted careers according to the emphasis of the work at hand unless a direct conflict exists between subdivisions on this list. For example, if a person such as *Luce, Clare Boothe, 1903-1987*, is discussed as a diplomat and politician, assign subdivisions from this list that are appropriate for use with that type of person. If another work deals with her as a playwright, assign subdivisions from this list that are specifically designated for use under literary authors.

### J.2. Direct conflicts between subdivisions for use under literary authors and subdivisions used under types of people

In situations where there is a direct conflict between general subdivisions on this list and subdivisions specifically designated for use only under literary authors, assign subdivisions pertinent to the field in which the person is better known. For example, the subdivision *--Writing skill* is used only under names of people who are not primarily known as literary authors. This subdivision would not be used under the name of an author such as *Fitzgerald, F. Scott (Francis Scott), 1896-1940*.

Once it has been determined that a person is better known in a particular field and a subdivision appropriate for that field has been assigned under that person, continue to use that subdivision for the concept in question. Do not assign a conflicting subdivision even if the work in hand focuses on another field of activity.

### K. Relation to specific disciplines, fields, or topics

In July 2020, three subdivisions on this list that related the person to specific disciplines, fields, or topics were cancelled: *--Career in [specific field or discipline]*, *--Characters--[name of individual character]*, and *--Knowledge--[specific topic]*. Guidelines on assigning headings to works on a person’s relation to a specific discipline, field, or topic are given below.

### K.1. Career in a specific field or discipline

Assign the name heading for the person without further subdivision to works limited to describing events in a person’s occupational life or participation in a profession or vocation. Also assign the name heading to works that discuss the person’s actual substantive contributions or accomplishments in a specific field or topic, whether made as a result of a vocation or an avocation. Assign an additional heading for the field or discipline. For example, a work on George Washington’s career in surveying is assigned *Washington, George, 1732-1799* and *Surveying*.

### K.2. Knowledge of a specific topic

Assign the name heading subdivided by *--Knowledge and learning* to works discussing the person’s knowledge of a specific topic, whether explicitly stated or inferred from his or her life and work, and for discussions of the person’s educational background in a specific topic. Assign an additional heading for the specific topic. Also assign the subdivision *--Knowledge and learning* to works about the person’s formal or informal learning or scholarship in general.

Note: In September 2004, two additional subdivisions relating individual persons to specific fields or topics were discontinued with no replacements: *--Contributions in [specific field or topic]* and *--Views on [specific topic]*. In July 2020, the subdivision *--Views on [specific topic]* was also discontinued under the headings where it had been specifically established: *Jesus Christ* and *Lincoln, Abraham, 1809-1865*.

For works discussing the person’s actual substantive contributions or accomplishments in a specific field or topic, whether made as a result of a vocation or an avocation, assign the name of the person in combination with a heading for the specific topic, etc. Also assign a heading for the specific topic along with the name of the person for works discussing the person’s philosophy or system of thought on a particular topic that he or she propounded or imparted to others. For works discussing the person’s opinions or attitudes on a specific topic, whether explicitly stated by the person or inferred from his or her life and works, assign the name of the person and a heading for the topic.

### K.3. Literary characters

Assign the name heading for the literary author subdivided by *--Characters* to works discussing a specific group or category of characters of a literary author, or a specific named character of the literary author. Assign an additional heading according to the guidance under *--Characters* in the list below. Also assign the subdivision *--Characters* to general works on the characters of a literary author.

### L. Relations with specific classes of persons or ethnic groups

In July 2020, the subdivision *--Relations with [specific class of persons or ethnic group]* was cancelled. Assign the personal name heading to works about the person’s relationship with a specific class of persons or ethnic group. Assign an additional heading for the class of persons or ethnic group, with appropriate subdivision if necessary.

The free-floating subdivisions *--Relations with men* and *--Relations with women*, which are used under names of persons for works on their intimate relations with men and women, respectively, remain valid. The subdivisions *--Adversaries*, *--Contemporaries*, *--Disciples*, *--Employees*, *--Family*, and *--Friends and associates* also remain valid, and represent the person’s relationships with those groups. Assign those subdivisions in accordance with the guidance and scope notes provided under each.

### M. Assignment of additional headings

Bring out by means of an additional heading the topic of a subdivision representing an important aspect by which the work should be retrieved.

Many subdivisions are not important topics in their own right, for example, some form subdivisions such as *--Pictorial works* and topical subdivisions such as *--Childhood and youth*, which express personal aspects of an individual’s life. Other subdivisions, however, do represent significant concepts which should be brought out by both a topical subdivision under the person’s name and a subject heading for the topic itself. Situations requiring such additional headings are noted in the list.

```
Example
Title: Goethe the alchemist : a study of alchemical symbolism in Goethe’s literary and scientific works.
600 10 $a Goethe, Johann Wolfgang von, $d 1749-1832 $x Knowledge and learning.
650 #0 $a Alchemy.
```

```
Example
Title: Voyage to China.
651 #0 $a China $x Description and travel.
600 10 $a Smith, John $x Travel $z China.
```

Note: Assign additional headings to biographical works and related material such as anecdotes, correspondence, diaries, interviews, portraits, etc., according to the instructions in H 1330, H 1480, H 1538, H 1678, etc.

### N. Implementation

LC practice: The procedures in this instruction sheet apply mainly to new materials being cataloged. Do not attempt to revise systematically the subject headings assigned to existing records in the LC database. Reevaluate and revise as necessary those existing records encountered during the course of normal cataloging activities. When assigning a new subdivision from this list for the first time under the name of a person, correct records using a conflicting subdivision under that person in the LC database.

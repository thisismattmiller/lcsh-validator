# H 1154 Pattern Headings: Languages

## Validation rules

1. Subdivisions on this list are free-floating under headings for individual languages and groups of languages, including named dialects, artificial languages, and sign languages (e.g. *Danish language*; *Indo-European languages*; *Navajo language*; *Abung dialect*; *Esperanto*). (H 1154 sec. B)
2. The list also applies under headings formed with the free-floating subdivision `--Languages` under ethnic groups and places (e.g. *Indians of North America--Languages*; *Gabon--Languages*; *New York (N.Y.)--Languages*). (H 1154 sec. B)
3. Do not use this list free-floating under the heading *Language and languages*. (H 1154 sec. B)
4. A listed subdivision may be used free-floating if appropriate and no conflict exists in the subject authority file. (H 1154 sec. C.1)
5. Multiple subdivisions are being cancelled in favor of specifically established strings (cf. H 1090). For two-part multiples such as `--Foreign elements--French, [Greek, Latin, etc.]` or `--Religious aspects--Baptists, [Catholic Church, etc.]`, only the first part is free-floating; each further subdivision by language or religion must be established editorially. (H 1154 sec. E.1)
6. Single-part multiples (`--Transliteration into Korean, [Russian, etc.]`, `--Translating into French, [German, etc.]`, `--Influence on French, [Italian, etc.]`, `--Computer-assisted instruction for French, [Spanish, etc.] speakers`) are no longer valid as free-floating; each use must be specifically established under the language (e.g. *Amharic language--Transliteration into Russian*; *Slavic languages--Transliteration into English*). Such subdivisions established under the pattern headings are not free-floating. (H 1154 sec. E.2)
7. Do not form *[language]--Business English*, *--Medical English*, *--Technical English*, or *--Written English* as subdivisions; establish phrase headings of the type *[...] language--Business [...]*, *[...] language--Medical [...]*, *[...] language--Technical [...]*, *[...] language--Written [...]* as needed. (H 1154 sec. G)
8. Period subdivisions established under *English language*: `--Old English, ca. 450-1100`; `--Middle English, 1100-1500`; `--Early modern, 1500-1700`; `--18th century`; `--19th century`; `--20th century`; `--21st century`. If inappropriate for another language, the appropriate period subdivision must be established under that language (e.g. *Russian language--To 1300*). (H 1154 sec. F)
9. Topical and form subdivisions valid for *English language* may follow any period subdivision (e.g. *English language--Middle English, 1100-1500--Etymology--Names*). (H 1154 sec. F)
10. `--History` is not further subdivided by period subdivisions. (H 1154 sec. G)
11. The following are used only under languages other than English: `--Acronyms`, `--Diacritics`, `--Epithets`, `--Figures of speech`, `--Lexicology`, `--Lexicology, Historical`, `--Spoken French, [Japanese, etc.]`, `--Vocabulary`, `--Readers`, `--Readers for new literates`, `--Textbooks for foreign speakers--English`; `--Acronyms--Dictionaries` is used under languages other than English, groups of languages, and topical headings. (H 1154 sec. G-H)
12. `--Classification` ($x) and `--Mutual intelligibility` are used only under groups of languages. (H 1154 sec. G)
13. `--Metrics and rhythmics` is used only under individual ancient languages; under modern languages use `--Rhythm` and `--Versification`. (H 1154 sec. G)
14. `--Vocalization` is used only under individual Semitic languages; under other languages use `--Vowels`; `--Vowels` is used under languages other than Semitic languages. (H 1154 sec. G)
15. `--Social aspects` is not used under *[place]--Languages*; use *Sociolinguistics--[place]* instead. (H 1154 sec. G)
16. `--Texts` and `--Texts--Dating` are used only under lesser-known languages (e.g. *Hittite language--Texts*), under *[ethnic group]--Languages* (and *[groups of Indians]--Languages*), under *[place]--Languages*, or under early periods of languages (ca. pre-1500) (e.g. *English language--Middle English, 1100-1500--Texts*); consult H 2190. (H 1154 sec. G-H)
17. `--Alphabet--Religious aspects` and `--Religious aspects` may be subdivided by religion or Christian denomination only by editorially established headings (e.g. *English language--Religious aspects--Islam*; *Abidji language--Religious aspects--Catholic Church*); see H 1998. (H 1154 sec. G)
18. `--Cognate words`, `--Foreign elements`, and `--Foreign words and phrases` may be subdivided by an individual language or language group only by editorially established headings (e.g. *English language--Cognate words--Dutch*; *French language--Foreign elements--Arabic*; *Romance languages--Foreign words and phrases--German*). (H 1154 sec. G)
19. `--Study and teaching` and its level-qualified forms (`(Continuing education)`, `(Early childhood)`, `(Elementary)`, `(Higher)`, `(Middle school)`, `(Preschool)`, `(Primary)`, `(Secondary)`) may be further subdivided by `--French, [Spanish, etc.] speakers` (language of the learners) only by editorially established headings (e.g. *English language--Study and teaching--Chinese speakers*); do not interpose a geographic subdivision before the speakers subdivision; consult H 2110. (H 1154 sec. G)
20. `--Foreign countries` ($z) is used under languages per H 1919.5. (H 1154 sec. G)
21. `--Dialects--Research--Law and legislation` may be further subdivided by legal-topic subdivisions in H 1154.5. (H 1154 sec. G)
22. Only subdivisions marked May Subd Geog (e.g. `--Ability testing`, `--Age differences`, `--Dialects`, `--Dialects--Research`, `--Machine translating`, `--Political aspects`, `--Provincialisms`, `--Remedial teaching`, `--Sex differences`, `--Social aspects`, `--Spoken French, [Japanese, etc.]`, `--Study and teaching` and level forms, `--Transcription`, `--Translating`, `--Variation`, `--Vocational guidance`) may be subdivided geographically. (H 1154 sec. G)
23. `--Dictionaries, Juvenile` and `--Glossaries, vocabularies, etc.` may also be used under any valid topical subdivision (except `--Reverse indexes` and `--Terms and phrases`) (e.g. *English language--Synonyms and antonyms--Dictionaries, Juvenile*; *English language--Orthography and spelling--Glossaries, vocabularies, etc.*). (H 1154 sec. H)
24. `--Readers--[form]` and `--Readers--[topic]` are used under languages other than English for readers in those languages in particular literary forms or on particular topics; consult H 1975. (H 1154 sec. H)
25. `--Textbooks for English, [French, etc.] speakers` is used only under artificial languages (e.g. *Esperanto--Textbooks for Spanish speakers*). (H 1154 sec. H)
26. Do not use `--Video recordings for foreign speakers` or `--Video recordings for French, [Spanish, etc.] speakers`; use `--Films for foreign speakers` and `--Films for French, [Spanish, etc.] speakers`. (H 1154 sec. H)
27. Form subdivisions combinations `--Dialects--Conversation and phrase books`, `--Dialects--Glossaries, vocabularies, etc.`, `--Dialects--Texts`, and `--Grammar--Terminology` are used under individual languages and groups of languages. (H 1154 sec. H)
28. `--Conversation and phrase books` may be qualified by an occupational group only in the listed forms, e.g. `--Conversation and phrase books (for accountants)` ... `(for tourism industry employees)`. (H 1154 sec. H)
29. [LC practice] Establish new subdivisions under *English language* if possible, otherwise under *French language* (individual languages) or *Romance languages* (groups of languages). (H 1154 sec. D)
30. [LC practice] If an exceptional variant form is retained, make a UF reference from the equivalent free-floating subdivision form (H 195); otherwise propose a change (H 193). (H 1154 sec. C.2)

## Full text

Patterns: *English language*; *French language*; *Romance languages*

### A. Recent revisions

2026-04: Updated template. Added new free-floating subdivision --Null object.

### B. Types of headings covered by the pattern

Headings for individual languages and groups of languages, including named dialects, artificial languages, and sign languages. Also included are headings formed using the free-floating subdivision *--Languages* under ethnic groups and names of countries, cities, etc. Examples: *Danish language*; *Indo-European languages*; *Navajo language*; *Abung dialect*; *Esperanto*; *Indians of North America--Languages*; *Gabon--Languages*; *New York (N.Y.)--Languages*. The category does not include the heading *Language and languages*.

### C. Conflicts

### C.1. General rule

Any subdivision listed here can be used as a free-floating subdivision under any heading belonging to the category if it is appropriate and no conflict exists in the subject authority file. Subject authority records may exist for headings employing variant phrases or subdivisions equivalent to subdivisions on this list.

### C.2. LC practice

If an exceptional variant form is to be retained, make a UF reference from the equivalent free-floating subdivision form following the procedures in H 195 if the reference does not yet exist. Otherwise, submit a proposal to change the variant form along with all bibliographic records requiring correction following the procedures in H 193.

### D. New Subdivisions

Establish new subdivisions under *English language*, if possible, or if it is not possible, under *French language* for individual languages or *Romance languages* for groups of languages. Subdivisions listed below which are not valid under *English language* are indicated in the Notes column.

### E. Special provisions for multiple subdivisions

The multiple subdivisions that appear on these lists are being cancelled in favor of specifically established heading strings (Cf. H 1090).

### E.1. Two-part subdivisions.

Where the cancelled multiple is the second part of a two-part subdivision (e.g., *--Foreign elements--French, [Greek, Latin, etc.]*; *--Religious aspects--Baptists, [Catholic Church, etc.]*), only the first part of the subdivision is retained in the list, with a footnote providing instructions on further subdivision.

### E.2. Single-part subdivisions

Where the cancelled multiple is the only subdivision (e.g., *--Transliteration into Korean, [Russian, etc.]*), the subdivision is retained in the list, but italicized to indicate that it is no longer valid.

Each use of the subdivision must be specifically established under the heading for the language or language group (e.g., *Amharic language--Transliteration into Russian*; *Slavic languages--Transliteration into English*).

Subdivisions of this type that are established under the pattern headings for languages are not free-floating. To determine whether a subdivision used under a pattern heading is free-floating, consult the list of subdivisions below.

### F. Period subdivisions

If the period subdivisions listed below and established under *English language* are not appropriate for another language, establish the appropriate subdivision under the specific language, for example, *Russian language--To 1300*.

Further subdivisions. Topical and form subdivisions valid for *English language* in general may also be used under any specific period subdivision, for example, *English language--Middle English, 1100-1500--Etymology--Names*.

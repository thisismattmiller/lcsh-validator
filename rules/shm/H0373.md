# H 373 "Used For" References

## Validation rules

1. A string that appears as a UF (4XX) reference is not a valid heading; replace it with the authorized heading it refers to. Typical UF patterns: inverted forms (`Driving, Truck` for `Truck driving`; `Abbot, Mount` for `Mount Abbot`), alternate order of nationality/ethnic qualified headings (`Irish songs` for `Songs, Irish`; `French art` for `Art, French`; `Arts, Mexican American` for `Mexican American arts`), and variant spellings (`Doorknobs` for `Door knobs`, `Glamor photography` for `Glamour photography`, `Historical farms` for `Historic farms`, `Clustered housing` for `Cluster housing`, `Serials (Publications)` for `Serial publications`, `Bus vandalism` for `Buses--Vandalism`). (H 373 sec. D.1–D.2, D.7)
2. Forms differing only in the presence of a hyphen, diacritic, or capital letter normalize to the same string as the heading; a validator should compare headings after this normalization and treat such variants as matching the authorized heading (but output the authorized form). (H 373 sec. E.2)
3. A former heading or a cancelled phrase heading converted to `[topic]--[free-floating subdivision]` (or a pattern-controlled subdivision) is kept as a UF; the old form is invalid. (H 373 sec. D.3)
4. For `[Topic A] and [Topic B]` headings the reversed form is a UF, not valid: `Arts and feminism` -> `Feminism and the arts`. (H 373 sec. D.8)
5. For topics subdivided by other topics the reversed form is a UF, not valid: `Newspapers--Advertising` -> `Advertising--Newspapers`; `Airlines--Strikes and lockouts` -> `Strikes and lockouts--Airlines`. (H 373 sec. D.9)
6. If a concept is better known by an acronym or abbreviation, the heading uses that form with an explanatory parenthetical qualifier, e.g. `DDT (Insecticide)`, and the full form is a UF; when the full form is the heading, the acronym (with qualifier) is a UF. (H 373 sec. D.5)
7. Topical headings generally have no UFs from foreign-language equivalents, except terms in widespread English use (e.g. `Esprit de corps` UF for `Morale`); named entities have romanized vernacular UFs. (H 373 sec. D.6, E.4)
8. A free-floating subdivision occasionally established under a heading (e.g. `Mice--Control`) has no UFs; `--Control` is normally free-floating under animals. (H 373 sec. E.6)

### B. Definitions

"Used For" references: Also known as cross-references, see references; UFs.

### C. General rule

After deciding which term or phrase is to represent a concept when formulating a new heading, add all other equivalent terms and phrases found while conducting authority research as UF references to the new heading. Place these references in alphabetical order.

Example:

```
150 ## $a Bait fishing
450 ## $a Bobber fishing
450 ## $a British float fishing
450 ## $a Fishing with natural bait
450 ## $a Float fishing, British
450 ## $a Ledgering (Fishing)
450 ## $a Livebait fishing
450 ## $a Still fishing
```

### D. Types of "Used For" references

### D.1. References beginning with significant words

In addition to the above UFs, rearrange the words of the new heading and its alternative forms so that additional UFs are provided under each significant word in the file position, as long as the resulting combination of words represents an expression under which a catalog user might logically be expected to search. The standard means of accomplishing this is by inverted references, although phrase equivalents of the inverted forms are also used.

Examples:

| Heading | UF references |
|---|---|
| *Truck driving* | Driving, Truck |
| *Songs, Irish* | Irish songs |
| *Mount Abbot* | Abbot, Mount |
| *Business intelligence* | Business espionage; Corporate intelligence; Espionage, Business; Espionage, Industrial; Intelligence, Corporate |

Exception: Do not make an inverted UF in instances where a BT is made that begins with the same word or words as the UF or where the heading is in a hierarchy leading up to the same word(s) except as noted in sec. D.7, below.

Examples:

```
150 ## $a Talking birds
550 ## $w g $a Birds
[The UF Birds, Talking is not made because the BT Birds begins with the same word.]

150 ## $a Freshwater plants
550 ## $w g $a Aquatic plants
[The UF Plants, Freshwater is not made because Aquatic plants has the BT Plants.]
```

### D.2. References from alternative spellings, word endings, etc.

If alternative spellings are possible, including singular and plural forms, alternative word endings, etc., make additional UFs covering these possibilities.

Examples:

| Heading | UF references |
|---|---|
| *Door knobs* | Doorknobs |
| *Cluster housing* | Clustered housing |
| *Glamour photography* | Glamor photography |
| *Historic farms* | Historical farms |
| *Serial publications* | Serials (Publications) |
| *Buses--Vandalism* | Bus vandalism |

See sec. E below for instructions on when not to make references from alternate spellings.

### D.3. References from changed or cancelled headings

If a heading is changed to a new form, or if a phrase heading is cancelled and converted to a heading of the type *[topic]--[free-floating subdivision]*, or *[topic]--[free-floating subdivision controlled by a pattern heading]*, make a UF from the old form or from the cancelled heading to the new heading (cf. H 193, sec. D.5.a).

### D.4. Upward "Used For" references

Occasionally it is appropriate to make UF references from other than strictly synonymous terms or phrases in order to inform the catalog user that a particular concept is entered under another, usually broader, heading. A UF reference from a specific term to a broader heading is referred to as an "upward" reference.

Examples:

```
150 ## $a Bait fishing
450 ## $a Worm fishing
```

Make references of this type only when it is clearly impractical to establish specific headings for the separate concepts.

### D.5. Abbreviations, acronyms, etc.

When a concept is well known both in its fully spelled out form and as an acronym or abbreviation, make UFs from the abbreviated forms if the full form is selected as the form of the heading. Add an explanatory parenthetical qualifier to the acronym or abbreviation.

If a concept is better known as an acronym or in abbreviated form, select that form for the heading, for example, *DDT (Insecticide)*. Make a UF from the full form of the heading.

### D.6. Foreign terms

In general, do not add UFs to topical headings from their equivalents in foreign languages. However, such references may be made from foreign terms that are in widespread use in English-language sources.

Example:

```
150 ## $a Morale
450 ## $a Esprit de corps
```

Note: This restriction does not apply to headings for named entities, for which UFs from vernacular forms are regularly made when the heading is established in English. When making vernacular UF references for those types of headings, provide the reference in romanized form.

### D.7. Headings qualified by nationality, ethnic group, or language

For all such headings, whether established in direct or inverted form, make a UF from the alternative form.

Examples:

```
150 ## $a Art, French
450 ## $a French art

150 ## $a Mexican American arts
450 ## $a Arts, Mexican American
550 ## $w g $a Arts—United States
```

Note: Make an inverted UF of this type even if a BT is made that begins with the same word or words.

See H 374 for instructions on making general see references from non-used variant forms of national and ethnic adjectival qualifiers.

### D.8. [...] and [...] headings

For headings established in the form *[Topic A] and [Topic B]*, make a UF from the reversed form (cf. H 310).

Example:

```
150 ## $a Feminism and the arts
450 ## $a Arts and feminism
```

### D.9. Topics subdivided by other topics

For headings constructed in the form of a single heading subdivided by various other topical headings, make a UF from the reversed form.

Examples:

```
150 ## $a Advertising $x Newspapers
450 ## $a Newspapers $x Advertising

150 ## $a Strikes and lockouts $x Airlines
450 ## $a Airlines $x Strikes and lockouts
```

### E. When to omit UF references

### E.1. First word would match BT

Do not make an inverted UF in instances where a BT is made that begins with the same word or words as the UF or where the heading is in a hierarchy leading up to the same word(s) except as noted in sec. D.7, above.

### E.2. Diacritics and normalization

Do not make a UF reference from a form in which the only difference is the presence of a hyphen, diacritic, or capital letter because these forms "normalize" to the same string of characters as the heading itself.

### E.3. Variations controlled on a base heading

In headings based on another heading, do not make UF references that repeat alternative spellings or variants that are already covered by a UF reference in the basic heading. For example, do not make the UF *Communication in paediatrics* on the heading *Communication in pediatrics* because the basic heading *Pediatrics* already has the UF *Paediatrics*.

### E.4. Foreign language equivalents

In general, do not add UFs to topical headings from their equivalents in foreign languages. See sec. D.6, above, for exceptions.

### E.5. Subheadings

Generally, when establishing a heading for a named entity in the subject authority file, do not add a UF in the form of a subheading under a higher body. Do not, for example, add a UF of the following type:

Example:

```
110 2# $a Filene Center (Va.)
[not 410 2# $a Wolf Trap National Park for the Performing Arts (Va.). Filene Center]
```

References of this type are normally made only for headings established in the name authority file.

### E.6. Free-floating subdivisions

Some subdivisions that are normally free-floating are occasionally established because of special circumstances. In such situations, make no UFs. For example, the heading *Mice--Control* does not have UFs such as Control of mice or Extermination of mice because the subdivision *--Control* is normally free-floating under animals.

### F. "Used For" references in change proposals

Regardless of the kind of change being proposed, all UFs should also be evaluated as part of the change proposal process. Ensure all UFs, including older UFs, are supported by source citations in the 670 fields, performing research and recording additional 670 fields as needed. See H 193, sec. D.7 for instructions on how to record research for references that cannot be supported by citations.

# H 290 Articles in the Initial Position

## Validation rules

1. A subject heading must not begin with a definite or indefinite article. Reject topical headings beginning with English `The `, `A `, `An `. (H 290 sec. 1)
2. The prohibition applies equally to foreign-language articles in the nominative case (e.g. `La `, `Le `, `Les `, `L'`, `El `, `Los `, `Las `, `Il `, `Lo `, `Gli `, `I `, `Der `, `Die `, `Das `, `Ein `, `Eine `, `De `, `Het `, `Un `, `Une `, `Uno `, `Una `, and other equivalents): `Questione della lingua`, not `La questione della lingua`. (H 290 sec. 1)
3. Where the article is retained as part of the concept, it is inverted to follow the noun after a comma: `Finite, The` (not `The finite`). (H 290 sec. 1)
4. Prefer the form without the article when it is not needed: `Arts`, not `The arts`. (H 290 sec. 1)
5. Geographic names beginning with an article are governed by H 690 sec. 6, not by this rule; do not reject a geographic name solely for an initial article without consulting that sheet. (H 290 sec. 2)
6. When a title of a publication (uniform title) is used as a subject heading, omit all initial articles that precede words in the nominative case. An initial article in a title subject heading is an error unless the following word is not in the nominative case. (H 290 sec. 3)

## Full text

### 1. General rule.

Do not establish subject headings with an article (definite or indefinite) in the initial position. This instruction applies both to English articles and to all foreign language equivalents in the nominative case. Examples:

```
150 ## $a Arts
   [not 150 ## $a The arts]

150 ## $a Finite, The
   [not 150 ## $a The finite]

150 ## $a Questione della lingua
   [not 150 ## $a La questione della lingua]
```

### 2. Geographic names.

For geographic names beginning with an article, see H 690, sec. 6.

### 3. Titles of publications.

Omit all initial articles that precede words in the nominative case when assigning titles as subject headings. For further details about the assignment of uniform titles as subject headings, see H 1435.

# H 1538 Diaries

## Validation rules

1. `--Diaries` is a free-floating form subdivision (`$v Diaries`) used under names of individual diarists, e.g. `600 10 $a Mann, Thomas, $d 1875-1955 $v Diaries.` (H 1538 sec. E)
2. Assign the names of individual diarists, subdivided by `--Diaries`, up to a maximum of three; if there are more than three diarists, do not designate any individual writer. (H 1538 sec. E)
3. `[class of persons or ethnic group]--Diaries` is valid when the writer(s) can be identified with a particular subject area, discipline, or ethnic group, e.g. `African Americans--Diaries`; `Dramatists, German--20th century--Diaries`; `Episcopal Church--Clergy--Diaries`; `Painters--Great Britain--Diaries`; `Women plantation owners--Southern States--Diaries`. (H 1538 sec. F)
4. `--Diaries` follows geographic and chronological subdivisions in the string (e.g. `Painters--Great Britain--Diaries`; `Novelists, German--20th century--Diaries`). (H 1538 sec. F, examples)
5. For a single diarist, do not assign both a class of persons heading and an ethnic group heading; prefer the class of persons heading. (H 1538 sec. F)
6. Do not add `--Sources` or `--History--Sources` to additional headings assigned for special topics discussed in diaries. (H 1538 sec. G)
7. Additional special-topic headings may include `[place]--Biography` (cf. H 1330) or `[individual war or event]--Personal narratives` (cf. H 1928), e.g. `World War, 1939-1945--Personal narratives, French`. (H 1538 sec. G)
8. Literary genre headings of the type `American diaries`; `English diaries`; `Japanese diaries` are assigned only to collections of diaries by more than one writer classed as literary works; if the collection is not limited to an individual body of literature, use `Diaries`. (H 1538 sec. H)
9. This sheet applies to true personal diaries only, not to memoirs, reminiscences, or personal narratives not in diary entry form, nor to official logs. (H 1538 sec. A)
10. [LC practice] LC catalogers no longer use the form subdivision `--Diaries`; assign the headings without it and add `655 #7 $a Diaries. $2 lcgft`. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign LCDGT (e.g. `386 ## $a Germans $2 lcdgt`). (H 1538 sec. A, D)
11. [LC practice] Assign a class of persons or ethnic group heading (without `--Diaries`) if that is a topic in the diary; special topics may be expressed with `[place]` or `[individual war or event]` headings without `--Biography` or `--Personal narratives`, with LCGFT `Diaries` or `Personal narratives`. (H 1538 sec. F, G)
12. [LC practice] Continue to supply a literary genre subject heading (e.g. `English diaries`) when it brings out the nationality and/or ethnicity of the writers; do not supply a subject heading that only expresses the genre/form, use LCGFT instead; optionally assign creator characteristics with LCDGT. (H 1538 sec. H)

## Full text

### A. Background

Diaries are registers or records of personal experiences, observations, thoughts, or feelings, kept daily or at frequent intervals. They are usually written as an aid to memory or reflection and are customarily intended for private rather than public use. The procedures below describe the specific treatment of works consisting of true personal diaries. This instruction sheet is not intended to cover memoirs, reminiscences, or personal narratives of events that are not in diary entry form and contemporaneous with the events described. It is also not intended to be applied to official logs of activities.

LC practice: As stated in H 1075, LC catalogers will no longer use form subdivisions. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT), e.g. *Diaries*. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign Library of Congress Demographic Groups Terms (LCDGT) to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *German speakers* or *Arabic, Students of*.

For guidelines on bio-bibliography, see H 1328. For guidelines on biography, see H 1530. For guidelines on interviews, see H 1678. For guidelines on personal narratives of wars or disasters, see H 1928. For guidelines on genealogy and family histories, see H 1631.

### C. Definitions

Diaries: registers or records of personal experiences, observations, thoughts, or feelings, kept daily or at frequent intervals, customarily intended for private use

### D. Array of headings

Assign the following array of headings to individual diaries or collections of diaries.

LC practice: Assign an appropriate combination of subject heading(s) for an individual diary or collection of diaries. See the more detailed guidance below regarding each category of subject heading. Also assign LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Diaries*.

```
Model:
600 X0 $a [name of diarist(s)] $v Diaries.
650 #0 $a [class of persons, or ethnic group] $v Diaries.
650 #0 $a [special topics discussed in the diary or diaries].
LC practice:
600 X0 $a [name of diarist(s)].
650 #0 $a [class of persons, or ethnic group].
650 #0 $a [special topics discussed in the diary or diaries].
655 #7 $a Diaries. $2 lcgft
```

### E. Individual diarists

Assign as subject headings the name or names of the individual writers of the diaries, up to a maximum of three. Subdivide the name(s) by the free-floating form subdivision *--Diaries*. If there are more than three diarists, do not designate any individual writer.

LC practice: Assign as subject headings the name or names of the individual writers of the diaries, up to a maximum of three. Also assign the LCGFT term appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Diaries*.

### F. [Class of persons, or ethnic group]--Diaries

Assign headings of the type *[class of persons or ethnic group]--Diaries* if the writer(s) of the diary or diaries can be identified with a particular subject area or discipline or a particular ethnic group.

LC practice: Assign a subject heading for classes of persons or ethnic group if that is a topic in the diary. Also assign the LCGFT term appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Diaries*.

Examples:

### G. Special topics

If the diary contains information about topics not covered by headings of the type *[class of persons]--Diaries* or *[ethnic group]--Diaries*, assign additional headings for the topics, including headings such as *[place]--Biography* (cf. H 1330, sec. 1.c.) or *[Individual war or event]--Personal narratives* (cf. H 1928), if appropriate.

Since the diary or diaries of any individual person may or may not be regarded as historical source material, depending upon the viewpoint of the reader, do not add the subdivision *--Sources* or *--History--Sources* to the additional headings assigned for special topics.

LC practice: If the diary contains information about topics not covered the types of headings described in Sections E-F, assign additional headings for the topics, including headings such as *[place]* (cf. H 1330, sec. 1.c.) or *[Individual war or event]* (cf. H 1928), if appropriate. Also assign the LCGFT term appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Diaries* or *Personal narratives*.

### H. Diaries as a literary genre

Headings of the type *American diaries*; *English diaries*; *Japanese diaries*; etc., are literary genre headings. Assign such headings only to collections of diaries by more than one writer that are classed as literary works. If the collection is not limited to an individual body of literature, assign the heading *Diaries*.

LC practice: Assign an appropriate combination of subject heading(s) for a diary or diaries. If a subject heading for literary genre brings out the nationality and/or ethnicity of the writers, continue to supply it. Optionally, assign the creator characteristics with LCDGT, e.g. *Germans* and/or *Novelists*. If it is describing just the genre/form of the content, do not provide a subject heading to express that. Instead, assign LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Diaries*.

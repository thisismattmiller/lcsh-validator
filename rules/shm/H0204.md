# H 204 Evaluating Subject Proposals

## Validation rules

1. This sheet describes LC's editorial workflow and contains no rules about the form of heading strings. (H 204)
2. A concept already expressible by an existing heading or a `[heading]--[subdivision]` combination (including one constructed on a free-floating basis) does not need a new heading; a proposed new heading duplicating such a combination is "Not necessary". (H 204 sec. E, F)
3. Headings are expected to conform to patterns and precedents in LCSH with respect to wording, form, and style, and to the guidelines in the SHM, and to use neutral (unbiased), clear, unambiguous terminology. (H 204 sec. E)

## Full text

### A. Background

Proposals are made by catalogers who determine that either 1) the topic addressed in a work is not yet represented in Library of Congress Subject Headings and should be added to the vocabulary, or 2) the topic is represented by an existing heading, but the record should be revised to increase access to the topic. The revisions may take the form of changes to the heading itself or to the references or scope note.

This instruction sheet describes the subject proposal workflow within the Policy, Training, and Cooperative Programs Division (PTCP). It also outlines in general terms the issues that policy specialists consider as they review the proposals that catalogers submit for consideration.

### B. Recent revisions

2025-08: Updated template. Added policy statement.

### C. Definitions

Tentative List: List of subject heading proposals to be evaluated by the Library of Congress.

Approved List: List of subject heading proposals approved for use in bibliographic records, authority records, and other places, and added to the Library of Congress Subject Headings official database.

### D. Compilation of Tentative Lists

The proposal is scheduled for a Tentative List by PTCP.

Tentative Lists are published monthly and include all of the proposals that are under consideration in that month. The lists are made publicly available on the SACO home page (http://www.loc.gov/aba/pcc/saco/index.html).

PTCP accepts comments on the proposals from LC and SACO catalogers, other library and archive professionals, and members of the general public. The email address to which comments should be directed is listcomments@loc.gov, and is also provided at the top of each list. The comment period begins when the Tentative List for a particular month is posted and continues until the date indicated on the list.

### E. Review of the Tentative List

The policy specialists consider the following factors for each proposal that appears on the Tentative List.

- Is the proposed heading appropriate for the work being cataloged?
- Is the concept already represented in LCSH, either through a main heading or a [heading]--[subdivision] combination?
- Has sufficient research been provided, according to the guidelines in H 202? Cataloging policy specialists may supply additional research on a case-by-case basis.
- Are the citations for both the work being cataloged and any reference sources consulted brief, clear, and understandable to users of authority records, as defined in H 203? Do they support the choice of the heading and references, and the scope note (if present)?
- Does the proposed heading reflect the terminology commonly used to refer to the concept, according to the sources consulted?
- Is the proposed heading clear and unambiguous? Should it be qualified?
- Does the proposed heading employ neutral (i.e., unbiased) terminology?
- Does the proposed heading conform to patterns and precedents in LCSH with respect to wording, form, and style, and to guidelines provided in the SHM?
- Do the UFs, BTs, and RTs conform to patterns and precedents in LCSH, and to guidelines provided in the SHM?
- Is a scope note necessary to help catalogers and users understand the meaning of the proposed heading?
- If a classification number is provided, does it conform to the guidelines for inclusion of classification numbers as provided in H 365?

Each proposal is unique, so other factors may be considered for individual proposals. For example, if a proposal relates to foreign policy and a "strong sense of Congress" (as demonstrated by overwhelming bipartisan and bicameral support of a resolution or bill) can be determined, policy specialists consider the congressional action in addition to the position of the U.S. Department of State. Likewise, policy specialists consider laws and information in legal reference sources when appropriate.

The Library of Congress is responsible for the growth, maintenance, and management of its standard, the Library of Congress Subject Headings (LCSH). Over the years LCSH's global impact has grown and the Library, recognizing this, put policies in place for the community to submit proposals while maintaining its authority to make final decisions on headings added. As such, in line with ANSI/NISO Z39.19-2005 (R2010) Guideline for the Construction, Format, and Management of Monolingual Controlled Vocabularies, in exceptional circumstances the Library may invoke organizational warrant and reject a proposal.

Additional factors are considered if the proposal was made to revise an existing heading. They include the following.

- Is the proposed revision well-supported by the research provided?
- Does the proposed revision seek to remove pejorative or otherwise offensive terminology?
- Will the proposed revision enhance access to library resources? Would library users find it easier to discover resources of interest to them if the proposed change were to be approved?

### F. Determination of the disposition of the proposals

One of five statuses is assigned to each proposal. The statuses are defined as follows.

- **Approved.** The proposal was accepted as submitted, or as revised by cataloging policy specialists.
- **Not approved.** The proposal was not appropriate for LCSH.
- **Not necessary.** The proposal was not appropriate for the resource being cataloged, or a heading for the concept heading already exists in LCSH. If the latter, the heading is either printed in LCSH or may be constructed on a free-floating basis.
- **Resubmit.** The proposal was not accepted in its current form, but the cataloger may revise it and return it to PTCP for another review.
- **Withdrawn.** The proposal was removed from consideration that month. It may appear on a future list or may be removed from consideration altogether.

### G. Writing of the Summary of Decisions

The policy specialist in charge of the list writes the Summary of Decisions, which is the official record of the reasons why individual proposals on a Tentative List were marked not approved, not necessary, or resubmit. The summaries also occasionally include announcements about categories of headings. New, revised, or deleted free-floating subdivisions are also reported there.

The Summary of Decisions is made publicly available on LC's Acquisitions and Bibliographic Access webpage at http://www.loc.gov/aba/pcc/saco/cpsoed/cpsoeditorial.html.

### H. Editing of the accepted proposals

Assistant editors in PTCP edit the authority records for approved proposals in accordance with decisions made by the cataloging policy specialists. The edits are then passed back to the cataloging policy specialists for a final review.

### I. Compilation of the Approved List

PTCP compiles and posts the Approved List. The Approved List includes all of the proposals that were accepted on that list and reflects all of the changes that the cataloging policy specialists made to those proposals. It is made publicly available on the Acquisitions and Bibliographic Access website at http://www.loc.gov/aba/cataloging/subject/weeklylists/.

### J. Completion of the bibliographic file maintenance

PTCP revises the subject headings in existing bibliographic records as appropriate to reflect newly approved proposals.

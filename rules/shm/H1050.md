# H 1050 Washington (D.C.)

## Validation rules

1. Use `Washington (D.C.)` as a subject heading or geographic subdivision for works on the city of Washington or the District of Columbia. (H 1050 sec. 1)
2. Do not use `District of Columbia` as a subject heading (discontinued 1981 for legal/administrative works) except as an element of corporate name headings, e.g. `District of Columbia. Board of Education`, `District of Columbia. Juvenile Court`. (H 1050 Background, sec. 2)
3. As a geographic subdivision, use `Washington (D.C.)` directly after topics (not through any state or `United States`). (H 1050 sec. 3)
4. Use `(Washington, D.C.)` as the geographic qualifier for entities in Washington, D.C., e.g. `Vietnam Veterans Memorial (Washington, D.C.)`. (H 1050 sec. 4)
5. Use `Washington Metropolitan Area`, `Washington Suburban Area`, and `Washington Region` without a geographic qualifier; as geographic subdivisions assign them directly after topics. (H 1050 sec. 5)
6. In 550 broader term fields for features or buildings in the city, subdivide geographically by `Washington (D.C.)`, e.g. `War memorials--Washington (D.C.)`. (H 1050 sec. 6)

## Full text

### BACKGROUND

Washington (D.C.) is used in subject headings for the city of Washington and the District of Columbia. The practice of assigning District of Columbia to works of a legal or administrative nature was discontinued in 1981. For classification of works on Washington and the District of Columbia, see F 750.

### 1. Usage of Washington (D.C.)

Use the heading Washington (D.C.) as a subject heading or geographic subdivision for works on the city or district.

### 2. Usage of District of Columbia

The name heading District of Columbia may be used as a subject heading only when it is an element of corporate body name headings such as District of Columbia. Board of Education or District of Columbia. Juvenile Court.

### 3. Geographic subdivision practice

As a geographic subdivision, use Washington (D.C.) directly after topics.

### 4. Geographic qualifier

Use the form (Washington, D.C.) as a geographic qualifier when establishing headings for entities in Washington, D.C.

### 5. Washington Metropolitan Area, Washington Suburban Area, and Washington Region

According to the definitions in H 790, use the headings Washington Metropolitan Area, Washington Suburban Area, and Washington Region, without a geographic qualifier, as the headings for the areas associated with the city. When using these headings as geographic subdivisions, assign them directly after topics.

### 6. Subdividing broader term fields

When adding 550 (broader term) fields for geographic features, or for individual buildings, structures, etc., use Washington (D.C.) to subdivide the reference geographically. Example:

```
150 ## $a Vietnam Veterans Memorial (Washington, D.C.)
550 ## $w g $a War memorials $z Washington (D.C.)
```

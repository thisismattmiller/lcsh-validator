# H 1330 Biography

## Validation rules

1. Do not subdivide a personal name heading by `--Biography`. (H 1330 sec. D.1)
2. Exception: `--Biography` is established and valid under exactly four individual names: `Shakespeare, William, 1564-1616--Biography`, `Muhammad, Prophet, -632--Biography`, `Jesus Christ--Biography`, and `Mary, Blessed Virgin, Saint--Biography`. Under any other personal name, `--Biography` is invalid, and proposals to establish it under other individuals are not accepted. (H 1330 sec. D.1)
3. [LC practice] LC uses the four names above without `--Biography` and assigns LCGFT (e.g. `Biographies`) instead; the subject authority records for the four `--Biography` headings remain valid for external institutions. (H 1330 sec. D.1)
4. Free-floating use of `--Biography` after headings for individual literary authors was discontinued in 1998; `[literary author]--Biography` is invalid except `Shakespeare, William, 1564-1616--Biography`. (H 1330 sec. D.1.a)
5. When a biography focuses on one aspect of a person's life, subdivide the name heading with an H 1110 topical subdivision (e.g. `--Childhood and youth`, `--Health`, `--Death and burial`), not `--Biography`. (H 1330 sec. D.1)
6. For an author with several name headings, use the "base heading" (the one with the complete set of references). (H 1330 sec. D.1.a)
7. For a person with both an official and a personal name heading, use the personal name heading in biography (e.g. `Reagan, Ronald`, not `United States. President (1981-1989 : Reagan)`). (H 1330 sec. D.1.b)
8. `--Biography` is a free-floating form subdivision (subfield `$v`) used after headings for classes of persons: `[Class of persons]--Biography` (e.g. `Musicians--Biography`, `Italy--Kings and rulers--Biography`). It may follow a geographic subdivision of the class of persons (e.g. `Costume designers--California--Los Angeles--Biography`). (H 1330 sec. D, D.2, D.2.b)
9. Headings qualifying a class of persons by sex or ethnic group (e.g. `Women architects`, `African American lawyers`) take `--Biography` in the same way and are assigned in addition to the unqualified class-of-persons heading. (H 1330 sec. D.2.b)
10. Where no class-of-persons heading exists or can be formulated for a collective biography, `--Biography` may be established under a topical heading (e.g. `Art--Biography`). (H 1330 sec. D.2.c)
11. `--Biography` may be used under headings for places, organizations, and events (e.g. `Ohio--Biography`, `United States. Department of Agriculture--Officials and employees--Biography`, `World War, 1939-1945--Biography`, `Texas Christian University--Alumni and alumnae--Biography`, `United States--Armed Forces--Biography`). (H 1330 sec. D.3, G)
12. `--Biography` may be followed by a further form subdivision, e.g. `Cape Breton Island (N.S.)--Biography--Anecdotes`. (H 1330 sec. G)
13. Chronological subdivisions may precede `--Biography`, e.g. `Authors, Canadian (English)--20th century--Biography`. (H 1330 sec. G)
14. Other topical headings assigned to a biography carry no biographical subdivisions. (H 1330 sec. D.4)
15. Assign no more than four personal name headings to a biography; for collective biographies of more than four persons, omit name headings. (H 1330 sec. D, D.1.c)
16. For founders of major religions, assign no class-of-persons heading. (H 1330 sec. E.1)
17. For spouses with no career of their own, the class-of-persons heading is a specific spouse category (e.g. `Ambassadors' spouses--Biography`, `Entertainers' spouses--Biography`). (H 1330 sec. E.3)
18. Partial biographies (less than 50% biographical) receive only the name heading (optionally with H 1110 subdivisions) and topical headings; do not assign class-of-persons, sex, ethnic group, place, or organization headings with `--Biography`. (H 1330 sec. F)
19. The provisions apply only to predominantly biographical narrative works, not predominantly critical works. (H 1330 sec. A)
20. [LC practice] LC no longer uses form subdivisions, including `--Biography`; it assigns the class-of-persons, place, organization, event, or topic heading without `--Biography` and adds LCGFT (e.g. `Biographies`, `Autobiographies`, `Personal narratives`) and, where a demographic group was formerly expressed, LCDGT. (H 1330 sec. A, D, D.2, D.2.c, D.3)

## Full text

### A. Background

A work about an individual person may be either biographical, critical, or both. Biographical works focus on the personal aspects of the individual's life, critical works on the individual's professional, intellectual, or artistic achievements. The provisions in this instruction sheet do not apply to works that are predominantly critical, only to works that are predominantly biographical and in narrative form.

LC practice: As stated in H 1075, LC catalogers will no longer use form subdivisions. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT), e.g. *Biographies*. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign Library of Congress Demographic Groups Terms (LCDGT) to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *German speakers* or *Arabic, Students of*.

For guidelines on bio-bibliography, see H 1328. For guidelines on diaries, see H 1538. For guidelines on interviews, see H 1678. For guidelines on personal narratives of wars or disasters, see H 1928. For guidelines on genealogy and family histories, see H 1631. For classification of biography, see F 275 (General); F 605, sec. 1 (Government officials); and F 632 (Literary authors writing in more than one language).

### B. Recent revisions

2026-01: Updated template. Added instructions related to LCGFT implementation.

### C. Definitions

Biography (including Autobiography): A narrative work more than 50% of which recounts the personal aspects of the life of one or more individuals. (In the case of historical figures, particularly those from the distant past, where little is known of the personal details of their lives, the limitation of the definition to "personal aspects" does not apply.) Personal aspects include such details as the individual's early years, education, marriage and other personal relationships, personal habits and personality, family life, travels, personal experiences and tragedies, last years and death, etc. Life means a relatively large portion of the individual's life, not just a single brief incident.

Collective biography: A biography of two or more individuals.

Individual biography: A biography of one person.

Partial biography: A work that includes some, but less than 50%, biographical material.

### D. General rule

Assign an appropriate combination of the following types of headings to a biography:

- the name heading(s) for the person(s) [no more than four]
- if appropriate, a "class of persons" heading with the free-floating form subdivision *--Biography*
- if appropriate, heading(s) to bring out the person's association with a place or organization, or involvement with a specific event
- topical headings, as appropriate for the work

Each of these headings is discussed specifically in sections D.1-D.4 below.

LC practice: Assign an appropriate combination of subject heading(s) for a biography. Also assign LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Biographies* or the more specific term.

### D.1. Name heading for the person

Assign the heading as established in RDA form in the name authority file. Do not subdivide the name by *--Biography*.

If the biography focuses on a specific aspect of the person's life subdivide the name heading using the appropriate topical subdivision from H 1110, for example, *--Childhood and youth*, *--Health*, *--Death and burial*. Assign this heading as the first heading.

Note Exception: *--Biography* has been established and may be used under the names of four individuals: *Shakespeare, William, 1564-1616*; *Muhammad, Prophet, -632*; *Jesus Christ*; and *Mary, Blessed Virgin, Saint*. Do not submit a proposal to establish *--Biography* under the name of any other individual.

LC practice: For the four names that are editorially established with the *--Biography* subdivision, use instead the name only. The existing subject authority records for those four names will continue to exist in order to accommodate existing practice for external institutions. Instead assign LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Biographies* or the more specific term.

### D.1.a. Literary authors

For literary authors who write under various names and have more than one name heading, use the heading that serves as the "base heading" for the author, that is, the heading having the complete set of references for the author. Free-floating use of the subdivision *--Biography* after headings for individual literary authors was discontinued in 1998. The subdivision is established under *Shakespeare, William, 1564-1616*, and continues to be used under that heading as an exceptional practice.

LC practice: Note exception above for LC practice for the four editorially established names.

### D.1.b. Official name heading vs. personal name heading

For individuals who have both an official name heading and a personal name heading, use the personal name heading. For example, use *Reagan, Ronald* rather than *United States. President (1981-1989 : Reagan)*.

### D.1.c. Collective biography

If the work is about two, three, or four persons, assign the name heading for each. If the work is about more than four persons, omit the name headings.

### D.2. [Class of persons]--Biography

This heading designates the group of people to which the biographee belongs, as discussed in the work being cataloged, for example, *Musicians*, *Heads of state*, *Football coaches*, *Italy--Kings and rulers*, etc.

LC practice: Assign the subject term for the class of persons. Also assign LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Biographies* or the more specific term.

Note: This heading is assigned to individual biographies primarily for the benefit of public library users who are seeking biographies of a particular type of person rather than a particular individual. The heading should be selected with that in mind. If the biographee belongs to no discernible class of persons of the type judged likely to be sought by the typical public library user, it may be omitted.

### D.2.a. Persons with multifaceted lives or careers

For a biography that discusses the entire life of a person, select the single heading that best encompasses his or her career or lifelong pursuits. In order to achieve consistency, check the bibliographic database first to determine the heading that has been used on other biographies of the same person. If a single heading cannot adequately encompass the person's career or lifelong pursuits, assign more than one heading, provided that the work being cataloged discusses the various aspects of the person's life designated by the headings assigned. If the work focuses on only one facet, assign only that one heading. For example, if a work discusses Albert Schweitzer's experiences only as a medical missionary, assign a heading for only that aspect of his career.

### D.2.b. Headings qualified by sex or ethnic group

Assign headings that specify the sex or ethnic group of the class of persons, such as *Women architects*, *African American lawyers*, etc., if that is a significant aspect of the work. When assigning this heading, assign it in addition to the unqualified heading for the class of persons.

Example:

```
Title: "Just make them beautiful" : the many worlds of a designing
woman / by Helen Rose.
600 10 $a Rose, Helen, $d 1904-1985.
650 #0 $a Costume designers $z California $z Los Angeles $v Biography.
650 #0 $a Women costume designers $z California $z Los Angeles $v Biography.
LC practice:

### D.2.c. Collective biography with no class of persons

If a collective biography encompasses a group of people for which a heading representing a class of persons does not exist and cannot be formulated, establish the subdivision *--Biography* under the appropriate topical heading. For example, establish *Art--Biography* for a collective biography of artists, dealers, collectors, museum personnel, etc.

LC practice: Assign the subject heading for the topic when a class of persons does not exist and cannot be formulated. Also assign LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Biographies* or the more specific term.

### D.3. Headings for place, organization, or event

Assign headings of this type, with the subdivision *--Biography*, in addition to the class of persons heading described above, if that is a significant aspect of the work.

LC practice: Assign the subject heading for the place, organization, or event. Also assign LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Biographies* or the more specific term.

Example:

```
651 #0 $a Ohio $v Biography.
610 10 $a United States. $b Department of Agriculture $x Officials and
employees $v Biography.
650 #0 $a World War, 1939-1945 $v Biography.
LC practice:

employees.

### D.4. Topical headings

Assign headings for other topics discussed in the work, using appropriate subject headings without biographical subdivisions.

### E. Special situations

### E.1. Founders of major religions

Assign no heading to designate the class of persons.

### E.2. Animals and pets

See H 1720, sec. 3.

### E.3. Spouses of famous people

For a biographical work about a famous person's spouse who is or was active in a particular field, assign the heading for the class of persons that designates the spouse's own field. If the spouse has no special career of his or her own, and the work describes personal experiences as the spouse of a more famous person and his or her career, assign the heading for the specific category of spouses, for example, *Ambassadors' spouses*, *Entertainers' spouses*, etc., as the "class of persons" heading. If appropriate, also assign headings for the name of the more famous person and the class of persons to which he or she belongs.

### E.4. Immigrants

See H 1581, sec. 3.c.

### F. Partial biography

To works about an individual person that are less than 50% biographical, assign only the name of the person, subdivided if appropriate by subdivisions listed in H 1110, and the appropriate topical headings. Do not assign headings to designate the class of persons, or the individual's sex, ethnic group, association with a place, organization, etc. Examples of this type of work are critical works about individual artists, literary authors, philosophers, theologians, etc., as well as works about the life and times of individual statesmen or rulers in which there is more about the history of the time than the life of the ruler.

# H 1158 Pattern Headings: Materials

## Validation rules

1. Subdivisions on this list are free-floating under headings for individual materials and types of materials (e.g. *Dental materials*; *Foam rubber*; *Glass*; *Laminated metals*; *Plastics*; *Plywood*; *Porous materials*). (H 1158, Types of headings covered)
2. The list also applies under headings formed with `--Materials` under scientific and technical disciplines and types of equipment and construction (e.g. *Telecommunication--Materials*; *Space vehicles--Materials*; *Nuclear reactors--Materials*). (H 1158, Types of headings covered)
3. Do not use this list free-floating under the heading *Materials*. (H 1158, Types of headings covered)
4. Substances such as *Polyethylene* or *Nonferrous metals* follow the materials pattern when treated as basic substances from which something is made (engineering properties, processing, suitability) and the chemicals pattern (H 1149) when treated from the standpoint of chemical structure, effects, reactions. (H 1158, Types of headings covered)
5. A listed subdivision may be used free-floating if appropriate and no conflict exists in the subject authority file. (H 1158, Conflicts)
6. Only subdivisions marked (May Subd Geog) may be followed by a geographic subdivision. (H 1158, list)
7. `--Corrosion` is not valid under the heading *Metals*. (H 1158, note 1)
8. `--Metallography` and `--Metallurgy` are not valid under the heading *Metals*; use them only under individual metals and groups of metals. (H 1158, note 3)
9. `--Microscopy` is not valid under the heading *Metals*; use under materials other than metals; under individual metals and groups of metals use `--Metallography`. (H 1158, note 4)
10. `--Radioactive contamination` is not valid under materials that are inherently radioactive. (H 1158, note 5)
11. `--Law and legislation` and `--Transportation--Law and legislation` may be further subdivided by the legal-topic subdivisions in H 1154.5. (H 1158, note 2)
12. `--Religious aspects` may be subdivided by a religion or Christian denomination only via editorially established headings *[material]--Religious aspects--[name of religion or denomination]* (see H 1998). (H 1158, note 6)
13. `--Specifications` is a form subdivision ($v) and may be subdivided geographically; it may also be used as $x for works about the form. (H 1158, list; H 1075 sec. 1.d)
14. [LC practice] If an exceptional variant form is retained, make a UF reference from the equivalent free-floating subdivision form (H 195); otherwise propose a change (H 193). (H 1158, Conflicts)

## Full text

August 2020

PATTERNS: *Concrete*; *Metals*

### Types of headings covered by the pattern

Headings for individual materials and types of materials. Examples: *Dental materials*; *Foam rubber*; *Glass*; *Laminated metals*; *Plastics*; *Plywood*; *Porous materials*. Included are headings formed using the free-floating subdivision --Materials under scientific and technical disciplines and under types of equipment and construction, for example, *Telecommunication--Materials*; *Space vehicles--Materials*; *Nuclear reactors--Materials*. The category does not include the heading *Materials*. Some overlap exists with the category for chemicals (H 1149). Headings for individual substances and types of substances such as *Polyethylene* and *Nonferrous metals* should follow the pattern for materials when they are discussed as basic substances from which something can be made, including their engineering properties, processing, suitability for intended use, etc. They should follow the pattern for chemicals when discussed from the standpoint of their chemical structure, effects, reactions, etc.

### Conflicts

Any subdivision listed here can be used as a free-floating subdivision under any heading belonging to the category if it is appropriate and no conflict exists in the subject authority file. Subject authority records may exist for headings employing variant phrases or subdivisions equivalent to subdivisions on this list.

LC practice: If an exceptional variant form is to be retained, make a UF reference from the equivalent free-floating subdivision form following the procedures in H 195 if the reference does not yet exist. Otherwise, submit a proposal to change the variant form along with all bibliographic records requiring correction following the procedures in H 193.

Note: Most form subdivisions coded $v in this list may also be used as topical subdivisions coded $x when assigned to works about the form (see H 1075, sec. 1.d.).

# H 1430 Comics and Comic Characters

## Validation rules

1. `--Comic books, strips, etc.` is a form subdivision (`$v`) used for comics/graphic novels under: names of biographees (`Aristotle--Comic books, strips, etc.`); historical events, periods, and entities (`World War, 1939-1945--Soviet Union--Comic books, strips, etc.`, `Saint Petersburg (Russia)--History--Siege, 1941-1944--Comic books, strips, etc.`); `Animals` or specific animals (`Horses--Comic books, strips, etc.`); nonfiction topical headings; classes of persons; settings; topics; and character names. (H 1430 sec. E.1-E.3, G, I.4-I.6)
2. Other form subdivisions may be interposed before or added after `--Comic books, strips, etc.`, e.g. `--Biography--Comic books, strips, etc.` (`Philosophers--Greece--Biography--Comic books, strips, etc.`), `--Comic books, strips, etc.--Periodicals` (`Dogs--Comic books, strips, etc.--Periodicals`). (H 1430 sec. E.4)
3. Do not add juvenile form subdivisions to `--Comic books, strips, etc.` (e.g. `--Comic books, strips, etc.--Juvenile fiction` or `--Juvenile literature` after it is invalid); assign a separate heading with `--Juvenile literature` (nonfiction) or `--Juvenile fiction`. (H 1430 sec. E.4)
4. `--Comic books, strips, etc.` under a historical event/period is assigned only when the event is the principal focus, not merely the backdrop. (H 1430 sec. E.2)
5. Works of criticism about a comic character get the character heading (and if appropriate the strip/cartoon heading); do not use `--History and criticism` under character headings. (H 1430 sec. F.2)
6. Groups of comic characters are established as subject headings; individual characters are in the name authority file, e.g. `Brown, Charlie (Fictitious character)`, `Guardian (Fictitious character from DC Comics, Inc.)`. Same-named unrelated characters get separate headings distinguished in the qualifier. (H 1430 sec. F.1, I.4.a)
7. Works about themes in comics: form heading with `--History and criticism` (e.g. `Comic books, strips, etc.--United States--History and criticism`, `Graphic novels--History and criticism`) plus `[topic] in comics` (e.g. `Politics in comics`, `War in comics`, `Holocaust, Jewish (1939-1945), in comics`, `National characteristics, American, in comics`). (H 1430 sec. H.1, H.3, J)
8. `[topic] in comics` headings, the free-floating subdivision `--In comics`, and the non-free-floating subdivision `--Comics and the war, [revolution, uprising, etc.]` were authorized effective March 2022. (H 1430 sec. H.1)
9. `--In comics` is free-floating (`$x`) under names of individual persons and families (including dynasties and royal houses), e.g. `Hemingway, Ernest, 1899-1961--In comics`, `Romanov, House of--In comics`, `Bender family--In comics`. (H 1430 sec. H.4)
10. `--In comics` is free-floating under individual corporate bodies, places, and sacred works, e.g. `United States. Federal Bureau of Investigation--In comics`, `Tahiti (French Polynesia : Island)--In comics`, `Bible--In comics`, `Qurʼan--In comics`. (H 1430 sec. H.6)
11. Criticism of comics about imaginary persons: the character's name (no `--In comics`), plus if appropriate `[author]--Characters`, e.g. `Doyle, Arthur Conan, Sir, 1859-1930--Characters` and `Holmes, Sherlock`. (H 1430 sec. H.5)
12. Comics of a specific war, revolution, uprising, etc.: `[name of event]--Comics and the war, [revolution, uprising, etc.]` as a topical subdivision, e.g. `World War, 1939-1945--Comics and the war`, `Korean War, 1950-1953--Comics and the war`, `Algeria--History--Revolution, 1954-1962--Comics and the revolution`. It is not free-floating (not in H 1200) and every instance must be editorially established. (H 1430 sec. H.7)
13. Works about individual graphic novels, strips, or cartoons get the authorized title or name-title heading, e.g. `Schulz, Charles M. (Charles Monroe), 1922-2000. Peanuts`. (H 1430 sec. H.2)
14. Fictitious characters created by the author get a heading only if the character appears in three or more works; imaginary places or organizations only if in three or more works, e.g. `Gotham City (Imaginary place)`. (H 1430 sec. I.4.a, I.5)
15. Do not assign headings for vague topics (fate, evil, belief, psychology, interpersonal relations, emotions, social customs, community life), and do not assign both a broader and a more specific heading. (H 1430 sec. I.6)
16. [LC practice] LC no longer uses form subdivisions such as `--Comic books, strips, etc.`, `--Biography`, `--Juvenile fiction`; it assigns the heading without them and adds LCGFT (`Comics (Graphic works)`, `Graphic novels`, `Biographical comics`, `Historical comics`, `Animal comics`, `Nonfiction comics`, `Superhero comics`, `Comics criticism`, etc.) and LCDGT (e.g. `Children`, `Teenagers`). In case of doubt the LCGFT term is `Comics (Graphic works)`. (H 1430 sec. D, E, G, I)

### A. Recent revisions

2026-02: Replaced LCGFT *Literary criticism* with *Comics criticism* in example.

### B. Background

This instruction sheet provides guidelines for assigning genre/form terms and subject headings to represent the content of graphic novels and selections from comic strips and cartoons; and also for assigning subject headings to works about individual comic strips, single panel cartoons, and comic characters.

It is subject cataloging policy to assign a genre/form term to selections from a comic strip or single panel cartoon, and to graphic novels; for single works of fiction, assign headings to bring out topic(s) and form as described below. For classification of comic books and graphic novels, see the Library of Congress Classification and Shelflisting Manual F 565.

For works that consist of selections from a comic strip or single panel cartoon, as well as for works about a comic strip or cartoon, it is descriptive cataloging policy to provide an added entry for the title of the strip or cartoon when that title differs from the work being cataloged, and to justify the added entry by means of a note if necessary. Descriptive catalogers are responsible for establishing these headings.

### C. Definitions

The following definitions apply for the purposes of this instruction sheet.

Consider to be a comic book:

- A collection or selection of comic strips or cartoons;
- A comic book cataloged as a serial; or,
- Selected issues of serial comics that have been collected and republished as monographs.

Consider to be a graphic novel any work created specifically for the publication in hand and that is complete, or intended to be complete, in one or more volumes.

Comics and graphic novels are considered a form that includes both the written word and visual art. They include both fiction and nonfiction genres.

### D. General rule

Assign terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT) (e.g., *Comics (Graphic works)*; *Graphic novels*) according to the guidelines in J 110 of the Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms Manual. In case of doubt, assign the LCGFT term *Comics (Graphic works)*.

LC practice: Assign subject headings according to the guidelines in section E below, which follow general principles for fiction presented in H 1790, section E. For an alternative approach to fictional comics, see the section I below.

### E. Selections of comic strips or cartoons and original works of fiction, including graphic novels

LC practice: As stated in H 1075, LC catalogers will no longer use form subdivisions. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT), e.g. *Comics (Graphic works)*, *Graphic novels*, or *Detective and mystery comics*. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign Library of Congress Demographic Group Terms (LCDGT) to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*.

### E.1. Biographical fiction

Assign the heading *[name of biographee]--Comic books, strips, etc.*.

LC practice: Do not use a form subdivision of the type *--Comic books, strips, etc.*. Instead, assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Comics (Graphic works)* or *Graphic novels*. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign LCDGT to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*.

Example:

```
Title: Aristotle : a graphic biography.
600 10 $a Aristotle $v Comic books, strips, etc.
650 #0 $a Philosophers $z Greece $v Biography $v Comic books, strips, etc.
650 #0 $a Philosophy, Ancient $v Comic books, strips, etc.
LC practice:
600 10 $a Aristotle.
650 #0 $a Philosophers $z Greece.
650 #0 $a Philosophy, Ancient.
655 #7 $a Biographical comics. $2 lcgft
655 #7 $a Comics (Graphic works) $2 lcgft
655 #7 $a Biographies. $2 lcgft
```

### E.2. Historical fiction

Assign headings for specific historical events, periods, etc., with the subdivision *--Comic books, strips, etc.*, e.g. *World War, 1939 1945--Comic books, strips, etc.*; *San Francisco Earthquake and Fire, Calif., 1906--Comic books, strips, etc.*.

Do not assign a heading of this type when the event or period is merely the backdrop to a story. Assign it only when the event or period is the principal focus of the work.

Interpret the term *historical fiction* broadly to include works about entities such as movements, corporate bodies or other jurisdictions, camps, parks, structures, geographical features other than regions, ethnic groups, disasters, categories of events, etc.

LC practice: Do not use a form subdivision of the type *--Comic books, strips, etc.*. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Comics (Graphic works)* or *Historical comics*. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign LCDGT to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*.

Example:

```
Title: The lions of Leningrad.
650 #0 $a World War, 1939-1945 $z Soviet Union $v Comic books, strips, etc.
651 #0 $a Saint Petersburg (Russia) $x History $y Siege, 1941-1944 $v Comic
books, strips, etc.
LC practice:
650 #0 $a World War, 1939-1945 $z Soviet Union.
651 #0 $a Saint Petersburg (Russia) $x History $y Siege, 1941-1944.
655 #7 $a Historical comics. $2 lcgft
655 #7 $a Graphic novels. $2 lcgft
```

### E.3. Animal stories

Assign the heading *Animals--Comic books, strips, etc.* to individual novels or stories about animals in general. For a work about a specific type of animal, assign a heading for the type of animal with the subdivision *--Comic books, strips, etc.*, e.g. *Horses--Comic books, strips, etc.*.

LC practice: Do not use a form subdivision of the type *--Comic books, strips, etc.*. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Comics (Graphic works)* or *Animal comics*. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign LCDGT to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*.

Example:

```
Title: Elephants : living large.
650 #0 $a Elephants $v Comic books, strips, etc.
650 #0 $a Elephants $v Juvenile literature.
LC practice:
650 #0 $a Elephants.
655 #7 $a Animal comics. $2 lcgft
655 #7 $a Graphic novels. $2 lcgft
655 #7 $a Nonfiction comics. $2 lcgft
385 ## $a Children $2 lcdgt
```

### E.4. Assignment of additional form subdivisions

The form subdivision *--Comic books, strips, etc.* denotes both fiction and non-fiction works. In general, other form subdivisions may be added or interposed if necessary, e.g. *--Biography--Comic books, strips, etc.*; *--Comic books, strips, etc.--Periodicals*.

However, do not add form subdivisions for juvenile literature to *--Comic books, strips, etc.*. For juvenile works assign an additional heading subdivided by *--Juvenile literature* (for nonfiction works) or *--Juvenile fiction*.

LC practice: Do not use a form subdivision of the type *--Comic books, strips, etc.*, *--Biography*, *--Juvenile fiction*, etc. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Comics (Graphic works)*, *Biographies*, etc. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign LCDGT to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*.

### F. Comics characters

### F.1. Establishing headings for characters

Establish new subject headings for groups of characters in graphic novels, comic strips, or single panel cartoons under their names (cf. H 1610). Names of individual characters are established in the name authority file.

### F.2. Assignment of headings for individual characters or groups of characters

Assign such headings to works of criticism about a particular comic or cartoon character. If appropriate, assign also the heading for the strip or cartoon itself. Do not use the subdivision *--History and criticism*. Do not assign headings for characters to fictional works featuring the characters unless following the guidelines in section I below.

### G. Nonfiction written in comic book or graphic novel style

Assign the appropriate subject heading array. Subdivide each heading by *--Comic books, strips, etc.*. Also assign a genre/form term from LCGFT (e.g., *Graphic novels*) according to the guidelines in the LCGFT manual.

LC practice: Do not use a form subdivision of the type *--Comic books, strips, etc.*. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Comics (Graphic works)*, *Nonfiction comics*, etc. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign LCDGT to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*.

Example:

```
Title: The graphic history of hip hop.
650 #0 $a Hip-hop $v Comic books, strips, etc.
650 #0 $a Hip-hop $x History $v Comic books, strips, etc.
650 #0 $a Rap (Music) $x History $v Comic books, strips, etc.
LC practice:
650 #0 $a Hip-hop.
650 #0 $a Hip-hop $x History.
650 #0 $a Rap (Music) $x History.
655 #7 $a Comics (Graphic works) $2 lcgft
655 #7 $a Nonfiction comics. $2 lcgft
```

### H. History and criticism

### H.1. General rule

For works about particular themes in comics, assign as many headings as necessary to bring out the form (with subdivision *--History and criticism*) and the topic(s) (normally using headings of the type *[topic] in comics*).

Note: Headings of the type *[topic] in comics*, the free-floating subdivision *--In comics*, and the non-free-floating subdivision *--Comics and the war, [revolution, uprising, etc.]* were authorized for use effective March 2022.

Examples:

```
650 #0 $a Comic books, strips, etc. $z United States $x History and
criticism.
650 #0 $a Politics in comics.
650 #0 $a Korean War, 1950-1953 $x Comics and the war.
```

### H.2. Works about individual graphic novels, comic strips, or cartoons

Assign the authorized title or name-title heading for a graphic novel, comic strip, or single panel cartoon to works about such comics.

### H.3. Works about graphic novels, comic strips, or cartoons, treated collectively

Assign form and topical headings following the general principles in H 180 to bring out the form (e.g., *Graphic novels*; *Science fiction comic books, strips, etc.*), place of origin, and/or special topics discussed. Subdivide form headings by *--History and criticism* (e.g., *Graphic novels--History and criticism*).

### H.4. Comics about individual persons and families

For criticism of comics about particular individuals or families, including dynasties and royal houses, assign the name of the person or family with the free-floating subdivision *--In comics*.

Examples:

```
600 10 $a Hemingway, Ernest, $d 1899-1961 $x In comics.
600 10 $a Shakespeare, William, $d 1564-1616 $x In comics.
600 30 $a Romanov, House of $x In comics.
600 30 $a Bender family $x In comics.
```

### H.5. Comics about imaginary persons

For criticism of comics about particular imaginary individuals, assign the name of the person. Also assign if appropriate for the work the name of the author with the subdivision *--Characters* (cf. H 1610).

Example:

```
600 10 $a Doyle, Arthur Conan, $c Sir, $d 1859-1930 $x Characters.
600 10 $a Holmes, Sherlock.
```

### H.6. Comics about individual corporate bodies, places, and sacred works

For criticism of comics about particular corporate bodies, places, or sacred works, assign the name of the entity with the free-floating subdivision *--In comics*.

Examples:

```
610 10 $a United States. $b Federal Bureau of Investigation $x In comics.
651 #0 $a Tahiti (French Polynesia : Island) $x In comics.
630 00 $a Bible $x In comics.
630 00 $a Qurʼan $x In comics.
```

### H.7. Comics about wars and similar events

For works about the comics of a specific war, revolution, uprising, etc., assign *[name of event]--Comics and the war, [revolution, uprising, etc.]* as a topical heading (cf. H 1200).

Note: The free-floating subdivisions under Wars (H 1200) will not include this subdivision. Establish editorially all instances of this subdivision, in accordance with the policy in H 1090.

Examples:

```
650 #00 $a World War, 1939-1945 $x Comics and the war.
651 #0 $a Algeria $x History $y Revolution, 1954-1962 $x Comics and the
revolution.
```

### I. Special provisions for increased subject access to fictional comics

### I.1. Application of special provisions

The Library of Congress applies special provisions for increased subject access to fictional comics as internal resources permit. As of April 2022, these provisions may be applied to cataloging for current acquisitions of fictional comic strips, cartoons, and graphic novels. As of 2026, it is no longer LC practice to assign headings that include form subdivisions.

### I.2. General principles

The assignment of subject headings to individual works of fictional comics is intended to provide the library users with an additional method of discovering and selecting comics. Several types of subject headings are available to provide this access. Any, all, or none of the following categories of headings may be appropriate for an individual fictional comic: form/genre, character, setting, topic. These headings should be assigned only as they come readily to mind after an initial review of the work being cataloged.

### I.3. Form and genre headings

Select an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms for Library and Archival Materials (LCGFT), for example, *Biographical comics*; *Spy comics*. Assign terms from LCGFT in accordance with the instructions in J 235 of the LCGFT manual. For MARC coding of LCGFT, see J 105.

### I.4. Character(s)

### I.4.a. Individual characters

If the work prominently features a real person, a legendary character, or other major character not created by the author of the work, assign a subject heading for the name of the character with the subdivision *--Comic books, strips, etc.*. If the primary character is a fictitious character created by the author, assign a subject heading only if the character appears in three or more works. For instructions on establishing subject headings for fictitious characters see H 1610.

LC practice: Do not use a form subdivision of the type *--Comic books, strips, etc.*. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Comics (Graphic works)*, *Graphic novels*, etc. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign LCDGT to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*.

Note: Fictitious characters may be borrowed by an author from another author, or from another source, and used in the creation of a new work. Assign the same heading to works by the original creator of the character and to works in which the character has been borrowed. Assign a separate heading when characters share a name but are clearly unrelated to each other.

Example:

```
600 00 $a Guardian $c (Fictitious character from DC Comics, Inc.) $v Comic
books, strips, etc.
LC practice:
600 00 $a Guardian $c (Fictitious character from DC Comics, Inc.)
655 #7 $a Superhero comics. $2 lcgft
```

Example:

```
600 00 $a Guardian $c (Fictitious character from Marvel Comics Group) $v
Comic books, strips, etc.
LC practice:
600 00 $a Guardian $c (Fictitious character from Marvel Comics Group)
655 #7 $a Superhero comics. $2 lcgft
```

### I.4.b. Classes of persons

A heading may also be assigned for the class of persons to which the primary character belongs if that class of persons is established and is likely to be sought by library users, for example, women detectives or teenage superheroes. Generally follow the same principles that are used for selecting class-of-persons headings for individual biographies (cf. H 1330). Subdivide the heading with the subdivision *--Comic books, strips, etc.*.

LC practice: Do not use a form subdivision of the type *--Comic books, strips, etc.*. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Comics (Graphic works)*, *Graphic novels*, etc. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign LCDGT to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*.

### I.5. Setting

Assign a subject heading for a place, event, or time period that is featured prominently in an individual work. Subdivide the heading with the subdivision *--Comic books, strips, etc.*. Since almost all fictitious works have a specific place and time, assign these headings only if the place or time is significant. Generally do not assign headings for a time period that is contemporaneous to the period in which the work was written, for example, do not express the 19th-century setting of a work that was written in the 19th century.

Assign a subject for an imaginary place or organization only if the place appears in three or more works. The place of the work may also be included as a subdivision of a topical heading, in accordance with normal subject cataloging practice, either in addition to a separate heading for place or as a substitute for a separate heading. Subdivide the heading with the subdivision *--Comic books, strips, etc.*.

LC practice: Do not use a form subdivision of the type *--Comic books, strips, etc.*. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Comics (Graphic works)*, *Graphic novels*, etc. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign LCDGT to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*.

### I.6. Topical access

Assign headings for specific topics that are the focal point of a fictional comic. Assign headings only for topics that have been made explicit by the author or publisher, such as those topics that are mentioned in the title, series, introductory matter, dustjacket, or other prominent location. The purpose of the topical heading is to provide access for those topics that distinguish the work from most other works.

Do not assign headings for vague and general topics, such as fate, evil, belief, psychology, interpersonal relations, emotions, social customs, or community life. The same principle of specificity should be applied as is used in non-literary cataloging. Do not assign both a broader and more specific heading to the same work; assign only the most specific heading that is appropriate. Some will have no topical heading.

Subdivide the heading with the subdivision *--Comic books, strips, etc.*.

LC practice: Do not use a form subdivision of the type *--Comic books, strips, etc.*. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Comics (Graphic works)*, *Graphic novels*, etc. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign LCDGT to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*.

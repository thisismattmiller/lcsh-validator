# H 1149.7 Pattern Headings: Comics and Graphic Novels

## Validation rules

1. Subdivisions on this list are free-floating under headings for individual genres and forms of comics and graphic novels, including the general heading *Comic books, strips, etc.* (e.g. *Biographical comic books, strips, etc.*; *Graphic novels*; *Manga (Comic books)*; *Romance comic books, strips, etc.*). (H 1149.7 sec. B)
2. Headings for comics and graphic novels, including *Comic books, strips, etc.*, may be subdivided geographically (e.g. `650 #0 $a Detective and mystery comic books, strips, etc. $z Brazil.`). (H 1149.7 sec. B.2)
3. A geographic subdivision may be followed by topical and form subdivisions (e.g. `650 #0 $a Horror comic books, strips, etc. $z Japan $x Technique.`). (H 1149.7 sec. B.2)
4. Do not combine a geographic subdivision with a period subdivision in either order: [not `650 #0 $a Comic books, strips, etc. $z Argentina $y 21st century.`]; [not `650 #0 $a Comic books, strips, etc. $y 21st century $z Argentina.`]. Assign additional headings instead. (H 1149.7 sec. B.2, E.1)
5. Do not combine a geographic subdivision with an author-group subdivision in either order: [not `650 #0 $a Graphic novels $z Spain $x Women authors.`]; [not `650 #0 $a Graphic novels $x Women authors $z Spain.`]. (H 1149.7 sec. B.2, F.1)
6. Period subdivisions (`--15th century` through `--21st century`) may be followed by topical or form subdivisions (e.g. `650 #0 $a Graphic medicine (Comic books) $y 21st century $x Publishing.`) but may not be combined with author-group subdivisions [not `650 #0 $a Manga (Comic books) $y 20th century $x Buddhist authors.`; not `650 #0 $a Manga (Comic books) $x Buddhist authors $y 20th century.`]. (H 1149.7 sec. E.1, F.1)
7. Author-group subdivisions may be followed by topical or form subdivisions (e.g. *Comic books, strips, etc.--Jewish authors--History and criticism*; *Science fiction comic books, strips, etc.--Black authors*). (H 1149.7 sec. F.1)
8. A topical subdivision may not be followed by a period subdivision or by an author-group subdivision: [not `... $x History and criticism $y 20th century.`]; [not `... $x History and criticism $x Women authors.`]. (H 1149.7 sec. G.1)
9. A topical subdivision not marked May Subd Geog may not be followed by a geographic subdivision: [not `650 #0 $a Underground comic books, strips, etc. $x History and criticism $z United States.`]; `--Social aspects` may be (e.g. *Fantasy comic books, strips, etc.--Social aspects--Germany*). (H 1149.7 sec. G.1)
10. Only `--Appreciation`, `--Censorship`, `--Conservation and restoration`, `--Expertising`, `--Political aspects`, `--Publishing`, and `--Social aspects` on the topical list may be subdivided geographically. (H 1149.7 sec. G.2)
11. `--[nationality/culture] influences` subdivisions are not valid after period subdivisions or under headings for comics identified in the subdivision. (H 1149.7 sec. G.2)
12. `--Audio adaptations`, `--Film adaptations`, and `--Television adaptations` ($x) are not valid after period subdivisions. (H 1149.7 sec. G.2)
13. Do not use `--Film and video adaptations`; use `--Film adaptations` and/or `--Television adaptations`. (H 1149.7 sec. G.2, H.2)
14. `--Black authors` is printed with the note "Not valid under headings for American literature or its genres." (H 1149.7 sec. F.2)
15. Religious author-group subdivisions denote authorship (e.g. *Comic books, strips, etc.--Christian authors*); comics with religious themes use phrase headings (e.g. *Christian comic books, strips, etc.*). (H 1149.7 sec. F.1)
16. For author groups other than national, ethnic, or religious groups, use phrase headings on the pattern *Children’s writings, English--[place]* or *Prisoners’ writings, French--[place]*. (H 1149.7 sec. F.1)
17. Topical subdivisions from H 1148 and H 1156 sec. G, and form subdivisions from H 1148 and H 1156 sec. H, are usable under comics headings. (H 1149.7 sec. G.1, H.1)
18. `--Translations` is not used for single translations. (H 1149.7 sec. G.2)
19. If a listed period subdivision is not appropriate for a specific heading, the appropriate subdivision must be established under that heading. (H 1149.7 sec. E.1)
20. [LC practice] LC catalogers no longer use form subdivisions; they assign LCGFT terms (and LCDGT terms for demographic groups) instead. Form subdivisions remain valid LCSH for other institutions. (H 1149.7 sec. H.1)
21. [LC practice] Establish new author-group subdivisions for national, ethnic, or religious groups only, under the pattern headings for literature and for comics and graphic novels. (H 1149.7 sec. F.1)
22. [LC practice] If an exceptional variant form is retained, make a "Used for" reference from the equivalent free-floating subdivision form (H 195); otherwise propose a change (H 193). (H 1149.7 sec. D)

## Full text

Pattern: *Biographical comic books, strips, etc.*

### A. Recent revisions

2025-04: Created instruction sheet.

### B. Types of headings covered by the pattern

Headings for individual genres and forms of comics and graphic novels, including the general heading *Comic books, strips, etc.* Restrictions are noted in each section. Examples: *Biographical comic books, strips, etc.*; *Graphic novels*; *Manga (Comic books)*; *Romance comic books, strips, etc.*

### B.1. General rule

Comics and graphic novels are considered to be a form that includes both the written word and visual art. Therefore, the free-floating subdivisions in this instruction sheet are drawn from pattern headings for art (H 1148) and literature (H 1156).

Period subdivisions are listed first, author groups second, and topical and form subdivisions last, which is the proper order for considering these elements when formulating subject headings about particular themes in comics and graphic novels.

### B.2. Geographic subdivision

Headings for comics and graphic novels — including the heading *Comic books, strips, etc.* — may be subdivided geographically. Headings with geographic subdivisions may be further subdivided by topical and form subdivisions from section F and G below. Period and author group subdivisions from section D and section E below may not be combined in a single heading with geographic subdivisions. Instead, assign additional headings to bring out those aspects.

Examples:

```
650 #0 $a Detective and mystery comic books, strips, etc. $z Brazil.
[Base heading] + [Geographic subdivision]

650 #0 $a Horror comic books, strips, etc. $z Japan $x Technique.
[Base heading] + [Geographic subdivision] + [Topical or form subdivision]

[not 650 #0 $a Comic books, strips, etc. $z Argentina $y 21st century.]

[not 650 #0 $a Graphic novels $z Spain $x Women authors.]
```

### C. Conflicts

Any subdivision listed here can be used as a free-floating subdivision under any heading belonging to the category if it is appropriate and no conflict exists in the subject authority file. Subject authority records may exist for headings employing variant phrases or subdivisions equivalent to subdivisions on this list.

### D. LC practice

If an exceptional variant form is to be retained, make a “Used for” reference from the equivalent free-floating subdivision form following the procedures in H 195 if the reference does not yet exist. Otherwise, submit a proposal to change the variant form along with all bibliographic records requiring correction following procedures in H 193.

### E. Period subdivisions

### E.1 General rule

Use the following free-floating general period subdivisions derived from H 1148, section 1. Period subdivisions may be followed by topical and form subdivisions from section G and H below, with the noted exceptions, but may not be combined with author group subdivisions from section F below, nor used after headings for comics or graphic novels with geographic subdivision. If there are specific headings where these subdivisions are not appropriate, establish the appropriate subdivision under the specific heading.

Examples:

```
650 #0 $a Queer comic books, strips, etc. $y 20th century.
[Base heading] + [Period subdivision]

650 #0 $a Graphic medicine (Comic books) $y 21st century $x Publishing.
[Base heading] + [Period subdivision] + [Topical or form subdivision]

[not 650 #0 $a Comic books, strips, etc. $y 21st century $z Argentina.]

[not 650 #0 $a Manga (Comic books) $y 20th century $x Buddhist authors.]
```

### F. Author group subdivisions

### F.1. General rule

Use the free-floating author group subdivisions listed in H 1156, section E, to denote author groups that identify subordinate bodies of comic or graphic novel genres. Author group subdivisions may be followed by any topical or form subdivision from section G and H below but may not be combined in a single heading with period subdivisions from section E above nor used after headings with geographic subdivisions.

Following this pattern, establish new subdivisions for national, ethnic, or religious groups only. Establish new subdivisions under the pattern heading for literature and for comics and graphic novels. For other author groups, follow the pattern of the phrase headings, *Children’s writings, English--[place]* or *Prisoners’ writings, French--[place]*.

Subdivisions for religious author groups are used for comics and graphic novels written by authors who are members of the group, whether or not the comic or graphic novel includes religious themes. For example, assign *Comic books, strips, etc.--Christian authors* to comics or graphic novels by Christians. Comics and graphic novels with religious themes are entered under phrase headings for the genre, for example, *Christian comic books, strips, etc.*

Examples:

```
650 #0 $a Science fiction comic books, strips, etc. $x Black authors.
[Base heading] + [Author group subdivision]

650 #0 Comic books, strips, etc. $x Jewish authors $x History and criticism.
[Base heading] + [Author group subdivision] + [Topical or form subdivision]

[not 650 #0 $a Graphic novels $x Women authors $z Spain.]

[not 650 #0 $a Manga (Comic books) $x Buddhist authors $y 20th century.]
```

These subdivisions may also be used under period subdivisions from section E above, or author group subdivisions from section F above with the noted exceptions. They may also be used after headings with geographic subdivision.

```
650 #0 $a Documentary comic books, strips, etc. $x Authorship.
[Base heading] + [Topical subdivision]

[The subdivision *--Social aspects* may be subdivided geographically.]

[not 650 #0 $a Underground comic books, strips, etc. $x History and criticism $z United States.]
[The subdivision *--History and criticism* may not be subdivided geographically.]

[not 650 #0 $a Biographical comic books, strips, etc. $x History and criticism $y 20th century.]

```

### H. Form subdivisions

### H.1. General rule

Use the free-floating form subdivisions listed in H 1148 and in H 1156, section H. These subdivisions may also be used under period subdivisions from section E above, or author group subdivisions from section F above with the noted exceptions. They may also be used after headings with geographic subdivision.

LC practice: As stated in H 1075, Library of Congress catalogers will no longer use form subdivisions. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT) to bring out the form(s) and/or genre(s) of the resource being cataloged. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign Library of Congress Demographic Group Terms (LCDGT) to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged. The form subdivisions maintained on this list are not available for use by LC catalogers. Catalogers at institutions external to LC may use them as dictated by their own institutional policies.

# H 1156 Pattern Headings: Literatures

## Validation rules

1. Subdivisions on this list are free-floating under headings for individual literatures and genres of those literatures (e.g. *Swedish literature*; *French drama*; *German essays*; *Epic poetry, Finnish*; *Short stories, Chinese*). (H 1156 sec. B)
2. Appropriate subdivisions may also be used under *[region or country]--Literatures* (e.g. *America--Literatures*). (H 1156 sec. B)
3. Do not use this list under *Literature*, under unqualified genre headings such as *Drama* or *Poetry*, or under non-belletristic genres such as *Scientific literature*. (H 1156 sec. C)
4. Order of elements: literature heading, then period subdivision OR author-group subdivision (or geographic subdivision for genres), then topical/form subdivisions. (H 1156 sec. B, E.1, F.1, G.1)
5. A period subdivision ($y) may not be combined in the same string with an author-group subdivision (e.g. not *English literature--20th century--Catholic authors*). (H 1156 sec. E.1, F.1)
6. Period subdivisions may not be used after headings for literatures or literary genres that are subdivided geographically. (H 1156 sec. E.1)
7. Author-group subdivisions may not be used after headings for literary genres with geographic subdivisions. (H 1156 sec. F.1)
8. Period subdivisions are used only after *[ . . . ] literature* and the major genres *[ . . . ] fiction*, *[ . . . ] drama*, *[ . . . ] poetry*, *[ . . . ] essays*, *[ . . . ] prose literature*; not after other genres (e.g. not after *Short stories, Chinese*). (H 1156 sec. E.1)
9. Period subdivisions are not free-floating under headings with a parenthetical language qualifier (e.g. *Nigerian fiction (English)*); they may be individually established under European literatures with such qualifiers (e.g. *Belgian literature (French)--20th century*; *Swiss poetry (German)--Early modern, 1500-1700*). (H 1156 sec. E.1)
10. Period subdivisions under literatures and genres other than drama: `--Old English, ca. 450-1100`; `--Middle English, 1100-1500`; `--Early modern, 1500-1700`; `--18th century`; `--19th century`; `--20th century`; `--21st century`. (H 1156 sec. E.2.a)
11. Period subdivisions under drama: `--To 1500`; `--Early modern and Elizabethan, 1500-1600`; `--17th century`; `--Restoration, 1660-1700`; `--18th century`; `--19th century`; `--20th century`; `--21st century`. (H 1156 sec. E.2.b)
12. Where the listed periods are inappropriate for a literature, special periods must be established under the individual literature or genre (not free-floating). (H 1156 sec. E)
13. An author-group subdivision that also designates the literature itself is invalid under that literature: *English literature--Celtic authors* is valid, *Celtic literature--Celtic authors* is not. (H 1156 sec. F.1)
14. `--Black authors` is not valid under headings for American literature or its genres. (H 1156 sec. F.2)
15. Do not formulate headings such as *French literature--African authors*; literatures of independent nations of Africa, Asia, and the Pacific are established as independent literatures (e.g. *African literature (French)*). (H 1156 sec. F.1)
16. For external author groups (living outside the country associated with the literature) use simple geographic subdivision (e.g. *German literature--Romania*), not an author-group subdivision; for non-national/ethnic/religious author groups use phrase headings like *Children’s writings, English--[place]* or *Prisoners’ writings, French--[place]*. (H 1156 sec. F.1)
17. Religious author-group subdivisions (e.g. *American poetry--Christian authors*) denote authorship by group members; literature with religious themes uses phrase headings (e.g. *Christian poetry, American*). (H 1156 sec. F.1)
18. Topical and form subdivisions may follow period or author-group subdivisions (with the noted exceptions) and may follow headings for literary genres with geographic subdivisions. (H 1156 sec. G.1, H.1)
19. `--[nationality/culture] influences` subdivisions (e.g. `--African influences`, `--Chinese influences`, `--Western influences`) are not valid after period subdivisions or under headings for the literature identified in the subdivision. (H 1156 sec. G.2)
20. `--English influences` is not established under *English literature* and is not valid under period subdivisions. (H 1156 sec. G.2)
21. `--Audio adaptations`, `--Comics adaptations`, `--Film adaptations`, `--Graphic novel adaptations`, and `--Television adaptations` ($x) are not valid after period subdivisions. (H 1156 sec. G.2)
22. Do not use `--Film and video adaptations`; use `--Film adaptations` and/or `--Television adaptations`. (H 1156 sec. G.2, H.2)
23. `--Foreign countries` ($z) is used under individual literatures qualified by language or nationality; `--Foreign countries` and `--Foreign countries--History and criticism` are not valid under subdivisions for periods, groups of authors, or places. (H 1156 sec. G.2)
24. Only `--Appreciation`, `--Censorship`, `--Political aspects`, `--Publishing`, and `--Social aspects` on this list may be subdivided geographically. (H 1156 sec. G.2)
25. `--Translations` ($x/$v) is not used for single translations; `--Translations into French, [German, etc.]` takes a language name in place of the bracketed examples. (H 1156 sec. G.2, H.2)
26. [LC practice] LC catalogers no longer use form subdivisions; they assign LCGFT terms (and LCDGT terms for demographic groups) instead. Form subdivisions remain valid LCSH for other institutions. (H 1156 sec. H.1)
27. [LC practice] Establish new author-group subdivisions only for internal national, ethnic, or religious groups. (H 1156 sec. F.1)
28. [LC practice] If an exceptional variant form is retained, make a UF reference from the equivalent free-floating subdivision form (H 195); otherwise propose a change (H 193). (H 1156 sec. D.2)

## Full text

Pattern: *English literature*

### A. Recent revisions

2026-01: Added instructions related to LCGFT implementation.

### B. Types of headings covered by the pattern

This instruction sheet contains the free-floating subdivisions that may be used under headings for individual literatures and genres of those literatures.

Examples of these literature headings:

```
Swedish literature
French drama
German essays
Epic poetry, Finnish
Short stories, Chinese
```

Appropriate subdivisions may also be used under headings formed using the free-floating subdivision *--Literatures* under names of regions and countries, for example, *America--Literatures*.

Period subdivisions are listed first (section E), author groups second (section F), and topical and form subdivisions last (sections G and H), to suggest the proper order for considering these elements in the formulation of literature subject headings. Limitations on combining multiple subdivisions in a single string appear in the appropriate sections below.

### C. Types of headings not covered by the pattern

The category does not include the general heading *Literature*, general headings for genres without adjectival qualifiers, such as *Drama* or *Poetry*, nor non-belletristic genres such as *Scientific literature*.

### D. Conflicts

### D.1. General practice

Any subdivision listed here can be used as a free-floating subdivision under any heading belonging to the category if it is appropriate and no conflict exists in the subject authority file. Subject authority records may exist for headings employing variant phrases or subdivisions equivalent to subdivisions on this list.

### D.2. LC practice

If an exceptional variant form is to be retained, make a UF reference from the equivalent free-floating subdivision form following the procedures in H 195 if the reference does not yet exist. Otherwise, submit a proposal to change the variant form along with all bibliographic records requiring correction following the procedures in H 193.

### E. Period subdivisions

The listed period subdivisions evolved from English literature practice and should be used only when appropriate for other literatures. If they are inappropriate for specific literatures, special periods should be established where needed under individual literatures and genres.

### E.1. Limitations for period subdivisions

Period subdivisions may be followed by topical and form subdivisions from sections G and H, with the noted exceptions, but may not be combined with author group subdivisions from sec. F, nor used after headings for literatures or literary genres with geographic subdivision.

Period subdivisions are used only after headings for individual literatures formulated as *[ . . . ] literature* and after major literary genres, that is, headings in the form *[ . . . ] fiction*, *[ . . . ] drama*, *[ . . . ] poetry*, *[ . . . ] essays*, or *[ . . . ] prose literature*.

Period subdivisions may not free-float under headings modified by parenthetical qualifiers for language, for example, *Nigerian fiction (English)*. However, period subdivisions may be individually established under headings for European literatures that are modified by parenthetical qualifiers for language.

Examples:

```
Belgian literature (French)--20th century
Swiss poetry (German)--Early modern, 1500-1700
```

### F. Author group subdivisions

### F.1. General rule

Use the following free-floating subdivisions under any literature or major genre of a literature for author groups that identify subordinate bodies of that literature. Any subdivision that also designates the literature is not valid under that literature, for example, use *English literature--Celtic authors*, but not "Celtic literature--Celtic authors". Author group subdivisions may be followed by any topical or form subdivision from sections G and H, but may not be combined in a single heading with period subdivisions from sec. E nor used after headings for literary genres with geographic subdivisions.

Following this pattern, establish new subdivisions for internal **national**, **ethnic**, or **religious** groups **only**. For other author groups, follow the pattern of the phrase headings, *Children’s writings, English--[place]* or *Prisoners’ writings, French--[place]*. However, for external author groups, that is, those living outside the country normally associated with the literature to which they are contributing, use simple geographic subdivision, for example, *German literature--Romania*.

Subdivisions for religious author groups are used for literature written by authors who are members of the group, whether or not the literature includes religious themes. For example, assign *American poetry--Christian authors* to poetry by American Christians. Literature with religious themes is entered under a phrase heading for the literature, for example, *Christian poetry, American*.

The literatures of the independent nations of Africa, Asia, and the Pacific must be established with the national or regional group as an independent literature (using a parenthetical language qualifier if necessary), for example, *African literature (French)*. Since these are no longer considered subordinate groups within the general body of literature in a language, do not formulate headings such as *French literature--African authors*.

### H. Form subdivisions

### H.1. General rule

The following free-floating subdivisions may also be used under period subdivisions from section E or author group subdivisions from section F with the noted exceptions. They may also be used after headings for literary genres with geographic subdivisions.

LC practice: As stated in H 1075, Library of Congress catalogers will no longer use form subdivisions. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT) to bring out the form(s) and/or genre(s) of the resource being cataloged. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign Library of Congress Demographic Group Terms (LCDGT) to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged. The form subdivisions maintained on this list are not available for use by LC catalogers. Catalogers at institutions external to LC may use them as dictated by their own institutional policies.

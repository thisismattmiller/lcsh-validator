# H 405 Establishing Certain Entities in the Name or Subject Authority File

## Validation rules

1. Every named entity type listed in Group 1 is established according to descriptive cataloging conventions and its authority record always resides in the name authority file; it is not a valid LCSH (subject authority) heading. (H 405 sec. A, F)
2. Every named entity type listed in Group 2 is established according to subject cataloging conventions; its record resides in the subject authority file if used only as a subject heading, or in the name authority file if needed as a descriptive access point. (H 405 sec. A, G)
3. To determine the group of a new named entity, find the precise category, a broader category encompassing it, or a very closely analogous category in the Group 1 or Group 2 table. (H 405 sec. C)
4. For a Group 1 entity, use the RDA name heading if one has been established; otherwise establish it as a name heading. A subject authority record for a Group 1 entity should be deleted (H 193) once the name heading exists. (H 405 sec. D)
5. For a Group 2 entity needed for subject cataloging with no name authority record, establish it as a subject heading (H 200 and category-specific sheets such as H 1334 for buildings and structures, H 1925 for parks). (H 405 sec. E.1.a)
6. An RDA or AACR2 name authority record for a Group 2 entity without a 667 field "Subj Headings Manual/RDA" is referred to PTCP to decide whether to cancel it and re-establish it as a subject heading. (H 405 sec. E.1.b)
7. A pre-AACR2 name authority record for a Group 2 entity is invalid; establish the entity as a subject heading and have the name authority record deleted. (H 405 sec. E.1.c)
8. Since 1995 the former Group 3 categories (cemeteries, city sections, concentration camps, country clubs) are established only in the name authority file. (H 405 sec. A)
9. Airports, arboretums, botanical gardens, cemeteries, concentration camps, country clubs, herbariums, and zoological gardens are tagged X10 (not X51). (H 405 sec. A)
10. Events that are formally convened, directed toward a common goal, capable of being reconvened, and that have a formal name are name headings tagged 111 (Group 1); events that generally cannot be repeated and have no formal name but are referred to by generic terms only are subject headings tagged 150 (Group 2). See H 1592. (H 405 sec. F, G)
11. Named cemeteries as corporate bodies are Group 1, tagged 110; cemeteries that are archaeological sites include the term `Site` in the heading and are Group 2, tagged 151, qualified according to subject cataloging conventions. (H 405 sec. F, G)
12. Free-standing chapels are Group 1, tagged 110; chapels that are building details or parts of complex structures are Group 2, tagged 150 (see H 1334 sec. 5-6). (H 405 sec. F, G)
13. Forests, parks, and reserves as administrative agencies are Group 1, tagged 110, and always qualified by `(Agency)`; as geographic entities they are Group 2, tagged 151, and qualified according to subject cataloging conventions. (H 405 sec. F, G)
14. Individual fictitious characters, legendary characters, gods, and mythological figures are Group 1, tagged 100. Groups are Group 2 subject headings tagged 150 and qualified, respectively, by `(Fictitious characters)`, `(Legendary characters)`, `(… deities)`, and `(… mythology)`. (H 405 sec. F, G)
15. Military expeditions that are not campaigns, battles, sieges, etc., are Group 1, tagged 111; military expeditions that are campaigns, battles, sieges, etc., are Group 2 subject headings tagged 150. (H 405 sec. F, G)
16. Individual video games are Group 1, established as titles (130); video game franchises are Group 2 topical subject headings (150). (H 405 sec. F, G)
17. Comic strips and computer programs/software are generally tagged 130 (title), but tagged 100 or 110 when established under a personal or corporate name. (H 405 sec. F)
18. City sections (including historic districts and special economic zones in cities) are Group 1, tagged 151; historic sites including historic districts not in cities are Group 2, tagged 151. (H 405 sec. F, G)
19. Military installations that are active, and all those after 1899, are Group 1 (151); inactive pre-1900 military installations are Group 2 (151). (H 405 sec. F, G)
20. Temples in use are Group 1 (110); temples in ruins are Group 2 (110). (H 405 sec. F, G)
21. Tribes as legal entities (U.S. and Canada only) are Group 1, tagged 151; tribes as ethnic groups are Group 2, tagged 150. (H 405 sec. F, G)
22. Research parks (land devoted to institutions carrying out academic or industrial research) are Group 1 (110); geographic headings for parks intended for recreation, conservation, etc., are Group 2. (H 405 sec. F)
23. Individual amusement park rides are tagged 110; themed amusement park rides existing in multiple amusement parks are tagged 150. (H 405 sec. G)
24. Artists', composers', etc., groups that are informally organized, may not be self-named, and do not collectively promote and market their work are subject headings tagged 150; such groups self-identified by a name and functioning as a business firm are corporate names established by descriptive conventions. (H 405 sec. G)
25. Building details are generally tagged 150, but those belonging to another category on the list may be tagged 110 (e.g., Auditoriums). (H 405 sec. G)
26. Buildings occupied by corporate bodies and known by names different from those bodies are Group 2, tagged 110; for buildings known by the same name as the occupying body see H 1334. (H 405 sec. G)
27. Walls built as military installations (e.g., the Great Wall of China, Hadrian's Wall, individual city walls) are tagged 151; all other walls are tagged 150. (H 405 sec. G)
28. Use the MARC tag given for each entity type in the tables below (sec. F and G) when judging the expected tagging of a heading of that type. (H 405 sec. F, G)

## Full text

### A. Background

Most headings fall into clearly defined categories and are established either as personal names, corporate bodies, jurisdictions, uniform titles, named meetings, etc., in the name authority file, or as topical headings, named objects such as automobiles, geographic features, etc., in the subject authority file. There are, however, certain named entities that have in the past been problematic as to

- whether the heading should go into the name authority file following descriptive cataloging conventions or the subject authority file following subject cataloging conventions; and
- how the heading should be tagged in both authority and bibliographic records.

To eliminate this confusion and to standardize the formulation and tagging of headings for such entities, the former Office for Descriptive Cataloging Policy (Desc Pol) and the Office for Subject Cataloging Policy (Subj Pol), working under the aegis of the Office of the Director for Cataloging, developed guidelines with respect to

- the responsibility for establishing the headings;
- the conventions to be used in formulating the headings;
- the tags to be used for content designation; and
- the file (name or subject authority) in which authority records for the headings should reside.

The guidelines originally developed by Desc Pol, Subj Pol, and the Director for Cataloging divided the problematic headings into three groups:

1. Headings established according to descriptive cataloging conventions with authority records that always resided in the name authority file;
2. Headings established according to subject cataloging conventions with authority records that resided in the subject authority file unless they were needed for use as descriptive access points; and
3. Headings established in either the name or subject authority file depending upon need for their use as either descriptive access points or as subject headings.

In 1994, the Cooperative Cataloging Council (CCC) established the CPSO/CCC Task Group on Issues Surrounding Maintenance of Separate Name and Subject Authority Files. This task group agreed to reduce the "logical" inconsistencies between the two files so that they could be more easily used together. To this end the task group recommended, and the CCC approved, the deletion from the subject authority file of duplicate records for name headings that had been included in the subject authority file in order to facilitate the production of various products, such as Library of Congress Subject Headings.

Duplicate records were deleted from LCSH to the extent practical. A limited number of records were retained in order to preserve scope notes that provide instructions on assignment of the heading as a subject and/or to preserve general see also references. In addition, some records were retained in order to provide UFs for synonymous words or phrases, when those references would not be appropriate for the Name Authority File. Examples of records retained for these reasons include *Catholic Church*; *Qur'an*; and *Bible. English*.

In 1995, the Program for Cooperative Cataloging (PCC) (successor to the CCC) created a follow-on task group, PCC Task Group on Name Versus Subject Authorities, that considered those categories of headings characterized as Group 3 that could be established in either the name or subject authority file (cemeteries, city sections, concentration camps, and country clubs), headings for events, and tagging conventions for certain "geographic" entities. The task group recommended that the headings that were previously established in either the name or subject authority file be established only in the name authority file; that headings for events that are formally convened, directed toward a common goal, capable of being reconvened, and that have a formal name be established in the name authority file instead of the subject authority file; and that certain entities that were formerly tagged as X51 be tagged as X10, including airports, arboretums, botanical gardens, cemeteries, concentration camps, country clubs, herbariums, and zoological gardens. The PCC approved the task group's recommendations, and the results are reflected in the lists below.

The revised guidelines divide the problematic headings into two groups:

1. Headings always established according to descriptive cataloging conventions with authority records that always reside in the name authority file.
2. Headings always established according to subject cataloging conventions with authority records that
   - reside in the subject authority file if used only as subject headings; or
   - reside in the name authority file if the headings are needed for use as descriptive access points.

Lists of the types of headings falling into both these groups are on pages 4-17. This instruction sheet describes the procedures to be followed when it is necessary for a subject cataloger to establish a heading in one of these categories, or to request a descriptive cataloger to establish a heading for subject usage.

### C. General rule

Whenever a new heading is needed for a named entity, consult the two lists on pages 4-17 to determine whether the heading is categorized as a Group 1 or Group 2 heading. Follow the procedures appropriate to the group. If neither the precise category, nor a broader category that encompasses the precise category, nor a very closely analogous category is found in either of the two groups, bring the matter to the attention of the Policy, Training, and Cooperative Programs Division (PTCP) for an interpretation and possible addition of the category to one of the lists.

### D. Group 1 instructions

First search to determine whether the required heading has already been established as an RDA name heading. If so, use the heading as established. If not, establish it as a name heading or request a descriptive cataloger to do so.

If a heading in this category has been established as a subject heading, submit a proposal to delete the subject authority record according to the procedures in H 193 after it has been established as a name heading.

### E. Group 2 instructions

### E.1. Heading required for subject cataloging purposes

First search to determine whether the entity has already been established as a name heading according to former guidelines. After making this determination, proceed as follows:

### E.1.a. Name authority record not found

Submit a proposal to establish the entity as a subject heading, following the standard procedures described in H 200, as well as any special procedures described in individual instruction sheets appropriate to particular categories of named entities, for example, H 1334 for buildings and structures, H 1925 for parks, etc.

Proposals of this type appear in the main alphabetical section of the tentative list and are treated as subject headings.

### E.1.b. RDA or AACR2 name authority record found

If the record does not have a 667 field with the notation "Subj Headings Manual/RDA," consult the PTCP Division to determine whether it should be canceled as a name heading and re-established as a subject heading.

### E.1.c. Pre-AACR2 name authority record found

Submit a proposal to establish it as a subject heading, as described in sec. E.1.a, above. Notify the PTCP Division of the invalid name heading so that the name authority record can be deleted.

### E.2. Heading needed for use as descriptive access point

Follow the guidelines in the Descriptive Cataloging Manual, Z1, Appendix 1: Ambiguous Entities, sec. 1.2 and sec. 3.1.

# H 1095 Free-floating Subdivisions

## Validation rules

1. A free-floating subdivision is a form or topical subdivision that may be used under designated subjects without the usage being established editorially and without an authority record being created for each main heading/subdivision combination; a heading string built with one is valid only if the subdivision is used under a category of heading that its usage statement designates. (H 1095 sec. A)
2. Free-floating status does not allow assigning a subdivision indiscriminately under any heading; most free-floating subdivisions are usable only under limited categories of headings in specifically defined situations, and every use must accord with common sense and subject cataloging rules, policies, and practices. (H 1095 sec. C)
3. There are five types of free-floating subdivisions, each governed by its own list: (1) form and topical subdivisions of general application — H 1095 Sections F and G; (2) subdivisions under classes of persons — H 1100, and under ethnic groups — H 1103; (3) subdivisions under names of corporate bodies — H 1105, names of persons — H 1110, family names — H 1120; (4) subdivisions under place names (regions, countries, cities, etc.) — H 1140, and under bodies of water — H 1145.5; (5) subdivisions controlled by pattern headings — H 1147–H 1200, with general instructions in H 1146. To validate a subdivision, check the list(s) that govern the category of the main heading. (H 1095 sec. D)
4. Free-floating terms and phrases added to existing headings (not as subdivisions) are governed by H 362, not by the subdivision lists. (H 1095 sec. D)
5. Determine authorized usage from the SHM lists (H 1095–H 1200) and individual instruction sheets, from subdivision authority records (18X fields, 073 codes naming the controlling sheet, 680/667 usage statements), scope notes (680), general see also references (360) and general see references (260), and from LCSH Tentative Lists/Summary of Decisions. Do not rely on the old *Free-floating subdivisions: An Alphabetical Index* (1989–2011), which is out of date. (H 1095 sec. E.1)
6. A free-floating subdivision must not be used when the combination conflicts with a previously established heading; the most common conflict is subdivision vs. phrase heading — e.g. use the established heading *School management and organization*, not *Schools--Management*. Use the established heading unless a policy exists to revise that type of heading. (H 1095 sec. E.2)
7. Headings of the type *[X] as a profession* were cancelled in the 1980s in favor of the free-floating subdivision *--Vocational guidance*; a string *[X] as a profession* should be *[X]--Vocational guidance*. (H 1095 sec. E.2)
8. For subdivisions controlled by pattern headings, usage is generally brought into conformity with the pattern lists unless there is a good reason to maintain an exceptional heading. (H 1095 sec. E.2)
9. Use a free-floating subdivision under a topical heading only when the topic of the subdivision is not already implied in the heading itself. (H 1095 sec. E.3)
10. Do not use *--Government policy* under headings that are inherently governmental in nature (cf. H 1642). (H 1095 sec. E.3)
11. Do not use *--Psychological aspects* under headings for psychological concepts, such as *Emotions*. (H 1095 sec. E.3)
12. Combine subdivisions with main headings in the prescribed order; the two basic orders of subject heading strings are given in H 1075 sec. E. (H 1095 sec. E.4)
13. A free-floating subdivision may be followed by a geographic subdivision only if the list marks it (May Subd Geog) ("Yes" in the May Subd Geog column; `may_subd_geog: true` in `rules/lists/H1095.json`). A subdivision not so marked must not be followed by a geographic subdivision. Further-subdivision-by-place rules are in H 860; interposition of geographic subdivisions is in H 870. (H 1095 sec. E.5)
14. Where a list entry restricts geographic subdivision (e.g. "Do not subdivide geographically under names of individual space vehicles" for *--Accidents*; "This subdivision may be further subdivided by place only under topical headings" for *--Awards*), a geographic subdivision after that subdivision is invalid under the excluded categories of headings even though the subdivision is marked (May Subd Geog). (H 1095 sec. F, G; see the `notes` field of each entry)
15. Each subdivision on the lists carries subfield coding: topical subdivisions are coded `$x`, form subdivisions `$v`, geographic `$z` (e.g. `$z Foreign countries`). Multi-element subdivisions keep the coding of each element, e.g. `$x Accidents $x Investigation`, `$x Abbreviations $v Dictionaries`. (H 1095 sec. E.6, F, G)
16. Most form subdivisions coded `$v` may also be used as topical subdivisions coded `$x` when assigned to works about the form (see H 1075). A form-subdivision term coded `$x` is valid only in that "works about the form" sense. (H 1095 sec. E.6)
17. When a subdivision appears on Section F as a topical subdivision and on Section G as a form subdivision (e.g. *--Abstracts*, *--Aerial photographs*, *--Congresses*, *--Video games*), each coding has its own usage statement; validate `$x` against the Section F entry and `$v` against the Section G entry. (H 1095 sec. F, G)
18. Using a free-floating subdivision in a new situation, or one not authorized in the written guidelines or the free-floating/pattern lists, requires editorial establishment through the normal proposal procedure (H 200); such an unestablished usage is not free-floating. (H 1095 sec. E.8)
19. A usage statement reading "Use under subjects" means the subdivision may be used under all types of headings — geographic, personal, corporate, title, and topical. (H 1095 sec. F)
20. A subdivision authorized "under topical headings" may be used under appropriate headings tagged 150 and also under headings with other tags that are divided by topical subdivisions. (H 1095 sec. F)
21. For subdivisions of general application that also appear on other special lists (H 1100–H 1200), all uses are enumerated on the H 1095 list; a subdivision whose application is governed solely by another list is not on H 1095 and must be validated against that list. (H 1095 sec. F)
22. Per-subdivision usage — which categories of headings a subdivision may follow, whether it may be subdivided geographically, related "See also" subdivisions, and the instruction sheets to consult — is given entry by entry in Sections F and G and in `rules/lists/H1095.json` (336 topical/geographic entries in F, 156 form entries in G). Prohibitions stated in an entry (e.g. *--Economic aspects*: "Do not use under topics that are inherently economic"; *--Health aspects*, *--Mythology*, *--Philosophy*, *--Social aspects* likewise) make the string invalid. (H 1095 sec. F, G)
23. *--Autonomous communities* (Spain), *--Autonomous regions* (China), *--Union territories* (India), *--Voivodeships* (Poland), and *--Cantons*, *--Governorates*, *--Provinces*, *--Regions*, *--Republics*, *--States*, *--Territories and possessions* are used after a geographic subdivision for a country, in headings of the type *[topic]--[country]*, for works discussing collectively those first-order divisions of the country in relation to the topic, e.g. *Law--Spain--Autonomous communities*, *Minorities--China--Autonomous regions*, *Transportation--Switzerland--Cantons*, *Income tax--United States--States*; the ones naming a country are valid only after that country, and *--Regions* only after countries whose legally designated first order divisions are called regions (consult H 713). *--Territories and possessions* may also be used directly under names of regions and countries. (H 1095 sec. F)
24. [LC practice] The form subdivisions on these lists (Section G, and form subdivisions referred to in Section F) remain valid LCSH and may be used by catalogers at institutions outside LC as their own policies dictate, but since the 2025/early-2026 phase-out Library of Congress catalogers do not assign them; LC assigns Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT) for the form(s)/genre(s) of the resource instead. A `$v` form subdivision in an LC-created record after the phase-out is contrary to LC practice, not invalid LCSH. (H 1095 sec. F, G; sec. E.6)

## Full text

### A. Definition

The term “free-floating” refers to a form or topical subdivision that may be used under designated subjects without the usage being established editorially, and, as a consequence, without an authority record being created for each main heading/subdivision combination that might be needed.

### B. Recent revisions

2026-01: Updated template. Simplified text. Added instructions related to LCGFT implementation.

### C. Caution regarding use

Free-floating status does not allow the indiscriminate assignment of a subdivision under any topic of interest. All free-floating subdivisions may be assigned only in accordance with common sense and subject cataloging rules, policies, and practices. Most subdivisions are usable only under limited categories of headings in specifically defined situations.

### D. Types of free-floating subdivisions

Five types of free-floating subdivisions are in use:

1. Form and topical subdivisions of general application. These subdivisions are listed with guidelines for their use in Section F of this instruction sheet.
2. Subdivisions used under classes of persons and ethnic groups. These subdivisions are given in two separate lists: H 1100 and H 1103.
3. Subdivisions used under names of individual corporate bodies, persons, and families. Subdivisions used under names of corporate bodies are listed in H 1105. Subdivisions used under names of persons are listed in H 1110. Subdivisions used under family names are included in H 1120.
4. Subdivisions used under place names. Subdivisions used under names of regions, countries, cities, etc., are listed in H 1140. Subdivisions used under names of bodies of water are listed in H 1145.5.
5. Subdivisions controlled by pattern headings. Separate lists of these subdivisions are given in the appropriate instruction sheet for each pattern heading (H 1147-H 1200). General instructions for their use may be found in H 1146.

It is also possible to add certain free-floating terms and phrases to existing headings. For instructions on those terms and phrases, see H 362.

### E. Procedures for assigning free-floating subdivisions

### E.1. Authorized usage

Before using any free-floating subdivision, determine that it is being used in accordance with subject cataloging policy, as stated in the Subject Headings Manual, the subject authority file, etc. Information about the authorized usage of free-floating subdivisions may be found in:

- The Subject Headings Manual -- the lists of free-floating subdivisions in H 1095-H 1200, as well as in separate instruction sheets that explain the use of many individual subdivisions.
- Subject authority records -- subdivision authority records in which free-floating subdivisions appear in 18X fields, codes identifying their controlling instruction sheet numbers (H 1095-H 1200) appear in 073 fields, and usage statements and instructions appear in 680 and 667 fields; scope notes in 680 fields of subject heading authority records (cf. H 400); general see also references in 360 fields of authority records for generic headings (cf. H 371); and general see references in 260 fields of reference records (cf. H 374) when no generic headings exist.
- LC Subject Headings Tentative List, Summary of Decisions -- announcements of new free-floating subdivisions and changes to existing ones.

Note: Free-floating subdivisions: An Alphabetical Index was published annually from 1989-2011. Because existing copies are out of date, they are not reliable guides to instructions on currently authorized free-floating subdivisions.

### E.2. Conflicts

Determine if the use under consideration conflicts with any previously established subject headings. The conflict most frequently encountered is the subdivision vs. phrase heading conflict, for example, *Schools--Management* vs. *School management and organization*. Use the established heading in each case unless a policy is in place to revise a particular type of heading. For example, in the 1980s, a decision was made to cancel all headings of the type *[X] as a profession* in favor of the free-floating subdivision *--Vocational guidance*. For subdivisions controlled by pattern headings, generally bring usage into conformity with the pattern lists unless there is a good reason to maintain an exceptional heading.

### E.3. Topics not implied in headings

Use a free-floating subdivision under a topical heading only when the topic of the subdivision is not already implied in the heading itself. For example, the subdivision *--Government policy* is not used under headings that are inherently governmental in nature (cf. H 1642). The subdivision *--Psychological aspects* should not be used under headings for psychological concepts, such as *Emotions*.

### E.4. Order of subdivisions

Combine subdivisions with main headings in the prescribed order to create meaningful subject heading strings. For a discussion of the two basic orders of subject heading strings, including examples that use Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms in place of form subdivisions, see H 1075 Section E.

### E.5. Geographic subdivisions

Free-floating subdivisions that are authorized for further geographic subdivision have the notation (May Subd Geog) on the lists of free-floating subdivisions. See H 860 for instructions on subdivisions further subdivided by place and H 870 for instructions on interposition of geographic subdivisions.

### E.6. Subfield coding

On the individual free-floating subdivision and pattern heading lists, subfield coding is provided for each subdivision listed. Most form subdivisions coded $v on the lists may also be used as topical subdivisions coded $x when assigned to works about the form (see H 1075, sec. 1.d.). (See LC practice note regarding form subdivisions in Section F.)

Note: As instruction sheets are updated to the current template, the lists of form and topical subdivisions will be separated.

### E.7. Updating of LC records

When assigning a particular subdivision for the first time under a particular heading, check the existing entries in the LC Database under the unsubdivided heading in order to locate those records with headings that require updating.

Example:

```
When assigning the subdivision *--Social aspects* (May Subd Geog) for
the first time under the heading *Hunting*, examine the other records
that have been assigned the heading *Hunting* to determine those
records, if any, that should receive this new subdivision (with
geographic subdivision, if appropriate).
```

### E.8. New usage

To use a free-floating subdivision in a new situation, or in a situation not authorized in the written guidelines here or in the individual free-floating or pattern heading lists, establish the new usage editorially according to the normal procedures in H 200, submitting a note of explanation at the same time.

### F. Topical Subdivisions of general application

Listed below are general free-floating form subdivisions and those topical subdivisions having general application, or an application not covered by the pattern and other lists of free-floating subdivisions outlined on p. 1. For subdivisions having applications governed solely by the other lists, consult the appropriate list(s). On this list, statements are given of the types of headings under which each subdivision may be used along with definitions of use in some cases. For subdivisions that may be used under all types of headings -- geographic, personal, corporate, title, and topical -- the statement reads, “Use under subjects.” Subdivisions authorized for use under topical headings may be used under appropriate headings tagged 150 as well as under headings with other tags that are divided by topical subdivisions. For subdivisions of general application that also appear on other special lists, all uses are enumerated here. See also references are provided between related subdivisions on this list. See also references to closely related subdivisions on other special lists are also given. Appearing with each subdivision are references to instruction sheets in the Subject Headings Manual which explain or mention its use.

LC practice: In 2025, the use of form subdivisions was phased out. Library of Congress catalogers assign Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT) to bring out the form(s) and/or genre(s) of the resource being cataloged. Therefore, the form subdivisions maintained on this list are not available for use by Library of Congress catalogers. Catalogers at institutions external to LC may use them as dictated by their own institutional policies.

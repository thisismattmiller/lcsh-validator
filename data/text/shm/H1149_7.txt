B. Types of headings covered by the pattern 
Headings for individual genres and forms of comics and graphic novels, including the general 
heading *Comic books, strips, etc.* Restrictions are noted in each section. Examples: 
*Biographical comic books, strips, etc.*; *Graphic novels*; *Manga (Comic books)*; 
*Romance comic books, strips, etc.* 
B.1. General rule 
Comics and graphic novels are considered to be a form that includes both the written word and 
visual art. Therefore, the free-floating subdivisions in this instruction sheet are drawn from 
pattern headings for art (H 1148) and literature (H 1156). 
Period subdivisions are listed first, author groups second, and topical and form subdivisions 
last, which is the proper order for considering these elements when formulating subject 
headings about particular themes in comics and graphic novels. 
B.2. Geographic subdivision 
Headings for comics and graphic novels — including the heading *Comic books, strips, etc.* — 
may be subdivided geographically. Headings with geographic subdivisions may be further 
subdivided by topical and form subdivisions from section F and G below. Period and author 
group subdivisions from section D and section E below may not be combined in a single heading 
with geographic subdivisions. Instead, assign additional headings to bring out those aspects. 
Examples: 
650 #0 $a Detective and mystery comic books, strips, etc. $z Brazil. 
[Base heading] + [Geographic subdivision] 
 
650 #0 $a Horror comic books, strips, etc. $z Japan $x Technique. 
[Base heading] + [Geographic subdivision] + [Topical or form subdivision] 
 
[not 650 #0 $a Comic books, strips, etc. $z Argentina $y 21st century.] 
 
[not 650 #0 $a Graphic novels $z Spain $x Women authors.] 
C. Conflicts 
Any subdivision listed here can be used as a free-floating subdivision under any heading 
belonging to the category if it is appropriate and no conflict exists in the subject authority file. 
Subject authority records may exist for headings employing variant phrases or subdivisions 
equivalent to subdivisions on this list.

D. LC practice 
If an exceptional variant form is to be retained, make a “Used for” reference from the 
equivalent free-floating subdivision form following the procedures in H 195 if the reference 
does not yet exist. Otherwise, submit a proposal to change the variant form along with all 
bibliographic records requiring correction following procedures in H 193. 
E. Period subdivisions 
E.1 General rule 
Use the following free-floating general period subdivisions derived from H 1148, section 1. 
Period subdivisions may be followed by topical and form subdivisions from section G and H 
below, with the noted exceptions, but may not be combined with author group subdivisions 
from section F below, nor used after headings for comics or graphic novels with geographic 
subdivision. If there are specific headings where these subdivisions are not appropriate, 
establish the appropriate subdivision under the specific heading. 
Examples: 
650 #0 $a Queer comic books, strips, etc. $y 20th century. 
[Base heading] + [Period subdivision] 
 
650 #0 $a Graphic medicine (Comic books) $y 21st century $x Publishing. 
[Base heading] + [Period subdivision] + [Topical or form subdivision] 
 
[not 650 #0 $a Comic books, strips, etc. $y 21st century $z Argentina.] 
 
[not 650 #0 $a Manga (Comic books) $y 20th century $x Buddhist authors.]

F. Author group subdivisions 
F.1. General rule 
Use the free-floating author group subdivisions listed in H 1156, section E, to denote author 
groups that identify subordinate bodies of comic or graphic novel genres. Author group 
subdivisions may be followed by any topical or form subdivision from section G and H below 
but may not be combined in a single heading with period subdivisions from section E above nor 
used after headings with geographic subdivisions. 
Following this pattern, establish new subdivisions for national, ethnic, or religious groups only. 
Establish new subdivisions under the pattern heading for literature and for comics and graphic 
novels. For other author groups, follow the pattern of the phrase headings, *Children’s 
writings, English—[place]* or *Prisoners’ writings, French—[place]*. 
Subdivisions for religious author groups are used for comics and graphic novels written by 
authors who are members of the group, whether or not the comic or graphic novel includes 
religious themes. For example, assign *Comic books, strips, etc.—Christian authors* to comics 
or graphic novels by Christians. Comics and graphic novels with religious themes are entered 
under phrase headings for the genre, for example, *Christian comic books, strips, etc.* 
Examples: 
650 #0 $a Science fiction comic books, strips, etc. $x Black authors. 
[Base heading] + [Author group subdivision] 
 
650 #0 Comic books, strips, etc. $x Jewish authors $x History and criticism. 
[Base heading] + [Author group subdivision] + [Topical or form subdivision] 
 
[not 650 #0 $a Graphic novels $x Women authors $z Spain.] 
 
[not 650 #0 $a Manga (Comic books) $x Buddhist authors $y 20th century.]

G. Topical Subdivisions 
G.1. General rule 
Use the free-floating topical subdivisions listed in H 1148 and in H 1156, section G. These 
subdivisions may also be used under period subdivisions from section E above, or author group 
subdivisions from section F above with the noted exceptions. They may also be used after 
headings with geographic subdivision. 
Examples: 
650 #0 $a Documentary comic books, strips, etc. $x Authorship. 
[Base heading] + [Topical subdivision] 
 
650 #0 $a Fantasy comic books, strips, etc. $x Social aspects $z Germany. 
[The subdivision *—Social aspects* may be subdivided geographically.] 
[Base heading] + [Topical subdivision] + [Geographic subdivision] 
 
[not 650 #0 $a Underground comic books, strips, etc. $x History and 
criticism $z United States.] 
[The subdivision *—History and criticism* may not be subdivided geographically.] 
 
[not 650 #0 $a Biographical comic books, strips, etc. $x History and 
criticism $y 20th century.] 
 
[not 650 #0 $a Romance comic books, strips, etc. $x History and criticism $x 
Women authors.]

H.1. General rule 
Use the free-floating form subdivisions listed in H 1148 and in H 1156, section H. These 
subdivisions may also be used under period subdivisions from section E above, or author group 
subdivisions from section F above with the noted exceptions. They may also be used after 
headings with geographic subdivision.

LC practice: As stated in H 1075, Library of Congress catalogers will no longer use form 
subdivisions. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form 
Terms (LCGFT) to bring out the form(s) and/or genre(s) of the resource being cataloged. To 
replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign Library of Congress 
Demographic Group Terms (LCDGT) to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged. 
The form subdivisions maintained on this list are not available for use by LC catalogers. 
Catalogers at institutions external to LC may use them as dictated by their own institutional 
policies.

A. Background 
The subdivision *--Personal narratives* is a free-floating form subdivision used under names of 
events and wars for collective or individual eyewitness reports and/or autobiographical 
accounts of these events and wars. Prior to 1977, the subdivision was used more widely under 
classes of persons, types of activities, and diseases. Those uses were replaced by the 
subdivision *--Biography* under classes of persons.  
LC practice: As stated in H 1075, LC catalogers will no longer use form subdivisions. Instead 
assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT), e.g. 
*Diaries*. For guidelines on bio-bibliography, see H 1328. For guidelines on assigning headings 
to personal correspondence, see H 1480. For guidelines on biography, see H 1330. For 
guidelines on diaries, see H 1538. For guidelines on interviews, see H 1678. For guidelines on 
genealogy and family histories, see H 1631.

C. Definitions 
Personal narratives: collective or individual eyewitness reports and/or autobiographical 
accounts of wars or disasters. 
D. General rule 
Use the subdivision *--Personal narratives* under names of individual events or wars. 
If the work being cataloged covers a particular event such as a battle which is part of a larger 
conflict, assign the heading for the specific event subdivided by *--Personal narratives*. 
LC practice: Assign the subject headings for the war or event. Do not assign the form 
subdivision *--Personal narratives*. Assign LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or genre of the 
resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Personal narratives* and/or a more specific term, e.g. *Diaries*. 
E. National or ethnic qualifier 
When used under names of wars, battles, etc., qualify the subdivision *--Personal narratives* 
by a national or ethnic qualifier to bring out the nationality or ethnic background of the 
narrator(s) of the account(s), for example, *World War, 1939 1945--Personal narratives, 
American*. Omit the national or ethnic qualifier if it would cause redundancy within the 
heading. 
Under names of events not associated with a war or military conflict, omit the national or 
ethnic qualifier. 
LC practice: Assign the subject headings for the war or event. If location is limited, subdivide 
geographically to bring out the location of the events in the narrative. If necessary, bring out 
the nationality or ethnic background of the narrator(s) through a separate class of persons 
heading designating nationality, ethnicity, etc., if not covered by the geographic subdivision. If 
using that separate class of persons heading, subdivide geographically to bring out the location 
of the events in the personal narrative, unless it would be redundant. If the contents of the 
personal narrative also include descriptions and explanations of past events related to that 
class of persons, also use the subdivisions *--History* as instructed in H 1647. Optionally, also 
assign LCDGT to express the nationality or ethnicity of the creators. Assign LCGFT appropriate 
to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Personal narratives* and/or a 
more specific term, e.g. *Diaries*.

F. Assignment of subject headings 
F.1. Name heading for the person 
For individual personal narratives, assign the name of the author of the work as a subject 
heading. 
F.2. [Class of persons]--Biography 
If an individual personal narrative contains at least 50% autobiographical information, treat it as 
a biography following the provisions of H 1330. If the work is an eyewitness report containing 
less than 50% autobiographical details, do not treat it as a biography. 
F.3. Other subject headings 
Assign other subject headings as appropriate to the work being cataloged.

G. Order of headings 
G.1. Narratives that include personal information about the author 
If an individual personal narrative contains a significant amount of personal information 
regarding the author, assign the name of the author as the first subject heading. 
G.2. Narratives with little personal information about the author 
If the narrative is essentially an eyewitness account of what happened with little personal 
information about the author, assign the name of the war or event as the first subject heading; 
assign the name of the author as an additional subject heading.

A. Background 
This instruction sheet presents guidelines on the assignment of Library of Congress subject 
headings to juvenile materials. These instructions do not apply to the assignment of Children's 
and Young Adults’ Subject Headings (CYAC; formerly called AC headings).  
For guidelines on the assignment of classification numbers to juvenile materials, see F 615 of 
the Library of Congress Classification and Shelflisting Manual (CSM). 
LC practice: As stated in H 1075, LC catalogers will no longer use form subdivisions. Instead 
assign an appropriate term or terms from the Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT), 
e.g. *Fiction*, *Children’s audiobooks*, *Picture books*, etc. To replace a form subdivision 
that includes a demographic group, also assign Library of Congress Demographic Group Terms 
(LCDGT) to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*, *Middle 
school students*, *Teenagers*, etc..

C. Determining whether a work is juvenile 
For the purposes of subject heading assignment, treat topical materials intended primarily for 
children and young people through the age of 12, or the 7th grade, as juvenile. Treat belles 
lettres -- such as fiction, poetry, etc. -- intended primarily for children and young people 
through the age of 12, or the 7th grade, as juvenile. 
If explicit information on age level (e.g., "pre-school through age 12," "ages 8-12," etc.) has 
been provided by the publisher on the dust jacket, cover, front matter, CIP data sheet, etc., use 
this as the basis for determining that the work is intended to be juvenile. If the age level is not 
explicitly stated, determine the intent by a careful examination of the preface, text, series 
statement, etc. In more difficult cases, use such indications as format, publisher, treatment of 
previous works in the same series, etc., as clues to the intended audience of the work. If in 
doubt, assign adult headings and subdivisions. 
D. Topical juvenile materials 
Assign LC subject headings and subdivisions to topical materials for juveniles up through age 12 
or 7th grade. Use the form subdivisions *–Juvenile literature*, *–Juvenile films*, *–Juvenile 
sound recordings*, and *–Juvenile software* as the final element in all subject headings 
assigned to topical juvenile materials unless special form headings or subdivisions have been 
established, for example, *Children's encyclopedias and dictionaries*; *–Dictionaries, 
Juvenile*; *–Encyclopedias, Juvenile*; *–Juvenile humor*. With rare exceptions, if a work is 
assigned any juvenile heading, all headings assigned must reflect the juvenile nature of the 
work. 
LC practice: Do not use the form subdivisions *--Juvenile literature*, *--Dictionaries, Juvenile*, 
etc. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Picture books*, 
*Dictionaries*, etc. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also 
assign LCDGT to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*. 
E. Textbooks 
For guidelines on the assignment of subject headings to textbooks and works about textbooks, 
see H 2187.

F. Juvenile belles lettres 
F.1. Definitions 
Belles lettres: Literature that is an end in itself and is not practical or purely informative. The 
term may refer to poetry, fiction, drama, literary essays, etc. 
F.2. General rule 
Assign juvenile form headings and subdivisions to belles lettres for juveniles up through age 12 
or 7th grade, for example, *Children's stories*; *Nursery rhymes*; *–Juvenile drama*; *–
Juvenile fiction*; *–Juvenile poetry*.  
Where juvenile form headings do not exist and are not deemed appropriate to establish, assign 
adult headings, for example, *Ghost stories*; *Detective and mystery stories*. Do not further 
subdivide these adult form headings by the juvenile form subdivisions *–Juvenile drama*, *–
Juvenile fiction*, or *–Juvenile poetry*. 
LC practice: Do not use the form subdivisions *--Juvenile drama*, *--Juvenile fiction*, and *--
Juvenile poetry*. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Drama*, 
*Fiction*, *Poetry*. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also 
assign LCDGT to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*.  
Example: 
Title: Sardines swim high across the sky and other poems 
650 #0 $a Imaginary creatures $v Juvenile poetry. 
650 #0 $a Children’s poetry, American. 
LC practice: 
650 #0 $a Imaginary creatures. 
650 #0 $a Children’s poetry, American. 
655 #7 $a Poetry. $2 lcgft 
655 #7 $a Picture books. $2 lcgft 
385 ## $a Children $2 lcdgt 
F.3. Exceptions 
Belles lettres for young adults 
Do not assign juvenile form headings or subdivisions to belles lettres for young adults.

The form subdivision *--Juvenile literature* 
Do not use the form subdivision *--Juvenile literature* under form headings for juvenile belles 
lettres. The exceptions to this practice that occurred in the past, for example, *Limericks--
Juvenile literature*; *Riddles--Juvenile literature*, have been removed from the subject 
authority file and should not be used or reestablished. 
Folklore 
Do not treat folklore materials as belles lettres. Do not use juvenile form subdivisions under 
headings for specific folklore genres, such as *Tales*, *Fairy tales*, *Folk literature*, etc., nor 
under the subdivision *--Folklore*. For guidelines on the assignment of subject headings to 
folklore materials, see H 1627. 
G. Polyglot books 
Establish and assign to both individual works and to collections of juvenile fiction, drama, and 
poetry presented in three or more languages headings of the type *Children's stories, 
Polyglot*; *Children's plays, Polyglot*; *Children's poetry, Polyglot*. 
H. Juvenile software 
Assign headings of the type *[topic]--Juvenile software* for materials coded for intellectual 
level a, b, or c in MARC field 008/22 (Target audience). Also assign headings of this type for 
materials coded f (Specialized) if there is evidence that the material is juvenile in nature. For 
guidelines on the assignment of subject headings to software, see H 2070. 
LC practice: Do not use the form subdivision *--Juvenile software*. Instead assign an 
appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Software*. To replace a form subdivision that 
includes a demographic group, also assign LCDGT to bring out the audience of the resource 
being cataloged, e.g. *Children*.  
I. Bibliographies of juvenile materials on specific topics 
Assign headings of the type *[topic]--Juvenile literature--Bibliography*. For lists of juvenile 
audiovisual materials, assign headings of the type *[topic]--Juvenile films--Catalogs* or 
*[topic]--Juvenile sound recordings--Discography*. 
LC practice: Do not use the form subdivisions of the type *--Juvenile films*, *--Bibliography*, 
etc. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Children’s films*, 
*Bibliographies*, etc. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also 
assign LCDGT to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*.

B. Types of headings covered

nationality, region, ethnic group, or time period.

C. Types of headings not covered 
The category does not include the heading *Art*, headings qualified by religion, nor headings 
for the decorative arts, performing arts, or architecture. The subdivisions in this list may be 
used for these categories if allowed by other instructions sheets or established editorially as 
appropriate to the topic. 
Examples: 
Buddhist art 
Pottery 
Theater 
Architecture, Modern 
D. Conflicts 
Any subdivision listed here can be used as a free-floating subdivision under any heading 
belonging to the category if it is appropriate and no conflict exists in the subject authority file. 
Subject authority records may exist for headings employing variant phrases or subdivisions 
equivalent to subdivisions on this list. 
LC practice: If an exceptional variant form is to be retained, make a UF reference from the 
equivalent free-floating subdivision form following the procedures in H 195 if the reference 
does not yet exist. Otherwise, submit a proposal to change the variant form along with all 
bibliographic records requiring correction following the procedures in H 193. 
E. New subdivisions 
Establish new subdivisions under *Art, Italian* whenever possible. Establish subdivisions 
appropriate only to the art of China, Japan, or Korea under *Art, Chinese*; *Art, Japanese*; or 
*Art, Korean*. 
F. Period subdivisions 
Note: These period subdivisions are not free-floating under unqualified headings for art forms, 
such as *Drawing*, nor under headings for art forms qualified by time period, such as 
*Sculpture, Modern*.

H. List of form subdivisions 
LC practice: In 2026, the use of form subdivisions was phased out. Library of Congress 
catalogers assign Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT) to bring out the form(s) 
and/or genre(s) of the resource being cataloged. Therefore, the form subdivisions maintained 
on this list are not available for use by Library of Congress catalogers. Catalogers at institutions 
external to LC may use them as dictated by their own institutional policies.

B. General rule 
For collections of poetry, assign as many headings as necessary to bring out both the form and 
the topic(s). For single poems, assign headings as required to bring out topic(s), but assign form 
headings only as noted in section E, below.

LC practice: As stated in H 1075, LC catalogers will no longer use form subdivisions. Instead 
assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT), e.g. 
*Poetry*, *Free verse*, etc. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, 
also assign Library of Congress Demographic Group Terms (LCDGT) to bring out the audience of 
the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*. 
C. Designating the form of collections 
C.1. General rule 
Bring out the form of a collection by assigning headings of the type *American poetry--20th 
century*; *College verse*; *Epic poetry*; etc., as in the first heading in the following example 
Example: 
Title: Maine lines; 101 contemporary poems about Maine. 
650 #0 $a American poetry $y 20th century. 
650 #0 $a Maine $x Description and travel $v Poetry. 
LC practice: 
650 #0 $a American poetry $y 20th century. 
650 #0 $a Maine $x Description and travel. 
655 #7 $a Poetry. $2 lcgft 
Certain phrase headings combine both form and topical aspects into one heading, for example, 
*War poetry, English*. These headings are used to designate both the form and the topic of 
collections, and no additional heading is usually required. 
Do not use a phrase heading of this type if a more specific *[topic]--Poetry* heading can be 
formulated to designate the topic of the collection. Instead, assign the more specific heading in 
conjunction with a broader, non-topical, form heading.  
LC practice: Do not use the form subdivision *--Poetry*. Instead, assign an appropriate term or 
terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Poetry* or *Free verse*. 
For example, assign the following headings to a collection of English poetry about the Atlantic 
Ocean: 
Example: 
650 #0 $a English poetry. 
650 #0 $a Atlantic Ocean $v Poetry.

C.2. Collections of poems by one author 
As a general rule, assign form headings  
a. if the form heading includes a topical aspect, for example, *Sea poetry, English*; *Love 
poetry, Cuban*; *War poetry, French*, or  
b. if it is readily apparent from a cursory examination of the work being cataloged that it 
comprises poems of a highly specific form and that this form is an essential point of the 
collection, for example, *Sonnets, American*; *Concrete poetry, German*; etc. 
Do not assign nonspecific form headings to collections of poems by one author, for example, 
*American poetry*; *Spanish poetry*. 
Do not attempt to assign a form heading to a collection of poems by one author if the form is 
not stated on the title page or in another prominent location. 
C.3. Collections of children's poetry 
Assign the heading *Children's poetry* or *Children's poetry, American, [English, etc.]* to 
collections of children's poems, whether by one author or several authors, in addition to the 
other required form and topical headings. 
Example: 
Title: Tomie dePaola’s book of poems. 
650 #0 $a Children’s poetry, American. 
650 #0 $a Children’s poetry, English. 
LC practice: 
650 #0 $a Children’s poetry, American. 
650 #0 $a Children’s poetry, English. 
655 #7 $a Poetry. $2 lcgft 
385 ## $a Children $2 lcdgt

D. Designating the topic(s) of collections by one or several authors 
Bring out identifiable topics, using one of the following types of headings, using one of the 
following types of headings listed in sections D.1-D.2, below. 
Do not attempt to discern topics which have not been made explicit by the author or publisher, 
or which could be interpreted as representing value judgments. 
D.1. [topic]--Poetry or [topic]--Juvenile poetry. 
Use headings of this type as the standard means of designating topics in poetry, for example, 
*Horses--Poetry*; * Washington, George, 1732-1799--Juvenile poetry*; *Callahan family--
Poetry*. 
LC practice: Do not use a form subdivision of the type *--Poetry* or *--Juvenile poetry*. 
Instead, assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Poetry*, *Free verse*, etc. To 
replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign LCDGT to bring out 
the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*, *Teenagers*, etc. 
Example: 
Title: I could pee on this : and other poems by cats. 
650 #0 $a Cats $v Poetry. 
LC practice: 
650 #0 $a Cats. 
655 #7 $a Poetry. $2 lcgft 
Example: 
Title: A universe of rainbows : multicolored poems for a multicolored 
world. 
650 #0 $a Children’s poetry, American.  
650 #0 $a Rainbows $v Juvenile poetry. 
650 #0 $a Colors $v Juvenile poetry. 
LC practice: 
650 #0 $a Children’s poetry, American.  
650 #0 $a Rainbows. 
650 #0 $a Colors.

D.2. Phrase headings with topical aspects 
If a phrase heading has both form and topical aspects, assign only the one heading to designate 
both form and topic, as described in section C.1, above. *Historical poetry*, *Love poetry*, 
and *Political poetry* are examples of such headings. 
Example: 
Title: Hour of freedom : American history in poetry. 
650 #0 $a Historical poetry, American. 
651 #0 $a United States $x History $v Poetry. 
LC practice: 
650 #0 $a Historical poetry, American. 
651 #0 $a United States $x History. 
655 #7 $a Historical poetry. $2 lcgft 
E. Individual poems 
Bring out the topic of an individual poem by assigning headings of the type described in section 
D, above. Assign a form heading only if it includes a topical aspect, or if the poem is of a highly 
specific form, for example, *War poetry, American*, *Humorous poetry, Chinese*; *Nursery 
rhymes, Spanish*. 
Assign the heading *Children’s poetry, American, [English, etc.]* to individual children’s poems 
in addition to the required topical heading(s). 
Do not assign topical headings to single poems which deal with vague and general topics, such 
as fate, mankind, belief, malaise, etc. 
LC practice: Do not use a form subdivision of the type *--Poetry* or *--Juvenile poetry*. 
Instead, assign an appropriate term or terms from LCGFT, e.g. *Poetry*, *Free verse*, etc. To 
replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign LCDGT to bring out 
the audience of the resource being cataloged, e.g. *Children*, *Teenagers*, etc. 
Example: 
Title: And, too, the fox. 
650 #0 $a Children’s poetry, American.  
650 #0 $a Foxes $v Juvenile poetry. 
LC practice: 
650 #0 $a Children’s poetry, American.  
650 #0 $a Foxes.

F. History and criticism 
F.1. General rule 
For works about particular themes in poetry, assign as many headings as necessary to bring out 
the form (with subdivision *--History and criticism*) and the topics (normally using headings of 
the type *[topic] in literature*). 
Examples: 
650 #0 $a English poetry $y 20th century $x History and criticism. 
650 #0 $a Mysticism in literature. 
If a heading has both form and topical aspects, assign only the single heading with the 
subdivision *--History and criticism*, for example, *Sea poetry--History and criticism*. 
F.2. Poetry about individual persons or families 
For criticism of poetry about particular individuals or families, including dynasties and royal 
houses, assign the name of the person or family with the free-floating subdivision *--In 
literature*. 
Examples: 
600 10 $a Columbus, Christopher $x In literature. 
600 30 $a Lincoln family $x In literature. 
F.3. Poetry about individual corporate bodies, places, and sacred works 
For criticism of poetry about particular corporate bodies, places, or sacred works, assign the 
name of the entity with the free-floating subdivision *--In literature*. 
Examples: 
610 20 $a Jesuits $x In literature. 
651 #0 $a Chesapeake Bay (Md. and Va.) $x In literature. 
630 00 $a Bible $x In literature. 
F.4. Poetry about wars and similar events 
For works about the poetry of a specific war, revolution, uprising, etc., assign *[name of event]-
-Literature and the war, [revolution, uprising, etc.]* as a topical heading (cf. H 1200).

Examples: 
651 #0 $a Spain $x History $y Civil War, 1936-1939 $x Literature and the 
war. 
650 #0 $a World War, 1939-1945 $x Literature and the war. 
F.5. Single poems 
For works about a single poem with a special theme, assign a heading for the poem as well as a 
heading for the theme. 
Example: 
600 10 $a Smith, John. $t Scenes of Ireland. 
651 #0 $a Ireland $x In literature.

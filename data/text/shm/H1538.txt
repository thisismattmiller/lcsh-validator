A. Background 
Diaries are registers or records of personal experiences, observations, thoughts, or feelings, 
kept daily or at frequent intervals. They are usually written as an aid to memory or reflection 
and are customarily intended for private rather than public use. The procedures below describe 
the specific treatment of works consisting of true personal diaries. This instruction sheet is not 
intended to cover memoirs, reminiscences, or personal narratives of events that are not in diary 
entry form and contemporaneous with the events described. It is also not intended to be 
applied to official logs of activities. 
LC practice: As stated in H 1075, LC catalogers will no longer use form subdivisions. Instead 
assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT), e.g. 
*Diaries*. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign Library 
of Congress Demographic Groups Terms (LCDGT) to bring out the audience of the resource 
being cataloged, e.g. *German speakers* or *Arabic, Students of*. 
For guidelines on bio-bibliography, see H 1328. For guidelines on biography, see H 1530. For 
guidelines on interviews, see H 1678. For guidelines on personal narratives of wars or disasters, 
see H 1928. For guidelines on genealogy and family histories, see H 1631.

D. Array of headings 
Assign the following array of headings to individual diaries or collections of diaries. 
LC practice: Assign an appropriate combination of subject heading(s) for an individual diary or 
collection of diaries. See the more detailed guidance below regarding each category of subject 
heading. Also assign LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. 
LCGFT *Diaries*. 
Model: 
600 X0 $a [name of diarist(s)] $v Diaries. 
650 #0 $a [class of persons, or ethnic group] $v Diaries. 
650 #0 $a [special topics discussed in the diary or diaries].

E. Individual diarists 
Assign as subject headings the name or names of the individual writers of the diaries, up to a 
maximum of three. Subdivide the name(s) by the free-floating form subdivision *--Diaries*. If 
there are more than three diarists, do not designate any individual writer. 
LC practice: Assign as subject headings the name or names of the individual writers of the 
diaries, up to a maximum of three. Also assign the LCGFT term appropriate to the form and/or 
genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Diaries*. 
F. [Class of persons, or ethnic group]--Diaries 
Assign headings of the type *[class of persons or ethnic group]--Diaries* if the writer(s) of the 
diary or diaries can be identified with a particular subject area or discipline or a particular 
ethnic group.

G. Special topics 
If the diary contains information about topics not covered by headings of the type *[class of 
persons]--Diaries* or *[ethnic group]--Diaries*, assign additional headings for the topics,

including headings such as *[place]--Biography* (cf. H 1330, sec. 1.c.) or *[Individual war or 
event]--Personal narratives* (cf. H 1928), if appropriate. 
Since the diary or diaries of any individual person may or may not be regarded as historical 
source material, depending upon the viewpoint of the reader, do not add the subdivision *--
Sources* or *--History--Sources* to the additional headings assigned for special topics. 
LC practice: If the diary contains information about topics not covered the types of headings 
described in Sections E-F, assign additional headings for the topics, including headings such as 
*[place]* (cf. H 1330, sec. 1.c.) or *[Individual war or event]* (cf. H 1928), if appropriate. Also 
assign the LCGFT term appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT 
*Diaries* or *Personal narratives*. 
H. Diaries as a literary genre 
Headings of the type *American diaries*; *English diaries*; *Japanese diaries*; etc., are 
literary genre headings. Assign such headings only to collections of diaries by more than one 
writer that are classed as literary works. If the collection is not limited to an individual body of 
literature, assign the heading *Diaries*. 
LC practice: Assign an appropriate combination of subject heading(s) for a diary or diaries. If a 
subject heading for literary genre brings out the nationality and/or ethnicity of the writers, 
continue to supply it. Optionally, assign the creator characteristics with LCDGT, e.g. *Germans* 
and/or *Novelists*. If it is describing just the genre/form of the content, do not provide a 
subject heading to express that. Instead, assign LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or genre of 
the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Diaries*.

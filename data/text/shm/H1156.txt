B. Types of headings covered by the pattern 
This instruction sheet contains the free-floating subdivisions that may be used under headings 
for individual literatures and genres of those literatures.  
Examples of these literature headings: 
Swedish literature 
French drama 
German essays 
Epic poetry, Finnish 
Short stories, Chinese 
Appropriate subdivisions may also be used under headings formed using the free-floating 
subdivision *--Literatures* under names of regions and countries, for example, *America--
Literatures*.

Period subdivisions are listed first (section E), author groups second (section F), and topical and 
form subdivisions last (sections G and H), to suggest the proper order for considering these 
elements in the formulation of literature subject headings. Limitations on combining multiple 
subdivisions in a single string appear in the appropriate sections below.  
C. Types of headings not covered by the pattern 
The category does not include the general heading *Literature*, general headings for genres 
without adjectival qualifiers, such as *Drama* or *Poetry*, nor non-belletristic genres such as 
*Scientific literature*. 
D. Conflicts 
D.1. General practice 
Any subdivision listed here can be used as a free-floating subdivision under any heading 
belonging to the category if it is appropriate and no conflict exists in the subject authority file. 
Subject authority records may exist for headings employing variant phrases or subdivisions 
equivalent to subdivisions on this list. 
D.2. LC practice 
If an exceptional variant form is to be retained, make a UF reference from the equivalent free-
floating subdivision form following the procedures in H 195 if the reference does not yet exist. 
Otherwise, submit a proposal to change the variant form along with all bibliographic records 
requiring correction following the procedures in H 193. 
E. Period subdivisions 
The listed period subdivisions evolved from English literature practice and should be used only 
when appropriate for other literatures. If they are inappropriate for specific literatures, special 
periods should be established where needed under individual literatures and genres. 
E.1. Limitations for period subdivisions 
Period subdivisions may be followed by topical and form subdivisions from sections G and H, 
with the noted exceptions, but may not be combined with author group subdivisions from sec. 
F, nor used after headings for literatures or literary genres with geographic subdivision.  
Period subdivisions are used only after headings for individual literatures formulated as *[ . . . ] 
literature* and after major literary genres, that is, headings in the form *[ . . . ] fiction*, *[ . . . ] 
drama*, *[ . . . ] poetry*, *[ . . . ] essays*, or *[ . . . ] prose literature*.

F. Author group subdivisions 
F.1. General rule 
Use the following free-floating subdivisions under any literature or major genre of a literature 
for author groups that identify subordinate bodies of that literature. Any subdivision that also 
designates the literature is not valid under that literature, for example, use *English literature--
Celtic authors*, but not “Celtic literature--Celtic authors”. Author group subdivisions may be 
followed by any topical or form subdivision from sections G and H, but may not be combined in

a single heading with period subdivisions from sec. E nor used after headings for literary genres 
with geographic subdivisions.  
Following this pattern, establish new subdivisions for internal <<national>>, <<ethnic>>, or 
<<religious>> groups <<only>>. For other author groups, follow the pattern of the phrase 
headings, *Children’s writings, English--[place]* or *Prisoners’ writings, French--[place]*. 
However, for external author groups, that is, those living outside the country normally 
associated with the literature to which they are contributing, use simple geographic 
subdivision, for example, *German literature--Romania*. 
Subdivisions for religious author groups are used for literature written by authors who are 
members of the group, whether or not the literature includes religious themes. For example, 
assign *American poetry--Christian authors* to poetry by American Christians. Literature with 
religious themes is entered under a phrase heading for the literature, for example, *Christian 
poetry, American*. 
The literatures of the independent nations of Africa, Asia, and the Pacific must be established 
with the national or regional group as an independent literature (using a parenthetical language 
qualifier if necessary), for example, *African literature (French)*. Since these are no longer 
considered subordinate groups within the general body of literature in a language, do not 
formulate headings such as *French literature--African authors*.

H. Form subdivisions 
H.1. General rule 
The following free-floating subdivisions may also be used under period subdivisions from 
section E or author group subdivisions from section F with the noted exceptions. They may also 
be used after headings for literary genres with geographic subdivisions. 
LC practice: As stated in H 1075, Library of Congress catalogers will no longer use form 
subdivisions. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form 
Terms (LCGFT) to bring out the form(s) and/or genre(s) of the resource being cataloged. To 
replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign Library of Congress 
Demographic Group Terms (LCDGT) to bring out the audience of the resource being cataloged. 
The form subdivisions maintained on this list are not available for use by LC catalogers. 
Catalogers at institutions external to LC may use them as dictated by their own institutional 
policies.

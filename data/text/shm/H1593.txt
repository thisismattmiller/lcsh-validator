A. Background 
Exhibitions are public displays of the work of artists, the skills of performers, products of farms 
or factories, objects of general or special interest, etc. This instruction sheet provides guidelines 
for assigning the heading *Exhibitions* and the free-floating subdivision *--Exhibitions*. 
LC practice: As stated in H 1075, LC catalogers will no longer use form subdivisions. Instead 
assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT), e.g. 
*Exhibition catalogs*. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also 
assign Library of Congress Demographic Groups Terms (LCDGT) to bring out the audience of the 
resource being cataloged, e.g. *German speakers* or *Arabic, Students of*.

C. Definitions 
Exhibition: public displays of the work of artists, the skills of performers, products of farms or 
factories, objects of general or special interest, etc. 
Exhibition catalog: publications that document the works displayed in an exhibition0F1 
D. General 
Assign the heading *Exhibitions* (May Subd Geog) to general works on exhibitions and to 
works that discuss exhibitions collectively in a particular region, country, city, etc. 
Do not assign the heading to works about an individual exhibition. 
E. Exhibitions on special subjects 
E.1. General 
Use the free-floating form subdivision *--Exhibitions* under any type of heading, including 
names of persons, classes of persons, ethnic groups, corporate bodies, literary works, place 
names, disciplines, types of objects, etc., for works on exhibitions about these topics and for 
individual exhibition catalogs containing descriptions of the exhibition and/or lists of the 
objects exhibited. For works on exhibitions of objects representing a discipline, use the heading 
for the objects rather than the discipline, if both headings exist. For works on exhibition 
techniques and methodology in a specific type of institution, use the topical subdivision *--
Exhibitions* under the heading for the type of institution, e.g. *Art museums--Exhibitions*, 
unless a phrase heading has been established (see sec. F.2, below). 
Do not subdivide *--Exhibitions* further by *--Catalogs*. 
LC practice: Assign subject heading(s) for the topic of an exhibition. For works on exhibitions of 
objects representing a discipline, use the heading for the objects rather than the discipline, if 
both headings exist. Also assign LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in 
hand, e.g. *Exhibition catalogs*.

E.2. Phrase headings for exhibitions. 
If a phrase heading exists for a special type of exhibition, assign it rather than using a heading of 
the type *[topic]--Exhibitions*. 
Examples: 
650 #0 $a Horticultural exhibitions. 
[not 650 #0 $a Gardening $x Exhibitions.] 
 
650 #0 $a Livestock exhibitions 
[not 650 #0 $a Livestock $x Exhibitions.] 
 
650 #0 $a Museum exhibits. 
[not 650 #0 $a Museums $x Exhibitions.] 
E.3. Bibliographical exhibitions. 
E.3.a. General 
Assign the heading *Books--History--Exhibitions* to works on exhibitions that illustrate the 
history of the book and book arts, including writing, printing, illustrating, collecting and 
preserving, etc., and to catalogs of individual bibliographical exhibitions of a general nature. 
E.3.b. Special subjects 
Use the free-floating subdivision *--Bibliography--Exhibitions* under any type of heading for 
works on exhibitions of books, periodicals, etc., about these topics and for individual exhibition 
catalogs containing descriptions of the exhibition and/or lists of the books displayed. 
Do not assign the heading *Books--History--Exhibitions* to exhibitions of books on special 
topics. 
LC practice: Assign a subject heading for the topic of the exhibition. Also assign LCGFT 
appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Exhibition catalogs*. 
See the base instructions under sec E.1. 
E.4. Exhibitions held in institutions 
Assign the heading *[name of institution]--Exhibitions* with the free-floating form subdivision 
if the objects exhibited are part of the institution's permanent collections. In addition, assign 
the appropriate headings, as described in sec. E, for the special subject matter of the exhibition.

Do not assign the heading *[name of institution]--Exhibitions* if the items displayed are not 
part of the institution's permanent collections. 
LC practice: Assign the name of institution followed by the topical subdivision *--Exhibitions* if 
the objects exhibited are part of the institution's permanent collections. 
F. Individual named exhibitions 
F.1. General 
Assign the name heading for the exhibition to works on individual named exhibitions treated as 
historical events and to all works on exhibitions without a topical focus to which headings of 
the type *[topic]--Exhibitions* cannot be assigned, such as world's fairs. 
Subdivide headings for named exhibitions in accordance with H 1105, if appropriate, including 
the subdivision *--Catalogs*. 
For historical works on exhibitions with a topical focus, assign additional headings for the 
subject matter of the exhibition. 
For works on the contributions of one country to an international exhibition, assign an 
additional heading of the type *[place]--Exhibitions*. If the exhibition has a topical focus, 
assign appropriate additional headings for the subject matter of the exhibition. 
LC practice: Follow the approach described above, except for the instructions related to form 
subdivisions. Assign the LCGFT *Catalogs* in place of the form subdivision *--Catalogs*, where 
appropriate. Assign the topical heading or place in combination with the LCGFT *Exhibition 
catalogs*. Do not assign both LCGFT *Catalogs* and *Exhibition catalogs*. 
F.2. Named exhibitions on special subjects 
For guidebooks, catalogs, etc., to individual named exhibitions on special subjects, assign only 
headings of the type *[topic]--Exhibitions*. 
Do not assign the name heading for the exhibition. 
LC practice: Assign the topical heading in combination with the LCGFT *Exhibition catalogs* or 
other appropriate term.

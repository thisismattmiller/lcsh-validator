A. Background 
The subdivision *--Cases*, clinical reports, statistics, formerly used under medical headings, 
was discontinued in 1979 in favor of the subdivisions *--Case studies* and *--Statistics*. The 
subdivisions *--Case studies* and *--Cases* are now the only authorized free-floating 
subdivisions used for descriptions of cases or works about them. This instruction sheet provides 
guidelines for assigning the subdivisions *--Case studies* and *--Cases* and the heading *Case 
method*. It also provides guidelines on cataloging works about conducting and writing case 
studies. 
LC practice: As stated in H 1075, LC catalogers will no longer use form subdivisions. Instead 
assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT), e.g. 
*Case studies*. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign 
Library of Congress Demographic Groups Terms (LCDGT) to bring out the audience of the 
resource being cataloged, e.g. *German speakers* or *Arabic, Students of*.

C. Definitions 
Case studies: descriptions of one or more recorded instances illustrating the conditions of a 
particular field, from which general principles may be drawn 
D. Case method 
Use the heading *Case method* as a topical heading for general works on the use of recorded 
case studies as a method of instruction. 
E. Case studies 
Case studies are descriptions of one or more recorded instances illustrating the conditions of a 
particular field, from which general principles may be drawn. Use the free-floating subdivision *-
-Case studies* under classes of persons, ethnic groups, and topical headings, except legal 
headings. 
Under medical headings, including names of diseases, use this subdivision for professional 
description or analysis of one or more case histories on the topic. 
Also use this subdivision under names of individual corporate bodies for case studies involving 
those corporate bodies. 
Code the subdivision as a form subdivision ($v) for works consisting of case studies and as a 
topical subdivision ($x) for works that discuss case studies. 
LC practice: Assign as subject headings the focus of the case study. Also assign the LCGFT term 
appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Case studies*. 
Examples: 
650 #0 $a Women farmers $z India $v Case studies. 
650 #0 $a Juvenile delinquency $x Case studies. 
650 #0 $a AIDS (Disease) $v Case studies. 
650 #0 $a Blood-vessels $x Endoscopic surgery $x Case studies. 
610 20 $a Camden County Children's Shelter $v Case studies. 
610 20 $a United Nations $x Case studies $v Bibliography. 
LC practice: 
650 #0 $a Women farmers $z India. 
655 #7 $a Case studies. $2 lcgft 
F. Cases 
Use the free-floating form subdivision *--Cases* under topical legal headings for compilations of 
court or regulatory agency decisions on the topic, including reporters or casebooks for students.

LC practice: Assign as subject headings the appropriate legal headings for compilations of court 
or regulatory agency decisions on a topic. Also assign the LCGFT term appropriate to the form 
and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Casebooks (Law)*. 
Examples: 
650 #0 $a Estates (Law) $v Cases. 
LC practice: 
650 #0 $a Estates (Law) 
655 #7 $a Court decisions and opinions. $2 lcgft 
 
650 #0 $a Torts $v Cases. 
LC practice: 
650 #0 $a Torts. 
655 #7 $a Casebooks (Law) $2 lcgft 
Note: For non-topical compilations, use either *Law reports, digests, etc.* (for reporters) or 
*Law--Cases* (for casebooks), as appropriate. 
LC practice: For non-topical compilations, assign the LCGFT term appropriate to the form 
and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Casebooks (Law)*. 
Also use the free-floating topical subdivision *--Cases* under topical legal headings for works 
about compilations of court or regulatory agency decisions on the topic, including reporters or 
casebooks for students. 
G. Methodology 
For works on the methodology of conducting case studies on a particular topic, see H 2020. 
H. Authorship 
For works on the authorship of case studies in a particular discipline, use the free-floating topical 
subdivision *--Authorship* under the heading for the discipline (e.g., *Psychiatry--Authorship*).

BACKGROUND:  Prior to 1997, the subdivisions BConstitution, BConstitutional history, and 
BConstitutional law, as well as other related subdivisions, were used under names of places to 
construct subject headings for texts of, and works about, the constitutions of political 
jurisdictions.  Beginning in 1997, at the recommendation of a task force of the American 
Association of Law Libraries, these subdivisions were discontinued in favor of geographic 
subdivision of the headings Constitutions, Constitutional history, and Constitutional law.  The 
subdivision BConstitution remains valid under names of individual corporate bodies.  Also in 
1997, Library of Congress subject cataloging practice was changed to allow the assignment of 
the name-title heading for an individual constitution to a work about the constitution.  This 
instruction sheet describes subject heading practice for texts of, and works about, constitutions 
of political jurisdictions and individual corporate bodies. 
 
1.  Constitutions of political jurisdictions. 
 
a.  Texts of constitutions.   
 
(1) Collections.  Assign the heading Constitutions (May Subd Geog) to 
collections of texts of constitutions of multiple jurisdictions or of a single 
jurisdiction.  Examples: 
 
Title:  Select constitutions of the world. 
650 #0 $a Constitutions. 
 
Title:  Las Constituciones latinoamericanas. 
650 #0 $a Constitutions $z Latin America. 
 
Title:  Constitutions of Maryland. 
650 #0 $a Constitutions $z Maryland. 
 
To collections of the constitutions of the political divisions of a particular place, 
further subdivide the heading Constitutions-[place] by the appropriate 
subdivision to designate the type of political division, for example, BStates or 
BProvinces (see H 713).  Example: 
 
Title:  The constitutions of the Australian states. 
650 #0 $a Constitutions $z Australia $x States. 
 
Note: Headings of the type Constitutions, State and Provincial 
constitutions, which were formerly used for collections of this type, have 
been cancelled.

1.  Constitutions of political jurisdictions. 
 
a.  Texts of constitutions.  (Continued)   
 
(2) Individual constitutions.  Assign the heading Constitutions (May Subd 
Geog) to the text of an individual constitution of a specific place.  Do not assign 
the name-title heading for the constitution to a work that consists solely of the text 
of the constitution.  Examples: 
 
Title:  The Constitution of the United States. 
650 #0 $a Constitutions $z United States. 
 
Title:  The California state constitution. 
650 #0 $a Constitutions $z California. 
 
Also assign this heading for the constitutions of Indian tribes that are recognized 
by the U.S. government and established as jurisdictional name headings.  
Example: 
 
Title:  Constitution and By-laws of the Kickapoo Tribe of 
Oklahoma. 
650 #0 $a Constitutions $z Kickapoo Tribe of 
            Oklahoma. 
 
b.  Works about constitutions and constitutional law.   
 
(1) General and comparative works.  Assign the heading Constitutional law, 
subdivided by place and further subdivided by BStates, BProvinces, etc., if 
appropriate (see H 713), to works about constitutions or constitutional law in 
general and to comparative works about the constitutions or constitutional law of 
multiple jurisdictions.  Examples: 
 
Title:  Constitutionalism. 
650 #0 $a Constitutional law. 
 
Title:  European constitutional law. 
650 #0 $a Constitutional law $z Europe. 
 
Title:  Recent developments in state constitutional law. 
650 #0 $a Constitutional law $z United States  
            $x States.

1.  Constitutions of political jurisdictions. 
 
b.  Works about constitutions and constitutional law.  (Continued)   
 
(2) Constitutional law of a specific place.  Assign the heading Constitutional 
law (May Subd Geog) to works that discuss the constitutional law of a particular 
place.   Examples: 
 
 
Title:  American constitutional law. 
650 #0 $a Constitutional law $z United States. 
 
Title:  A manual of Australian constitutional law. 
650 #0 $a Constitutional law $z Australia. 
 
 
To works that discuss a specific constitution as a document, or that include a 
significant amount of material about its construction and formulation, also assign 
the name-title heading for the constitution, if it is judged to be a useful access 
point.  Example: 
 
 
Title:  The New York State Constitution : a reference guide. 
610 10 $a New York (State). $t Constitution 
            (1894) 
650 #0 $a Constitutional law $z New York (State) 
 
 
(3) Constitutional interpretation and construction.  Although the subdivision 
BInterpretation and construction is free-floating under most legal topics (see H 
1154.5), do not use this subdivision under the headings Constitutions or 
Constitutional law, since most works on constitutional law deal to a greater or 
lesser degree with the interpretation and construction of constitutions.  Instead, 
assign the heading Constitutional law, subdivided by place if appropriate, for 
works on constitutional interpretation and construction  Example: 
 
 
Title:  Contemporary perspectives on constitutional 
interpretation. 
650 #0 $a Constitutional law $z United States.

1.  Constitutions of political jurisdictions.  (Continued) 
 
c.  Constitutional amendments. 
 
 
Note: 
 
The 
subdivisions 
BConstitutionBAmendments 
and 
BConstitutional lawBAmendments, which were used under names of 
places prior to 1997, have been discontinued. 
 
 
(1) Texts of constitutional amendments.  To works that consist solely of the text 
of one or more particular amendments to a constitution, assign the appropriate 
topical heading(s) to bring out the subject.  Also assign Constitutional 
amendmentsB[place] as a form heading.  Do not assign the name-title heading 
for the amendment(s).  Examples: 
 
Title:  The complete text of the Bill of Rights. 
650 #0 $a Civil rights $z United States. 
650 #0 $a Constitutional amendments $z United 
            States. 
 
 
(2) Works about constitutional amendments in general.  To works that discuss 
constitutional amendments in general, including the amending process, as well as 
works on various constitutional amendments of multiple or particular jurisdictions, 
assign the heading Constitutional amendments, subdivided by place and further 
subdivided by BStates, BProvinces, etc., if appropriate (see H 713).  Examples: 
 
Title:  Federalism and constitutional change. 
650 #0 $a Constitutional amendments. 
650 #0 $a Federal government. 
 
 
Title:  Amending the constitution of Canada : a discussion paper. 
650 #0 $a Constitutional amendments $z Canada. 
 
 
Title:  Major problems in state constitutional revision. 
650 #0 $a Constitutional amendments $z United 
            States $x States.

1.  Constitutions of political jurisdictions. 
 
c.  Constitutional amendments.  (Continued) 
 
(3) Works about specific constitutional amendments.  To works that discuss a 
specific constitutional amendment or group of amendments, assign the name-title 
heading for the amendment.  Also assign Constitutional amendmentsB [place] 
as a topical heading, and one or more additional topical headings to bring out the 
subject of the amendment or amendments.  Example: 
 
 
 
Title:  The Fourth Amendment. 
610 10 $a United States. $t Constitution. $n 4th 
            Amendment. 
650 #0 $a Constitutional amendments $z United 
            States. 
650 #0 $a Searches and seizures $z United 
            States. 
 
 
 
Title:  The Bill of Rights : what it is, what it means, and how it's 
been misused. 
610 10 $a United States.  $t Constitution.   
            $n 1st-10th Amendments. 
650 #0 $a Constitutional amendments $z United 
            States. 
650 #0 $a Civil rights $z United States. 
 
 
 
 
d.  Constitutional history.  To works that discuss the historical development of 
constitutions, assign, as appropriate Constitutional historyB[place], Constitutional 
history, Ancient, and/or Constitutional history, Medieval.   
 
Do not use the subdivision BHistory under the headings Constitutions or Constitutional 
law.

2.  Constitutions of corporate bodies.  To texts of, or works about, the constitution of a 
particular corporate body, assign the heading for the corporate body with the subdivision 
BConstitution.  Examples: 
 
Title:  Constitution of the Bible Society of Virginia. 
610 20 $a Bible Society of Virginia $v Constitution. 
 
Title:  Les constitutions d'Anderson : vérité ou imposture. 
610 20 $a Freemasons $x Constitution. 
600 10 $a Anderson, James, $d ca. 1680-1739. 
650 #0 $a Freemasonry $x History.

A. Recent revisions 
2026-05: Added subdivision. 
B. Types of headings covered by the pattern 
Headings for individual plants and crops and groups of plants and crops, including algae, fungi, 
and lichens, at all taxonomic levels, established using either common or scientific names. The 
pattern also covers headings for extinct and fossil plants. 
Examples: 
Camellias 
Eucalyptus alba 
Rice 
Basidiomycetes 
Forage plants 
Fruit 
Weeds 
Yeast fungi 
Davis peppergrass 
Franklinia 
Gymnosperms, Fossil

C. Types of headings not covered 
The category does not include the headings *Plants*; *Plants, Cultivated*; *Crops*; and *Field 
crops*. 
D. Conflicts 
D.1. General rule 
Any subdivision listed here can be used as a free-floating subdivision under any heading 
belonging to the category if it is appropriate and no conflict exists in the subject authority file.  
Subject authority records may exist for headings employing variant phrases or subdivisions 
equivalent to subdivisions on this list. 
D.2. LC practice 
If an exceptional variant form is to be retained, make a UF reference from the equivalent free-
floating subdivision form following the procedures in H 195 if the reference does not yet exist.  
Otherwise, submit a proposal to change the variant form along with all bibliographic records 
requiring correction following the procedures in H 193.

F. List of form subdivisions  
LC practice: As stated in H 1075, Library of Congress catalogers will no longer use form 
subdivisions. Instead assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form 
Terms (LCGFT) to bring out the form(s) and/or genre(s) of the resource being cataloged. The 
form subdivisions maintained on this list are not available for use by LC catalogers. Catalogers 
at institutions external to LC may use them as dictated by their own institutional policies.

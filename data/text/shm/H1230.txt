A. Background 
Archives are collections of documents or records relating to the activities, business dealings, 
etc., of a person, family, corporation, association, community, or nation. This instruction sheet 
provides guidelines for assigning the subdivisions *--Archives* and *--Archival resources*. For 
guidelines on classification of archival inventories, see F 495, sec. 1-2. For guidelines on 
classification of works on archival resources, see F 630. 
LC practice: As stated in H 1075, LC catalogers will no longer use form subdivisions. Instead 
assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT), e.g. 
*Catalogs*. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign 
Library of Congress Demographic Groups Terms (LCDGT) to bring out the audience of the 
resource being cataloged, e.g. *German speakers* or *Arabic, Students of*.

C. Definitions 
Archives: documentary material, such as records, manuscripts, household records, diaries, 
correspondence, photographs, memorabilia, etc. pertaining to an agent or a topic. 
D. Archives 
D.1. General 
Use the free-floating subdivision *--Archives* as a form or topical subdivision under types of 
corporate bodies and educational institutions, classes of persons, and ethnic groups, and under 
names of individual corporate bodies, military services, educational institutions, persons, and 
families, for collections of documentary material, such as manuscripts, household records, 
diaries, correspondence, photographs, memorabilia, etc., pertaining to these persons or 
institutions. Use the topical subdivision for discussions of such collections. 
Assign an array of headings to bring out both the archive and the subject matter of the 
documents it contains. 
LC practice: For discussions of documentary material, assign a subject heading with the topical 
subdivision *--Archives* under types of corporate bodies and educational institutions, classes 
of persons, and ethnic groups, and under names of individual corporate bodies, military 
services, educational institutions, persons, and families. For collections of documentary 
material, such as manuscripts, household records, diaries, correspondence, photographs, 
memorabilia, etc., assign subject headings to reflect the topical focus of the documentary 
materials, including the relevant agents that created or collected the materials. Also assign 
LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Records 
(Documents)*.

D.2. Individual corporate bodies 
Use the free-floating subdivision *--Archives* under names of corporate bodies, including 
individual educational institutions, provided that the corporate body or educational institution 
prepares the archive for public access, and is not merely the institution that houses the archive. 
If the collection is a formally organized archive for which a name heading can be established, 
use that heading, as appropriate, instead of the subdivision *--Archives* under the name of the 
corporate body. 
LC practice: For corporate bodies, including individual educational institutions, that prepare the 
archive for public access, not merely the institution that houses the archive, assign a subject 
heading for the body. If the collection is a formally organized archive for which a name heading 
can be established, use that heading instead. Also assign LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or 
genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Records (Documents)*. 
D.3. Catalogs

for inventories of documents and materials housed in an individual archive. In accordance with

E. Archival resources 
E.1. General 
Use the free-floating topical subdivision *--Archival resources* under topical headings and 
names of countries, cities, etc., for brief descriptions of the types of documents and historical 
records about a particular topic or place available in an institution or institutions. 
Do not use the subdivision *--Archival resources* for catalogs containing an item-by-item list of 
the holdings of an institution or institutions on a topic. See sec. D.3 above. 
Note: *--Archival resources* was formerly further subdivided by place. That practice was 
discontinued in 1990. If the location of the resources is judged to be significant, bring it out by 
assigning the heading *Archival resources--[place]* as an additional heading. 
Example: 
Title: Christianity in China : a scholar's guide to resources in The 
archives of the United States. 
650 #0 $a Christianity $z China $x Archival resources. 
651 #0 $a China $x Church history $x Archival resources. 
650 #0 $a Archival resources $z United States. 
LC practice: 
No change in subject headings. 
655 #7 $a Collection catalogs. $2 lcgft 
E.2. Directories 
Use the free-floating form subdivision *--Directories*, if appropriate, under headings of the 
type *[topic or place]--Archival resources* for works containing the names, addresses, and 
brief descriptions of institutions housing archival materials on a particular topic. 
Example: 
Title: Peace : archival and library collections in the United States. 
650 #0 $a Peace $x Archival resources $v Directories. 
650 #0 $a Peace $x Library resources $v Directories. 
650 #0 $a Archival resources $z United States $v Directories. 
650 #0 $a Library resources $z United States $v Directories. 
LC practice: 
650 #0 $a Peace $x Archival resources. 
650 #0 $a Peace $x Library resources. 
650 #0 $a Archival resources $z United States.

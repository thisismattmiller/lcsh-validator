BACKGROUND:  Past practice in subject cataloging provided several methods to show that a 
work discussed societies related to a particular subject or to show that a serial was published by 
a society.  Phrase headings for societies in a particular discipline have been replaced by 
headings using the subdivision BSocieties, etc. The subdivision BSocieties, periodicals, etc., 
previously used under names of persons was replaced by the subdivisions BSocieties, etc. and 
BPeriodicals.  Also discontinued is the practice of using the subdivision BSocieties, etc. to show 
that a serial publication is issued by a society and contains information about the society. 
 
For classification of society publications, see F 225. 
 
 
 
 
 
1.  BSocieties, etc.  Use the subdivision BSocieties, etc. as a free-floating topical subdivision 
under subjects for works discussing two or more societies or institutions related to those subjects. 
 Examples: 
 
 
 
650 #0 $a African Americans $z Massachusetts $x Societies, etc. 
650 #0 $a Chemistry $x Societies, etc. 
650 #0 $a Religious literature $x Publishing $x Societies, etc. 
651 #0 $a United States $x History $y Civil War, 1861-1865  
          $x Societies, etc. 
600 10 $a Bolívar, Simón, $d 1783-1830 $x Societies, etc. 
610 20 $a Catholic Church $z Italy $x Societies, etc. 
 
 
 
Do not use the subdivision under headings for classes of persons, if the corresponding heading 
for the discipline can be assigned.  Example:  
 
 
 
650 #0 $a Engineering $x Societies, etc. 
  [not 650 #0 $a Engineers $x Societies, etc.]

2.  BSocieties and clubs.  Use the subdivision BSocieties and clubs as a free-floating topical 
subdivision under headings for age and sex groups for works on organizations devoted to these 
groups or whose membership is drawn from these groups.  Such works may discuss methods of 
establishing and conducting these organizations or may discuss specific organizations 
collectively.  Use the subdivision also under qualified headings for age and sex groups.  
Examples: 
 
650 #0 $a Boys $x Societies and clubs. 
650 #0 $a Women $x Societies and clubs. 
650 #0 $a Rural women $x Societies and clubs. 
650 #0 $a Jewish women $x Societies and clubs. 
650 #0 $a Older people $x Societies and clubs. 
650 #0 $a Minority older people $x Societies and clubs. 
 
 
3.  Further subdivision by form subdivision.  The subdivisions BSocieties, etc. and BSocieties 
and clubs may be further subdivided by any appropriate form subdivision such as BBibliogra-
phy or BDirectories. 
 
   
4.  Societies or clubs in a particular place.  For general works on the societies and institutions 
in a particular place assign the heading Associations, institutions, etc.B[place].  For works 
discussing collectively the learned institutions or societies of a particular place assign Learned 
institutions and societiesB[place].  For works discussing collectively the clubs of a particular 
place assign ClubsB[place]. 
 
 
5.  Society publications.  Individual serial publications issued by a society may contain 
information on business matters of the society and/or information on the topic in which the 
society specializes.  Assign headings to these works according to the type of information 
contained. 
 
a.  Business information only.  Assign a heading for the name of the society, with the 
subdivision BPeriodicals if appropriate. 
 
b.  Topical information only.  Assign the heading or headings for the topic, with the 
subdivision BPeriodicals if appropriate. 
 
c.  Both business and topical information.  Assign headings for both the society and 
the topic, with the subdivision BPeriodicals if appropriate.

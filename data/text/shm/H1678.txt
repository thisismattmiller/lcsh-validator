A. Background 
This instruction sheet provides guidelines for the use of the heading *Interviews*, as well as for 
the use of the free-floating subdivision *--Interviews*. The free-floating subdivision *--
Interviews* is used as a form subdivision under headings for classes of persons, ethnic groups, 
names of individual corporate bodies, and names of individual persons, including literary 
authors, for transcripts of what was said during the course of interviews or conversations with 
one or more persons on one or more occasions. The free-floating subdivision *--Interviews* is 
used as a topical subdivision under the same types of headings for works about those 
interviews. 
LC practice: As stated in H 1075, LC catalogers will no longer use form subdivisions. Instead 
assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT), e.g. 
*Interviews*. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign 
Library of Congress Demographic Groups Terms (LCDGT) to bring out the audience of the 
resource being cataloged, e.g. *German speakers* or *Arabic, Students of*. 
For guidelines on bio-bibliography, see H 1328. For guidelines on biography, see H 1330. For 
guidelines on diaries, see H 1538. For guidelines on personal narratives of wars or disasters, see 
H 1928. For guidelines on genealogy and family histories, see H 1631.

C. Definitions 
Interviews: transcripts of what was said during the course of interviews or conversations with 
one or more persons on one or more occasions. 
D. The heading Interviews 
Assign the heading *Interviews* to general collections of interviews with persons from a wide 
variety of groups or with undesignated backgrounds. 
Example: 
Title: Talks with prominent Americans. 
650 #0 $a Interviews $z United States. 
E. --Interviews as a form subdivision 
E.1. Classes of persons or ethnic groups 
Assign a heading or headings of the type *[class of persons]--Interviews* or *[ethnic group]--
Interviews* to works consisting of interviews with one or more persons from specific fields or 
ethnic groups. 
Examples: 
650 #0 $a African Americans $v Interviews. 
650 #0 $a Children of divorced parents $z United States $v Interviews. 
650 #0 $a Scientists $z Hungary $v Interviews. 
650 #0 $a Terminally ill $z Sweden $v Interviews. 
610 10 $a United States. $b Navy $x Surgeons $v Interviews. 
650 #0 $a Women mountaineers $z Europe $v Interviews. 
LC practice: 
650 #0 $a Children of divorced parents $z United States. 
655 #7 $a Interviews. $2 lcgft 
Exception: If the heading representing the special class of persons does not exist and it is not 
possible to establish such a heading, use *--Interviews* under the heading for the appropriate 
field or discipline. In such situations the subdivision is not free floating and must be established. 
An example of a work requiring such treatment would be a collection of interviews with several

persons involved in the field of dance, including dancers, choreographers, impresarios, 
composers, costume designers, patrons, etc. 
Also use the subdivision *--Interviews* under names of individual corporate bodies, including 
performing groups, for collections of interviews with persons associated with the corporate 
body, for example, *Minnesota Vikings (Football team)--Interviews*; *Guarneri Quartet--
Interviews*. Use more specific subdivisions representing classes of persons, for example, *--
Employees* or *--Presidents*, under the corporate body heading, if appropriate. 
LC practice: Assign the subject term for the class of persons, ethnic group, or corporate bodies 
if the group is a focus of the topic. If no appropriate class of persons exists, use the heading for 
the field or discipline. Also assign LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource 
in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Interviews* or the more specific term. 
E.2. Individual persons 
Use the free-floating subdivision *--Interviews* under names of individual persons, including 
literary authors, for works consisting of interviews with them. 
LC practice: Assign the subject term for individual person. Also assign LCGFT appropriate to the 
form and/or genre of the resource in hand, e.g. LCGFT *Interviews* or the more specific term. 
Examples: 
Title: Entretiens avec Marcel Duchamp. 
600 10 $a Duchamp, Marcel, $d 1887-1968 $v Interviews. 
650 #0 $a Artists $z France $v Interviews. 
LC practice: 
600 10 $a Duchamp, Marcel, $d 1887-1968. 
650 #0 $a Artists $z France. 
655 #7 $a Interviews. $2 lcgft 
 
Title: Borges at eighty : conversations. 
600 10 $a Borges, Jorge Luis, $d 1899-1986 $v Interviews. 
650 #0 $a Authors, Argentine $y 20th century $v Interviews. 
LC practice: 
600 10 $a Borges, Jorge Luis, $d 1899-1986. 
650 #0 $a Authors, Argentine $y 20th century.

F. --Interviews as a topical subdivision 
For works about interviews or conversations with one or more persons on one or more 
occasions, use *--Interviews* as a topical subdivision under headings for classes of persons, 
ethnic groups, names of individual corporate bodies, and names of individual persons, including 
literary authors. 
Examples: 
Title: Frost/Nixon. 
600 10 $a Nixon, Richard M. $q (Richard Milhous), $d 1913-1994 $x 
Interviews. 
650 #0 $a Presidents $z United States $x Interviews. 
650 #0 $a Ex-presidents $z United States $x Interviews. 
600 10 $a Frost, David, $d 1939 Apr. 7- 
 
Title: Enron 1986-2001. 
610 20 $a Enron Corp. $x Employees $x Interviews. 
650 #0 $a Energy industries $z United States. 
G. Assigning additional headings 
Assign additional appropriate headings as needed to bring out the focus of the interviews or 
specific topics discussed. Do not further subdivide these headings by *--Interviews*. 
Examples: 
Title: Soviet leaders face American journalists. 
651 #0 $a Soviet Union $x Foreign relations $y 1953-1975. 
651 #0 $a Soviet Union $x Foreign relations $z United States. 
651 #0 $a United States $x Foreign relations $z Soviet Union. 
650 #0 $a Statesmen $z Soviet Union $v Interviews. 
LC practice: 
651 #0 $a Soviet Union $x Foreign relations $y 1953-1975. 
651 #0 $a Soviet Union $x Foreign relations $z United States. 
651 #0 $a United States $x Foreign relations $z Soviet Union. 
650 #0 $a Statesmen $z Soviet Union.

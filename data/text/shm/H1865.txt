A. Background 
Map headings are assigned not only to maps and atlases, but also to works about maps and 
atlases, and to works with both textual and cartographic content in which the cartographic 
content is secondary, serving mainly to support, illustrate, or explain the textual content. This 
instruction sheet discusses the assignment of headings to works of this latter type in order to 
bring out their cartographic content. For instructions on providing subject access to maps 
themselves, see the Map Cataloging Manual. 
LC practice: As stated in H 1075, LC catalogers will no longer use form subdivisions. Instead 
assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT), e.g. 
*Textbooks*. To replace a form subdivision that includes a demographic group, also assign 
Library of Congress Demographic Groups Terms (LCDGT) to bring out the audience of the 
resource being cataloged, e.g. *German speakers* or *Arabic, Students of*. 
For guidelines on assigning LCGFT, see the LCGFT Manual J 215 Cartographic Materials.

C. General rule 
Assign the appropriate map heading(s) if at least 20% of the text of a work consists of maps, or 
discusses maps, even though the primary emphasis of the work is on the textual content.  
Example 
650 #0 $a Aeronautical charts. 
650 #0 $a City planning $z Washington (State) $z Seattle $v Maps.

G. Incidental maps 
Do not bring out the presence of maps in the text if the maps in question are of an incidental or 
routine nature.

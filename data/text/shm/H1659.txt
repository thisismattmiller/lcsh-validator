A. Background 
The subdivision *--Illustrations* is used as a form or topical subdivision under types of 
published materials, literatures, literary forms, names of individual persons, individual works, 
and sacred works for collections or discussions of pictorial representations found in these works 
or depicting these subjects. This instruction sheet provides guidelines for using this subdivision. 
The subdivision is coded as a $v subfield for collections of illustrations or as an $x subfield for 
discussions of illustrations. 
LC practice: As stated in H 1075, LC catalogers will no longer use form subdivisions. Instead 
assign an appropriate term or terms from Library of Congress Genre/Form Terms (LCGFT), e.g. 
*Illustrated works*. 
For guidelines on place as a theme in art, see H 910. For guidelines on art and fine art, see H 
1250. For guidelines on artistic photography, see H 1255. For guidelines on pictorial works, see 
H 1935. For guidelines on exhibitions, see H 1593.

C. Definition 
Illustrations: Pictorial representations in published materials. 
D. Types of published materials 
Use the free-floating subdivision *--Illustrations* under types of published materials for 
collections or discussions of illustrations used in these publications. 
LC practice: Use the free-floating topical subdivision *--Illustrations* under types of published 
materials for discussions of illustrations used in these publications. For collections of 
illustrations in these publications, assign a subject heading for the type of published materials 
and the topic, if applicable. Assign LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource 
in hand. 
Examples: 
650 #0 $a Newspapers $x Illustrations. 
650 #0 $a Paperbacks $v Illustrations. 
LC practice for collections of illustrations: 
650 #0 $a Paperbacks. 
655 #7 $a Illustrated works. $2 lcgft 
E. Literature 
Use the free-floating form subdivision *--Illustrations* under literatures and literary forms for 
collections of pictorial representations of literary subjects. For discussion of these topics, use 
the free-floating topical subdivision. 
LC practice: Use the free-floating topical subdivision *--Illustrations* under literatures and 
literary forms for discussions of pictorial representations of these literary subjects. For 
collections of illustrations, assign a subject heading for the type of literature and the topic, if 
applicable. Assign LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand. 
Examples: 
650 #0 $a English literature $x Illustrations. 
650 #0 $a Nursery rhymes, English $x Illustrations. 
LC practice for collections of illustrations: 
650 #0 $a English literature.

F. Individual persons 
Use the free-floating form subdivision *--Illustrations* under names of individual persons for 
collections of pictorial representations of the person's written works. For discussion of these 
topics, use the free-floating topical subdivision. 
LC practice: Use the free-floating topical subdivision *--Illustrations* under names of individual 
persons for discussions of pictorial representations of the person's written works. For 
collections of illustrations, assign a subject heading for the individual persons and the topic, if 
applicable. Assign LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand. 
Examples: 
600 10 $a Shakespeare, William $d 1564-1616 $v Illustrations. 
600 10 $a Josephus, Flavius $x Illustrations. 
LC practice for collections of illustrations: 
600 10 $a Shakespeare, William $d 1564-1616. 
655 #7 $a Illustrated works. $2 lcgft 
G. Individual works 
G.1. General 
Use the free-floating form subdivision *--Illustrations* under individual works (author-title or 
uniform title entries) for collections of pictorial representations of these works without text. For 
discussion of these topics, use the free-floating topical subdivision. 
LC practice: Use the free-floating topical subdivision *--Illustrations* under individual works 
(author-title or uniform title entries) for discussions of these illustrations. For collections of 
pictorial representations of these works, assign a subject heading for the individual works and 
the topic, if applicable. Assign LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in 
hand. 
Examples: 
630 00 $a Beowulf $x Illustrations. 
600 10 $a Milton, John, $d 1608-1674. $t Paradise lost $v Illustrations. 
LC practice for collections of illustrations: 
600 10 $a Milton, John, $d 1608-1674. $t Paradise lost. 
655 #7 $a Illustrated works. $2 lcgft

G.2. Sacred works 
Use the free-floating form subdivision *--Illustrations* under uniform titles for sacred works 
and their individual parts for collections of pictorial representations of these works. For 
discussion of these topics, use the free-floating topical subdivision. 
Assign a heading of this type to illustrated editions of sacred works, to collections of such 
illustrations without text, and to discussions of these illustrations. 
LC practice: Use the free-floating topical subdivision *--Illustrations* under uniform titles for 
sacred works and their individual parts for discussions of pictorial representations of these 
works. This includes illustrated editions of sacred works, collections of such illustrations without 
text, and to discussions of these illustrations. For collections of pictorial representations of 
these works, assign a subject heading for the individual works and the topic, if applicable. 
Assign LCGFT appropriate to the form and/or genre of the resource in hand. 
Example: 
630 00 $a Bible. $p Revelation $v Illustrations. 
630 00 $a Qur’an $x Illustrations. 
LC practice for collections of illustrations: 
630 00 $a Bible. $p Revelation. 
655 #7 $a Illustrated works. $2 lcgft 
H. Other 
In accordance with H 1935, use the subdivision *--Pictorial works* under other types of 
headings for collections of pictorial representations of these subjects. Do not use the 
subdivision *--Illustrations* under these topics. 
LC practice: For guidelines on pictorial works, see H 1935. 
I. Technique 
To works discussing the technique of making illustrations, assign the appropriate phrase 
heading, if one exists or can be established. 
Example: 
650 #0 $a Caricature. 
650 #0 $a Illustration of books. 
650 #0 $a Medical illustration. 
650 #0 $a Scientific illustration.

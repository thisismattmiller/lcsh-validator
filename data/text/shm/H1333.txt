BACKGROUND:  Books and reading is the heading for the general topic of the significance of 
books to people, including their reading attitudes and habits.  In the past, the concepts of books 
and reading for special classes of persons as well as their reading habits were covered by 
several different types of headings:  Books and reading for [class of persons]; [class of 
persons]BBooks and reading; [class of persons], Books for; and [class of persons]BBooks for.  
In 1980 the expression of these topics was standardized in the usage of the free-floating 
subdivision BBooks and reading.  This subdivision is used under headings for classes of 
persons, ethnic groups, and names of individual persons for works about books for those persons 
or about their reading habits and interests.  
 
General lists of recommended books are assigned the heading Best books.  The subdivision 
BBooks and reading under classes of persons, ethnic groups, or names of individual persons 
may be further subdivided by the form subdivision BBibliography to designate lists of books 
recommended for, or read by, those persons. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
1.  Books and reading.  Assign the heading Books and reading to works that discuss the 
significance of books in people's lives, including their reading habits and attitudes and general 
interest in reading. 
 
For works limited to the topic of reading interests, assign the heading Reading interests. 
 
 
 
2.  Best books.  Assign the heading Best books (May Subd Geog) to general lists of 
recommended books. 
 
For lists of recommended books on specific topics, assign two headings:  
 
650 #0 $a [topic] $v Bibliography. 
650 #0 $a Best books. 
 
Note:  The heading BibliographyBBest books was changed to Best books (May Subd 
Geog) in November 1994.  The heading Best books is not subdivided by topic.

3.  BBooks and reading. 
 
a.  Classes of persons and ethnic groups.  Use the free-floating subdivision BBooks and 
reading (May Subd Geog) as a topical subdivision under headings for classes of persons 
and ethnic groups for works that discuss books for those groups and/or their reading 
habits and interests.  For works that consist of lists of such books, further subdivide 
BBooks and reading by BBibliography.  Examples: 
 
650 #0 $a Blind $x Books and reading. 
650 #0 $a Girls $x Books and reading $v Bibliography. 
650 #0 $a Legislators $x Books and reading $z United States 
            $v Bibliography. 
650 #0 $a Mexican Americans $x Books and reading. 
 
b.  Individual persons.  Use the free-floating subdivision BBooks and reading as a 
topical subdivision under names of persons for works that discuss their reading interests. 
 For works that consist of lists of books read by those persons, further subdivide BBooks 
and reading by BBibliography.  Examples: 
 
600 10 $a Goethe, Johann Wolfgang von, $d 1749-1832 $x Books 
            and reading . 
600 10 $a Lincoln, Abraham, $d 1809-1865 $x Books and 
            reading $v Bibliography.
